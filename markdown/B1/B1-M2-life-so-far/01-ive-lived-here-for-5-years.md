## 🧩 Grammar: Present perfect para duração: for / since

Este é o uso do present perfect que mais confunde brasileiros. Em português dizemos:
"**Moro** aqui **há** 5 anos" (presente).

Em inglês, se a situação **começou no passado e continua agora**, usamos o **present perfect**:
"I**'ve lived** here **for** 5 years." ✅
"I live here for 5 years." ❌ (erro muito comum!)

**For** + **período** (quanto tempo): for two hours, for three weeks, for ten years, for ages, for a long time.
**Since** + **ponto de partida** (desde quando): since 2019, since Monday, since January, since I was a child, since 8 a.m.

**Pergunta:** **How long have you** + particípio? → "How long have you worked here?"

**Negativa com for/since:** "I **haven't seen** her **for** months." / "We **haven't talked since** the party."

**Stative verbs** (know, be, have, like) usam present perfect simples: "I've **known** him for years." / "She's **been** a teacher since 2010."

| for + período | since + ponto de partida |
|---|---|
| for ten minutes | since 9 o'clock |
| for three days | since Monday |
| for six months | since January |
| for five years | since 2021 |
| for a long time | since I was a kid |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L1/example-01.m4a) How long have you had your car? — I've had it for three years. — _Há quanto tempo você tem seu carro? — Tenho há três anos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L1/example-02.m4a) She's been my best friend since we were six. — _Ela é minha melhor amiga desde que tínhamos seis anos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L1/example-03.m4a) I haven't been to the beach for ages! — _Não vou à praia há séculos!_

> 💡 **Dica:** Compare: "I **lived** in Rio **for** 5 years" (simple past = já não moro mais lá) x "I**'ve lived** in Rio **for** 5 years" (present perfect = ainda moro). Uma palavra muda toda a história!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I live here since 2018.  
  ✅ **I've lived here since 2018.**  
  Situação que começou no passado e continua = present perfect.
- ❌ I've worked here since three years.  
  ✅ **I've worked here for three years.**  
  Período = for; ponto de partida = since.
- ❌ How long do you know her?  
  ✅ **How long have you known her?**  
  Há quanto tempo = How long have you + particípio.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: For e since fracos e o 've

**For** é fraco: "fâr" ("I've lived here fâr five years"). Não esqueça o **'ve**: "I've lived" ("áivlivd") — sem ele, a frase vira presente ("I lived" / "I live") e muda o sentido.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L1/pron-01.m4a) I've lived here for five years.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L1/pron-02.m4a) We've known each other since 2010.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L1/pron-03.m4a) How long have you worked there?

## 📖 Reading — The longest-serving... [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L1/reading.m4a)

Some people stay in the same place for an incredibly long time. In Japan, a hotel called Nishiyama Onsen Keiunkan has welcomed guests since the year 705 — it's been run by the same family for more than fifty generations. In the UK, a man named Bob Tanner delivered newspapers for 70 years in the same town. And in Brazil, many small businesses have passed from parents to children for decades. Why do people stay? Researchers say a sense of belonging is one of the main reasons: when you've known your neighbors for years and your customers since they were children, leaving feels like losing part of your identity. In a world where people change jobs every few years, these stories feel almost magical.

**Questions:**
1. Since when has the Japanese hotel welcomed guests?
2. For how many generations has the same family run it?
3. What did Bob Tanner do for 70 years?
4. What is one of the main reasons people stay?

## 📝 Writing

Write a profile of someone you admire who has done the same thing for a long time (100–120 words). Use for, since and How long.

**Model:**

> My uncle Zé has been a fisherman for more than forty years. He's lived in the same small village in Bahia since he was born, and he's had the same boat, "Esperança", since 1990. He's never wanted to live in a big city. "I've seen this beach change a lot," he says, "but the sea has always been my office." He's taught three generations of kids to fish, including me. He hasn't taken a vacation for years, but he says he doesn't need one. For me, he's the happiest person I've ever known.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Long time no see!"

Ao reencontrar alguém, você vai ouvir **"Long time no see!"** (Quanto tempo!) — uma expressão informal e gramaticalmente "estranha", mas muito usada. Outras: **"It's been ages!"** e **"Look who it is!"** (Olha quem está aqui!).
