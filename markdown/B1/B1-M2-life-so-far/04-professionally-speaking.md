## 🧩 Grammar: Present perfect continuous

**Estrutura:** **have / has been** + verbo**-ing**
- I**'ve been working** here since 2020.
- She**'s been studying** all day.
- **How long have** you **been learning** English?

**Usos:**
1. **Atividade que começou no passado e continua** (ênfase na **duração**/continuidade): "I**'ve been studying** English **for** three years."
2. **Atividade recente com resultado visível agora:** "Why are you so tired?" "I**'ve been running**." / "Your eyes are red. **Have** you **been crying**?"
3. **Atividades temporárias recentes:** "**Lately**, I**'ve been working** from home."

**Simple x continuous:**
- **Continuous** = foco na **atividade/duração**: "I**'ve been writing** emails all morning." (o processo)
- **Simple** = foco no **resultado/quantidade**: "I**'ve written** 20 emails." (quantos — completo)

Com **stative verbs** (know, be, have, like, want), use sempre o **simple**: "I**'ve known** her for years" (❌ "I've been knowing").

Com **work, live, study, teach**, os dois funcionam com for/since: "I've worked / I've been working here for 5 years."

| Present perfect continuous (processo) | Present perfect simple (resultado) |
|---|---|
| I've been reading this book all week. | I've read 200 pages. |
| She's been painting the house. | She's painted two rooms. |
| We've been saving money for a year. | We've saved R$10,000. |
| How long have you been waiting? | How many times have you called? |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L4/example-01.m4a) I've been working as a nurse for twelve years, and for the last four years I've been leading the ICU team. — _Trabalho como enfermeira há doze anos, e nos últimos quatro lidero a equipe da UTI._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L4/example-02.m4a) Sorry I'm sweaty — I've been cycling. — _Desculpe, estou suado — estava pedalando._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L4/example-03.m4a) What have you been up to lately? — I've been learning to code! — _O que você tem feito? — Tenho aprendido a programar!_

> 💡 **Dica:** **"What have you been up to?"** é uma das perguntas mais comuns ao reencontrar alguém. Responda com present perfect continuous: "I've been working a lot / traveling / taking a course..."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I'm working here since 2019.  
  ✅ **I've been working here since 2019.**  
  Desde/há + continua = present perfect (continuous), não present continuous.
- ❌ I've been knowing him for years.  
  ✅ **I've known him for years.**  
  Know é stative verb: sem -ing.
- ❌ I've been drinking five coffees today.  
  ✅ **I've drunk / I've had five coffees today.**  
  Quantidade (resultado) = present perfect simple.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: I've been = "áivbin"

**I've been** é dito como uma palavra: "áivbin" (americano) ou "áivbín" (britânico). **She's been** = "shízbin". A força vai no verbo principal: "I've been **WORK**ing here for **SIX** years."

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L4/pron-01.m4a) I've been working here for six years.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L4/pron-02.m4a) What have you been up to?

## 📖 Reading — The career-change generation [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M2-L4/reading.m4a)

For our parents, a career was often a single straight line: one company, one profession, a gold watch at retirement. Today, it looks more like a zigzag. Surveys suggest that many young professionals expect to change careers — not just jobs — several times in their lives. Take Rodrigo, 38. He worked as a lawyer for ten years, but he'd been feeling unhappy for a long time. Three years ago, he started a coding bootcamp at night. "I've been working as a software developer for two years now, and I've never been happier," he says. Experts say the key skills for this new world are adaptability and continuous learning. In other words, the question is no longer "What do you want to be when you grow up?" but "What have you been learning lately?"

**Questions:**
1. How was a career different for our parents?
2. What do many young professionals expect?
3. What did Rodrigo do three years ago?
4. What are the key skills for this new world?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Currículo americano x brasileiro

Currículos em inglês (**résumé** nos EUA, **CV** no Reino Unido) geralmente **não** têm foto, idade, estado civil, CPF ou endereço completo — isso evita discriminação. O foco é em **conquistas mensuráveis** com verbos de ação: "**Increased** sales by 30%", "**Led** a team of 12", "**Reduced** costs by 15%".

## ✅ Resumo
- have/has been + -ing.
- Duração até agora / resultado visível / atividade recente.
- Continuous = processo · simple = resultado/quantidade.
- Stative verbs: só simple (I've known).
