## 🧩 Grammar: must / have to / mustn't / don't have to + might / may

**OBRIGAÇÃO**
- **Must** + verbo → obrigação forte, regras escritas, ou algo que **você** acha necessário: "Passengers **must** wear seatbelts." / "I **must** call my mom."
- **Have to** + verbo → obrigação externa, do dia a dia (regras, circunstâncias). É mais comum na fala e funciona em todos os tempos: "I **have to** work on Saturday." / "She **has to** wear a uniform." / "I **had to** wait for an hour." / "You'll **have to** pay a fine."

**⚠️ A grande pegadinha das negativas:**
- **mustn't** = **proibido** (não pode!): "You **mustn't** use your phone while driving."
- **don't have to** = **não precisa** (é opcional): "You **don't have to** wear a tie. It's casual Friday."

**POSSIBILIDADE (talvez)**
- **might / may / could** + verbo = pode ser que, talvez: "It **might** rain." / "She **may** be late." / "That **could** be a problem."
- Negativa: **might not / may not** (não could not): "I **might not** go."

**DEDUÇÃO (tenho quase certeza)**
- **must be** = deve ser (certeza positiva): "You worked 12 hours? You **must be** exhausted!"
- **can't be** = não pode ser (certeza negativa): "That **can't be** Ana — she's in Paris."

| Forma | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| must / have to | obrigação | You have to show your ID. |
| mustn't | proibição | You mustn't park here. |
| don't have to | não é necessário | You don't have to pay. It's free. |
| might / may / could | possibilidade (talvez) | We might move to Lisbon. |
| must be | dedução positiva | He must be at home. |
| can't be | dedução negativa | It can't be true! |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L4/example-01.m4a) Visitors must not touch the paintings. — _Visitantes não podem tocar nos quadros._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L4/example-02.m4a) You don't have to bring anything — we have everything. — _Você não precisa trazer nada — temos tudo._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L4/example-03.m4a) Might an AI robot become self-aware some day? — It might, but it's unlikely soon. — _Será que um robô com IA pode se tornar autoconsciente um dia? — Pode ser, mas é improvável tão cedo._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Must** não tem passado. Para obrigação no passado, use **had to**: "Yesterday I **had to** work late" (❌ "I musted").

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ You mustn't come to the party if you're tired. (querendo dizer "não precisa")  
  ✅ **You don't have to come to the party if you're tired.**  
  Mustn't = proibido. "Não precisa" = don't have to.
- ❌ I must to go now.  
  ✅ **I must go now. / I have to go now.**  
  Must + verbo base, sem to (mas have to tem to!).
- ❌ Yesterday I must work late.  
  ✅ **Yesterday I had to work late.**  
  Obrigação no passado = had to.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Have to = "réfta" / mustn't

**Have to** soa "**réfta**" (com F!), **has to** = "**résta**", **had to** = "**rétta**". **Mustn't** = "mâsent" — o primeiro T é mudo. **Might** = "máit", com T leve.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L4/pron-01.m4a) I have to go. ("ái réfta gôu")
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L4/pron-02.m4a) She has to work. ("shí résta uórk")
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L4/pron-03.m4a) You mustn't smoke here.

## 📖 Reading — Strange laws around the world [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L4/reading.m4a)

Every country has its rules, but some laws might surprise you. In Singapore, you mustn't chew gum in most situations — selling it has been illegal since 1992 to keep the streets clean. In Switzerland, some apartment buildings have rules saying residents mustn't flush the toilet late at night because of the noise. In Japan, companies with employees aged 40 to 74 have to measure their waists during health checks — a policy to fight obesity. In Denmark, parents must choose their babies' names from an official list of approved names, or ask for special permission. These laws might seem strange, but each one tells us something about the culture that created it.

**Questions:**
1. Why is selling gum illegal in Singapore?
2. What mustn't residents of some Swiss buildings do late at night?
3. What do some Japanese companies have to do?
4. What must parents in Denmark do?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "You must try it!"

Na fala informal, **must** também é usado para **recomendações entusiasmadas**: "You **must** visit Lisbon!" / "This book is a **must-read**!" / "It's a **must-see**!". Não é uma obrigação — é empolgação.

## ✅ Resumo
- must / have to = obrigação (had to no passado).
- mustn't = proibido · don't have to = não precisa.
- might / may / could = talvez.
- must be = deve ser · can't be = não pode ser.
