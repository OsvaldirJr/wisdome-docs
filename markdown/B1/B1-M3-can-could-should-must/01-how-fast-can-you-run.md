## 🧩 Grammar: Can / could / be able to

**Can** = habilidade **presente** (saber, conseguir, poder):
"I **can** speak three languages." / "**Can** you swim?"

**Could** = habilidade **geral no passado**:
"I **could** read when I was five." / "I **couldn't** sleep last night."

**Be able to** = o mesmo sentido de can, mas funciona em **todos os tempos**, onde can não pode ir:
- futuro: "I**'ll be able to** come tomorrow." (❌ "I'll can")
- present perfect: "I**'ve never been able to** whistle." (❌ "I've could")
- depois de outro verbo: "I'd like **to be able to** play the piano."

**Sucesso numa situação específica do passado:** use **was able to** ou **managed to** (não could):
"The fire was huge, but everyone **was able to** escape." / "I **managed to** finish on time."
Na negativa, **couldn't** funciona sempre: "I **couldn't** find my keys."

**Perguntas com How:** How **fast** can you run? How **long** can you hold your breath? How **well** can you cook? How **far** can you swim?

| Tempo | Forma | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Presente | can / can't | I can cook very well. |
| Passado (geral) | could / couldn't | She could dance at age 3. |
| Passado (situação específica) | was able to / managed to | We managed to get tickets! |
| Futuro | will be able to | You'll be able to drive next year. |
| Present perfect | have been able to | I haven't been able to call her. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L1/example-01.m4a) How long can you hold your breath under water? — _Por quanto tempo você consegue prender a respiração debaixo d'água?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L1/example-02.m4a) My grandma couldn't read, but she could tell amazing stories. — _Minha avó não sabia ler, mas sabia contar histórias incríveis._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L1/example-03.m4a) After this course, you'll be able to give presentations in English. — _Depois deste curso, você vai conseguir fazer apresentações em inglês._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Can** também significa "poder" no sentido de **permissão** ("Can I go?") e **possibilidade** ("It can be cold in June"). O contexto mostra qual é.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I will can go tomorrow.  
  ✅ **I'll be able to go tomorrow.**  
  Can não tem futuro com will. Use will be able to.
- ❌ I can to speak English.  
  ✅ **I can speak English.**  
  Modais não levam to.
- ❌ Yesterday I could finish the report on time. (sucesso específico)  
  ✅ **Yesterday I managed to / was able to finish the report on time.**  
  Para um sucesso numa situação específica do passado, prefira was able to / managed to.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Could, couldn't e o L mudo

**Could** = "kúd" (L mudo, como would e should). **Couldn't** = "kúdent" — e o T final muitas vezes some: "I kúden(t) sleep". **Able** = "êibol".

## 📖 Reading — It's never too late [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L1/reading.m4a)

Many adults believe that they can't learn new skills after a certain age. "I couldn't learn a language now — my brain is too old," they say. Neuroscience says otherwise. Thanks to neuroplasticity, our brains can create new connections throughout our lives. Children may learn some things faster, but adults have advantages too: they can organize their study, understand grammar rules and connect new information to what they already know. Consider Dona Neusa from Minas Gerais: she couldn't read until she was 70. She went back to school, and two years later she was able to read her first novel. "I've been able to do things I never imagined," she says. Her secret? "I never gave up."

**Questions:**
1. What do many adults believe?
2. What is neuroplasticity?
3. What advantages do adults have?
4. What was Dona Neusa able to do two years after going back to school?

## 📝 Writing

Write about your abilities: past, present and future (100–120 words). Use can, could, be able to and managed to.

**Model:**

> When I was a child, I could climb any tree in my grandparents' garden, and I could speak a bit of Italian with my grandmother. Sadly, I can't do either now! I've never been able to sing well — my family asks me to stop at birthday parties. But I can cook very well, and I can make a perfect feijoada. Last year I managed to finish my first 10K race, which was a big achievement for me. My goal for next year is to be able to have a conversation in English without feeling nervous. I'm sure I'll be able to do it if I practice every day.
