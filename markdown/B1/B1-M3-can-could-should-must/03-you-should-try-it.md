## 🧩 Grammar: Conselhos: should, ought to, had better

**Should / shouldn't** + verbo base → conselho, opinião (o mais comum):
"You **should** see a doctor." / "You **shouldn't** work so late." / "**Should** I call him?"

**Ought to** + verbo base → mesmo sentido de should, um pouco mais formal e menos comum em perguntas e negativas:
"You **ought to** apologize."

**Had better ('d better)** + verbo base → conselho **forte, com aviso** de consequência negativa (é presente/futuro, apesar do "had"):
"You**'d better** leave now, **or** you'll miss your flight."
Negativa: "You**'d better not** tell her."

**Outras formas de aconselhar:**
- **If I were you, I'd** + verbo → "If I were you, I'd take the job." (note: **were** para todas as pessoas)
- **Why don't you** + verbo? → "Why don't you try yoga?"
- **Have you tried** + -ing? → "Have you tried meditating?"
- **I'd recommend** + -ing → "I'd recommend talking to HR."

Lembre-se: **advice** é incontável → "some advice", "a piece of advice" (❌ "an advice", "advices").

| Forma | Força | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Why don't you...? / Have you tried...? | sugestão leve | Why don't you take a break? |
| should / ought to | conselho | You should rest more. |
| If I were you, I'd... | conselho pessoal | If I were you, I'd quit. |
| had better | aviso forte | You'd better call her now. |
| must | muito forte / ordem | You must see this movie! |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L3/example-01.m4a) Should I accept the job offer in São Paulo? — If I were you, I'd sleep on it. — _Devo aceitar a proposta em São Paulo? — Se eu fosse você, pensaria com calma._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L3/example-02.m4a) You'd better back up your files, or you might lose everything. — _É melhor você fazer backup dos arquivos, ou pode perder tudo._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L3/example-03.m4a) Have you tried turning off your phone an hour before bed? — _Você já tentou desligar o celular uma hora antes de dormir?_

> 💡 **Dica:** **Had better** soa como ameaça se usado com chefes ou pessoas mais velhas ("You'd better pay me!"). Com eles, prefira **should** ou **If I were you**.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ You should to rest.  
  ✅ **You should rest.**  
  Should + verbo base, sem to.
- ❌ You had better to go.  
  ✅ **You'd better go.**  
  Had better + verbo base, sem to.
- ❌ Can you give me an advice?  
  ✅ **Can you give me some advice?**  
  Advice é incontável.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: You'd better / ought to

**You'd better** na fala rápida vira "iúd bérer" — e muitas vezes o 'd some: "You better go!" (informal). **Ought to** = "órâ" no americano. **Should** = "shúd" (L mudo).

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L3/pron-01.m4a) You'd better hurry!

## 📖 Reading — Advice from 100-year-olds [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M3-L3/reading.m4a)

What can we learn from people who have lived for a century? Journalists and researchers who have interviewed centenarians around the world have noticed some common advice. "You shouldn't worry so much about money — you can't take it with you," said a 102-year-old woman from Okinawa, Japan. A former farmer from Sardinia, Italy, recommended: "Walk every day, and eat with your family." Many said that people ought to forgive more easily, because holding grudges only hurts yourself. And almost all of them mentioned relationships: "If I were you, I'd call my friends more often. In the end, people are what matter." Interestingly, very few of them talked about work or success. Maybe we'd better listen to them before it's too late.

**Questions:**
1. What did the woman from Okinawa say about money?
2. What did the farmer from Sardinia recommend?
3. Why ought people to forgive more easily?
4. What did very few centenarians talk about?

## 📝 Writing

Write a reply to an advice column letter (120–150 words). Problem: "I've been offered a job abroad, but my family wants me to stay. What should I do?"

**Model:**

> Dear Confused,  
> First of all, congratulations on the offer! It's completely normal to feel divided. Here's my advice. You should start by making a list of pros and cons — not only about the job, but about your life, your goals and your relationships. Then, why don't you talk openly with your family? They probably want you to stay because they'll miss you, not because they don't believe in you. If I were you, I'd also ask the company if you could try it for a year. That way, the decision doesn't feel permanent. Finally, remember: you ought to make this decision for yourself, not for others. Many people regret the chances they didn't take more than the ones they took. Sleep on it — but don't wait too long!  
> Good luck,  
> Aunt Bia

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Conselho não pedido

Em culturas anglo-saxãs, dar **unsolicited advice** (conselho não pedido) pode ser malvisto, principalmente sobre peso, filhos ou dinheiro. Antes, pergunte: **"Do you want my advice, or do you just need to vent?"** (Quer um conselho ou só precisa desabafar?).
