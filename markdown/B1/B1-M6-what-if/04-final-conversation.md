## 🧩 Grammar: Revisão do B1 + estratégias de conversação

**Mapa do B1 — o que você já sabe:**
- **Tag questions:** It's nice, isn't it?
- **Present perfect:** experiências (Have you ever...?), duração (for/since), recentes (already/yet/just), continuous (I've been working...)
- **Modais:** can/could/be able to, could you...?, should/had better, must/have to/mustn't/don't have to, might/must be
- **Causative:** I got my hair cut.
- **Preferências:** I'd rather... / I prefer A to B
- **Verb patterns:** enjoy doing, decide to do, good at doing
- **Perguntas:** Who called? / Who did you call? / Do you know where...?
- **Passiva:** It was built in... / It is said that...
- **Narrativa:** past continuous + simple past + past perfect
- **Condicionais:** zero, first, second + unless, as soon as, I wish

**Estratégias de fluência** (o que nativos fazem o tempo todo):
1. **Ganhar tempo:** "Well...", "Let me think...", "That's a good question."
2. **Contornar palavras que faltam (paraphrasing):** "It's a kind of... / It's something you use to... / It's like..."
3. **Pedir esclarecimento:** "What do you mean by...?", "Could you rephrase that?"
4. **Desenvolver respostas** com a fórmula **A-R-E**: **A**nswer (resposta) → **R**eason (motivo) → **E**xample (exemplo).
   "Would you live abroad? — **Yes, I would.** **Because I love learning about other cultures.** **For example, when I visited Chile, I...**"
5. **Devolver a pergunta:** "What about you?"

| Estratégia | Frases |
|---|---|
| Ganhar tempo | Well... / Let me think... / That's a good question. |
| Paráfrase | It's a kind of... / It's something you use to... / It's like... |
| Esclarecer | What do you mean by...? / Could you rephrase that? |
| Exemplificar | For example... / For instance... / Like when I... |
| Contrastar | On the one hand... on the other hand... / However... |
| Concluir | So, to sum up... / Anyway, that's what I think. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L4/example-01.m4a) That's a good question. Let me think... I'd say yes, because... — _Boa pergunta. Deixa eu pensar... Eu diria que sim, porque..._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L4/example-02.m4a) I don't remember the word, but it's a kind of tool you use to open bottles. — _Não lembro a palavra, mas é um tipo de ferramenta que você usa para abrir garrafas._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L4/example-03.m4a) On the one hand, living abroad is exciting. On the other hand, you miss your family. — _Por um lado, morar fora é empolgante. Por outro lado, você sente falta da família._

> 💡 **Dica:** Na conversa, **fluência** (continuar falando) é mais importante que **precisão** (não errar nada). Nativos também erram, hesitam e se corrigem. Se travar, use uma estratégia em vez de parar ou mudar para o português!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Answering only "Yes" or "No".  
  ✅ **Yes, I would, because... For example...**  
  Respostas curtas matam a conversa. Use A-R-E: resposta, motivo, exemplo.
- ❌ Stopping and saying "Como fala...?" in Portuguese.  
  ✅ **How do you say...? / It's a kind of...**  
  Continue em inglês: peça ajuda ou descreva a palavra.
- ❌ Never asking questions back.  
  ✅ **What about you? / Have you ever...?**  
  Conversa é troca. Devolver perguntas mostra interesse.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Ritmo e pausas naturais

Falar rápido não é falar bem. Use **pausas** curtas entre ideias e **enfatize** as palavras-chave. Hesitações naturais em inglês são "um", "uh", "well", "you know" — não "é...", "tipo..." em português!

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L4/pron-01.m4a) Well... let me think about that.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L4/pron-02.m4a) On the one hand... on the other hand...
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L4/pron-03.m4a) It's a kind of... you know... a thing you use to...

## 📖 Reading — The intermediate plateau [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L4/reading.m4a)

Many English learners feel fast progress at the beginning: every week, they can say new things. Then, around B1, something strange happens: progress seems to stop. This is known as the intermediate plateau. It happens because you can already communicate in most everyday situations, so you're no longer forced to learn new language. You keep using the same words and structures that "work". How can you break the plateau? Teachers suggest three things. First, consume more authentic content — podcasts, series, YouTube channels and articles about topics you love. Second, push yourself to use new vocabulary on purpose, even if you make mistakes. Third, get feedback: record yourself, write texts and ask someone to correct them. The plateau isn't a sign that you've stopped learning. It's a sign that you're ready for the next level.

**Questions:**
1. What is the intermediate plateau?
2. Why does it happen?
3. What are the three suggestions?
4. What is the plateau a sign of, according to the text?

## 📝 Writing

Write a reflective essay (150–180 words): "My English journey: where I was, where I am and where I'm going." Use at least 6 different B1 structures.

**Model:**

> When I started learning English, I couldn't understand a single song, and I was terrified of speaking. I had tried to learn several times before, but I had always given up after a few months. This time was different. I've been studying for almost two years now, and I've learned much more than grammar. I've learned to stop translating everything in my head and to keep talking even when I make mistakes. The most difficult part was the present perfect — I still sometimes say "I live here since..."! Last month, I had a video call with a client from Canada, and I didn't need my boss to help me. That was the moment I realized how far I'd come. I wish I had more time to study, but I'm proud of my progress. As soon as I finish this level, I'm going to start B1+. If everything goes well, I'll take an international exam next year. And unless something changes, I'll never stop learning.
