## 🧩 Grammar: Second conditional + I wish

**Second conditional** → situações **imaginárias, hipotéticas ou improváveis** (no presente ou futuro):
**If + past simple, would + verbo**
- "If I **won** the lottery, I**'d buy** a house in the mountains."
- "If I **had** more time, I**'d learn** to play the guitar."
- "What **would** you **do** if you **lost** your job?"

O passado aqui **não fala do passado** — ele mostra **distância da realidade** (como o "se eu ganhasse" do português).

**If I were...** → com o verbo to be, use **were** para todas as pessoas (formal e recomendado): "If I **were** rich..." / "If she **were** here..." (Na fala informal, "if I was" também aparece.)

**Could / might** também funcionam: "If I had a car, I **could** visit you more often."

**First x second:**
- "If it **rains** tomorrow, I**'ll** stay home." → possível, realista
- "If it **snowed** in Rio, I**'d** build a snowman." → imaginário, improvável

**I wish + past simple** → desejo de que o **presente** fosse diferente:
- "I **wish** I **lived** near the beach." (mas não moro)
- "I **wish** I **were** taller."
- "I **wish** I **could** speak Japanese." (habilidade → could)

| Realidade | Second conditional / wish |
|---|---|
| I don't have money. | If I had money, I'd travel. |
| I'm not you. | If I were you, I'd apologize. |
| I can't drive. | If I could drive, I'd go by car. / I wish I could drive. |
| I live far from work. | I wish I lived closer to work. |
| She isn't here. | If she were here, she'd know what to do. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L2/example-01.m4a) If I could have any superpower, I'd choose teleportation. No more traffic! — _Se eu pudesse ter qualquer superpoder, escolheria teletransporte. Chega de trânsito!_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L2/example-02.m4a) Would you live abroad if you had the chance? — _Você moraria fora se tivesse a chance?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L2/example-03.m4a) I wish I had more free time, but I wouldn't change my job. — _Queria ter mais tempo livre, mas não mudaria de emprego._

> 💡 **Dica:** O **'d** pode ser **would** ou **had**. No second conditional, a parte com **'d** é sempre **would** + verbo base: "I'd travel" = I would travel.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ If I would have money, I would travel.  
  ✅ **If I had money, I would travel.**  
  Depois de if, use o past simple (não would).
- ❌ If I was you, I'd go. (em provas)  
  ✅ **If I were you, I'd go.**  
  A forma padrão é If I were.
- ❌ I wish I have a car.  
  ✅ **I wish I had a car.**  
  I wish (presente) + past simple.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: I'd, you'd, wouldn't

**I'd** = "áid", **you'd** = "iúd", **we'd** = "uíd" — o D é curto, mas não pode sumir ("I travel" ≠ "I'd travel"). **Wouldn't** = "uúdent" (L mudo, T leve).

## 📖 Reading — Would money make you happier? [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M6-L2/reading.m4a)

If you suddenly became a millionaire, would you be happier? Most people say yes immediately. But research on lottery winners shows a more complicated picture. In a famous study from the 1970s, psychologists compared lottery winners with ordinary people and found that, after some time, the winners weren't much happier — they had adapted to their new lives. More recent research suggests that money does increase happiness, especially when it removes financial stress. But how you use it matters. People who spend money on experiences, on other people, or on buying time — like paying someone to do the chores they hate — tend to report more satisfaction than those who buy expensive things. So, if you won the lottery tomorrow, what would make you happier: a sports car, or more time with the people you love?

**Questions:**
1. What did the 1970s study find?
2. When does money increase happiness, according to recent research?
3. How should you spend money to be happier?
4. What does "buying time" mean in the text?

## 📝 Writing

Write an opinion text (120–150 words): "If I could change one thing in the world, it would be...". Use the second conditional and I wish.

**Model:**

> If I could change one thing in the world, I would give every child access to good education. If all children went to good schools, they would have more opportunities and fewer of them would live in poverty. Education would also help people make better decisions about health, money and the environment. If I were a politician, I would pay teachers much better salaries, because they are the most important professionals in any country. I would also make sure that every school had internet, libraries and healthy food. I know it's not simple — it would cost a lot of money and take many years. But I wish more leaders thought about the long term. If we invested in children today, the whole world would be different in twenty years.
