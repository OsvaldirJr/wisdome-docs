## 🧩 Grammar: Tempos narrativos combinados

Para contar um evento passado de forma completa, combine **quatro** estruturas:

1. **Past continuous** → o **cenário**, o que estava acontecendo:
"It **was raining** heavily, and most people **were sleeping**."

2. **Past perfect** → o que tinha acontecido **antes** (contexto, causas):
"It **had been raining** for three days, and the river **had risen** two meters." (had been + -ing = past perfect continuous, para duração antes de um ponto do passado)

3. **Simple past** → os **eventos principais**, em sequência:
"At 3 a.m., the water **reached** the houses. Families **climbed** onto their roofs."

4. **Past passive** → o que **aconteceu com** pessoas e coisas:
"Thousands of people **were evacuated**, and two bridges **were destroyed**."

**Ordem típica de uma reportagem:** o que aconteceu (simple past/passive) → contexto e causas (past perfect) → detalhes do momento (past continuous) → consequências (passive) → situação atual (present / present perfect).

| Função | Tempo | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Cenário | past continuous | People were sleeping when... |
| Antes do evento | past perfect (continuous) | It had been raining for days. |
| Evento principal | simple past | The river overflowed at 3 a.m. |
| Consequência | past passive | 200 homes were damaged. |
| Situação agora | present perfect / present | The water has gone down. Families need help. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M5-L3/example-01.m4a) In May 2024, after it had been raining for weeks, large areas of Rio Grande do Sul were flooded. — _Em maio de 2024, depois de semanas de chuva, grandes áreas do Rio Grande do Sul foram inundadas._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M5-L3/example-02.m4a) We were having dinner when we heard the alarm. — _Estávamos jantando quando ouvimos o alarme._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M5-L3/example-03.m4a) Thousands of volunteers came from all over the country, and tons of donations were sent. — _Milhares de voluntários vieram de todo o país, e toneladas de doações foram enviadas._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Past perfect continuous** (had been + -ing) é o "primo" do present perfect continuous, só que no passado: "It **had been raining** for days **when** the dam broke." (Estava chovendo havia dias quando a barragem rompeu.)

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Many houses destroyed by the flood.  
  ✅ **Many houses were destroyed by the flood.**  
  A passiva precisa do verbo be.
- ❌ We slept when the earthquake started.  
  ✅ **We were sleeping when the earthquake started.**  
  Ação em andamento (cenário) = past continuous.
- ❌ It rained for three days when the river overflowed.  
  ✅ **It had been raining for three days when the river overflowed.**  
  Duração antes de outro ponto do passado = past perfect continuous.

## 📖 Reading — Climate change and extreme weather [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M5-L3/reading.m4a)

Scientists have been warning us for decades: as the planet gets warmer, extreme weather events become more frequent and more intense. In recent years, the world has seen record-breaking heatwaves in Europe, devastating wildfires in Canada and Australia, and severe floods in Pakistan and southern Brazil. Warmer air can hold more water, which means heavier rain when storms happen. At the same time, some regions are suffering from longer droughts. In 2023 and 2024, parts of the Amazon experienced one of the worst droughts on record: rivers dropped to historic lows, and entire communities were left isolated because boats couldn't travel. Experts say that we need two kinds of action: reducing greenhouse gas emissions and adapting our cities — with better drainage, early-warning systems and protected green areas.

**Questions:**
1. What have scientists been warning us about?
2. Why does warmer air cause heavier rain?
3. What happened in the Amazon in 2023 and 2024?
4. What two kinds of action do experts recommend?

## 📝 Writing

Write a news report (120–150 words) about a natural disaster (real or invented). Include the setting, causes, main events, consequences and current situation.

**Model:**

> HEAVY RAIN CAUSES LANDSLIDES IN THE MOUNTAINS  
>   
> On Tuesday night, a series of landslides hit three towns in the mountain region. It had been raining for five days, and the soil was extremely wet. At around 11 p.m., while most residents were sleeping, part of a hillside collapsed onto a residential street. Fortunately, the Civil Defense had sent warning messages earlier that afternoon, and many families had already left their homes. More than 1,500 people were evacuated, and twenty houses were destroyed. Rescue teams worked through the night. Today, schools and gyms are being used as shelters, and donations of water, food and clothes have been arriving from across the state. Authorities say more rain is expected this weekend.
