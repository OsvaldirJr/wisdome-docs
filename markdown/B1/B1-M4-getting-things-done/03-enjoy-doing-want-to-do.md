## 🎯 Nesta aula você vai
- Usar verbos seguidos de -ing (enjoy, avoid, finish, mind...)
- Usar verbos seguidos de to + verbo (want, decide, hope, plan...)
- Usar -ing depois de preposições (interested in, good at...)

## 🔥 Warm-up
"Gosto de cozinhar", "decidi viajar", "parei de fumar". Em português, tudo é infinitivo. Em inglês, cada verbo tem seu "par" preferido: -ing ou to. Vamos descobrir os mais importantes.

- What do you enjoy doing on weekends?
- What have you decided to change this year?

## 🧩 Grammar: Verb + -ing / verb + to

**1. Verbos + -ing (gerúndio):**
enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, keep, give up, consider, suggest, miss, practice, can't stand, don't mind
"I **enjoy reading**." / "She **gave up smoking**." / "Do you **mind waiting**?"

**2. Verbos + to + verbo (infinitivo):**
want, need, decide, hope, plan, expect, agree, refuse, promise, learn, afford, manage, offer, would like
"I **want to travel**." / "They **decided to move**." / "I can't **afford to buy** a car."

**3. Preposição + -ing (sempre!):**
"I'm interested **in learning**." / "She's good **at drawing**." / "Thanks **for coming**." / "I'm thinking **of moving**." / "**Before leaving**, check the windows."
Atenção: **look forward to** e **be used to** terminam em preposição **to**, então pedem **-ing**: "I look forward to **meeting** you."

**4. Verbos que mudam de sentido:**
- **stop + -ing** = parar de fazer: "I **stopped smoking**." · **stop + to** = parar para fazer: "I **stopped to buy** water."
- **remember + to** = lembrar de fazer (antes): "**Remember to lock** the door!" · **remember + -ing** = lembrar de ter feito: "I **remember meeting** him in 2010."
- **try + to** = tentar (esforço): "I **tried to open** the jar." · **try + -ing** = experimentar: "**Try drinking** tea with ginger."

**5. Like, love, hate, start, begin, continue** → aceitam os dois com pouca diferença: "I like **swimming** / **to swim**."

| + -ing | + to + verbo | preposição + -ing |
|---|---|---|
| enjoy cooking | want to cook | good at cooking |
| avoid driving | decide to drive | afraid of driving |
| finish working | plan to work | tired of working |
| miss living there | hope to live there | used to living there |
| suggest going | agree to go | look forward to going |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L3/example-01.m4a) I've been considering moving abroad, but I can't afford to leave my job yet. — _Tenho considerado morar fora, mas ainda não tenho condições de deixar meu emprego._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L3/example-02.m4a) Remember to send the report! — Don't worry, I remember sending it yesterday. — _Lembre de enviar o relatório! — Não se preocupe, lembro de ter enviado ontem._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L3/example-03.m4a) I'm really looking forward to seeing you next week. — _Estou muito ansioso para te ver semana que vem._

> 💡 **Dica:** Aprenda esses verbos **em chunks**, não em listas: "enjoy doing", "want to do", "good at doing", "look forward to doing". Repetir os blocos é mais eficiente que decorar regras.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I enjoy to cook.  
  ✅ **I enjoy cooking.**  
  Enjoy + -ing.
- ❌ I look forward to see you.  
  ✅ **I look forward to seeing you.**  
  Look forward to + -ing (o to é preposição).
- ❌ I'm interested in learn Japanese.  
  ✅ **I'm interested in learning Japanese.**  
  Depois de preposição, sempre -ing.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Want to = "wanna", going to = "gonna", have to = "hafta"

Com verbos + to, a fala informal funde as palavras: **want to** → "**wâna**", **got to** → "**gára**", **have to** → "**réfta**". Você vai ouvir muito em séries: "I wanna go", "I gotta go". Use na fala informal; na escrita, a forma completa.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L3/pron-01.m4a) I want to go. ("ái wâna gôu")

## 📖 Reading — Why we procrastinate [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L3/reading.m4a)

We all know the feeling: you need to finish a report, but you keep checking your phone, cleaning your desk, or watching "just one more" video. Most people think procrastination is about being lazy, but psychologists disagree. Research suggests that procrastination is mainly about avoiding negative emotions — boredom, anxiety, fear of failure. When a task makes us feel bad, our brain decides to look for something more pleasant. So how can we stop procrastinating? Experts suggest breaking big tasks into tiny steps ("open the document" instead of "write the report"), using a timer to work for just 25 minutes, and forgiving yourself for procrastinating in the past. Interestingly, people who forgive themselves are less likely to procrastinate again. Now, stop reading and go do that thing you've been avoiding!

**Questions:**
1. What do most people think procrastination is about?
2. What is it really about, according to research?
3. Give two tips from experts.
4. What's interesting about people who forgive themselves?
