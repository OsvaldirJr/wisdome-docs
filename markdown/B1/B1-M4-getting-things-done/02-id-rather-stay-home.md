## 🧩 Grammar: prefer / would prefer / would rather

**Prefer** → preferência **geral** (sempre):
- prefer + **substantivo** + **to** + substantivo: "I prefer **tea to** coffee."
- prefer + **-ing** + **to** + -ing: "I prefer **walking to** driving."

**Would prefer ('d prefer)** → preferência **específica** (agora, nesta situação):
- 'd prefer + **to** + verbo: "I'd prefer **to stay** home tonight."
- 'd prefer + substantivo: "I'd prefer a window seat."

**Would rather ('d rather)** → preferência **específica**, muito comum na fala:
- 'd rather + verbo **base** (sem to!): "I'd rather **stay** home."
- comparação com **than**: "I'd rather **walk than take** the bus."
- negativa: "I'd rather **not** go."
- pergunta: "**Would you rather** watch a movie **or** go out?"

Resumo das estruturas:
- prefer A **to** B
- would prefer **to** do A (**rather than** do B)
- would rather do A **than** do B

| Estrutura | Exemplo | Uso |
|---|---|---|
| prefer + noun + to + noun | I prefer dogs to cats. | geral |
| prefer + -ing + to + -ing | She prefers reading to watching TV. | geral |
| would prefer + to + verbo | I'd prefer to eat at home. | específico |
| would rather + verbo | I'd rather eat at home. | específico |
| would rather + verbo + than + verbo | I'd rather cook than order food. | comparação |
| would rather not | I'd rather not say. | recusa educada |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L2/example-01.m4a) Would you rather live in a big city or a small town? — _Você preferiria morar numa cidade grande ou numa cidade pequena?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L2/example-02.m4a) I usually prefer texting to calling, but today I'd rather talk in person. — _Geralmente prefiro mensagem a ligação, mas hoje prefiro falar pessoalmente._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L2/example-03.m4a) "Pizza or sushi?" "I don't mind. It's up to you." — _"Pizza ou sushi?" "Tanto faz. Você decide."_

> 💡 **Dica:** **I'd rather not** é uma ótima forma educada de recusar sem dar explicações: "How much do you earn?" — "**I'd rather not say.**"

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I'd rather to stay home.  
  ✅ **I'd rather stay home.**  
  Would rather + verbo base, sem to.
- ❌ I prefer coffee than tea.  
  ✅ **I prefer coffee to tea.**  
  Prefer A to B (não than).
- ❌ I'd prefer stay home.  
  ✅ **I'd prefer to stay home.**  
  Would prefer + to + verbo.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: I'd rather = "áid réder"

**I'd rather** soa "**áid réder**" (americano) ou "**áid ráde**" (britânico). O 'd é curto mas importante — sem ele, "I rather" está errado. **Either** e **neither** têm duas pronúncias: "íder / áider".

## 📖 Reading — Introverts and extroverts at work [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L2/reading.m4a)

Some people would rather work in a quiet room alone; others prefer a busy open office full of conversation. Psychologists often describe this difference using the terms introvert and extrovert. Introverts usually prefer deep one-on-one conversations to big group meetings, and many would rather write an email than make a phone call. Extroverts, on the other hand, often get energy from people and prefer brainstorming out loud. Most people, however, are somewhere in the middle — sometimes called ambiverts. The problem is that many workplaces are designed for extroverts: open-plan offices, constant meetings and team-building events. Smart companies now offer options: quiet zones, "no-meeting" days and flexible work. After all, people do their best work when they can choose how they work.

**Questions:**
1. What do introverts usually prefer?
2. Where do extroverts get energy from?
3. What are ambiverts?
4. What options do smart companies offer?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short opinion text (120–150 words): "Would you rather live in a big city or in the countryside?" Give reasons and use prefer, would rather and would prefer.

**Model:**

> I was born in São Paulo, and I've always lived in big cities. However, if I had to choose now, I'd rather live in the countryside. I used to prefer the energy of the city to the quiet of small towns, but things have changed. Today I'd rather spend my weekends in nature than in shopping malls, and I prefer cooking with fresh food to ordering delivery. Of course, there are disadvantages: fewer job opportunities and longer distances to hospitals. That's why I'd prefer to live in a small town near a big city, not in the middle of nowhere. With remote work, I think it's possible to have the best of both worlds: a calm life and a good career.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Either is fine"

Quando alguém oferece duas opções e você não tem preferência, diga **"Either is fine"** ou **"I'm easy"** (informal britânico, = tanto faz). Mas atenção: em algumas culturas, "I don't mind" repetido demais pode parecer falta de opinião. Às vezes, é educado escolher!
