## 🧩 Grammar: Causative: get / have + objeto + particípio

Quando **outra pessoa** faz algo **para você** (geralmente um serviço pago), use o **causative**:

**get / have + objeto + particípio passado**
- I **got my hair cut**. (o cabeleireiro cortou — não eu)
- She **had her car washed**.
- We need to **get the roof repaired**.

Compare:
- "I **cut my hair**." = eu mesmo cortei (com tesoura em casa! ✂️)
- "I **got my hair cut**." = cortei no salão

**Get** é mais informal e comum na fala; **have** é um pouco mais formal. O sentido é o mesmo.

O causative funciona em **todos os tempos** — só muda o get/have:
- presente: I **get** my nails done every two weeks.
- passado: I **had** my eyes tested yesterday.
- futuro: I**'m going to get** my car fixed.
- present perfect: Have you **had** your passport renewed?
- modal: Where **can** I **get** this printed?

**Perguntas:** "Where **did** you **get** your hair **cut**?" / "Where can I **have** my shoes **repaired**?"

| Eu mesmo faço | Outra pessoa faz (causative) |
|---|---|
| I fixed my bike. | I got my bike fixed. |
| She cleans her house. | She has her house cleaned. |
| We painted the kitchen. | We had the kitchen painted. |
| I'm going to cut my hair. | I'm going to get my hair cut. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L1/example-01.m4a) I dropped my phone again. I need to get the screen replaced. — _Deixei o celular cair de novo. Preciso trocar a tela._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L1/example-02.m4a) Where did you get your glasses made? They look great! — _Onde você fez seus óculos? Ficaram ótimos!_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L1/example-03.m4a) We're having our apartment painted next week, so we're staying with my parents. — _Vamos pintar o apartamento semana que vem, então vamos ficar com meus pais._

> 💡 **Dica:** Expressões fixas muito úteis: **get your nails done** (fazer as unhas), **get a tattoo** (fazer uma tatuagem), **get your eyes tested** (fazer exame de vista), **get your teeth cleaned** (fazer limpeza nos dentes).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I cut my hair yesterday. (no salão)  
  ✅ **I got my hair cut yesterday.**  
  Se outra pessoa cortou, use o causative.
- ❌ I need to fix my car in the mechanic.  
  ✅ **I need to get my car fixed at the mechanic's.**  
  O mecânico conserta: causative + at the mechanic's.
- ❌ I got my phone fix.  
  ✅ **I got my phone fixed.**  
  O verbo vai no particípio (fixed).

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Particípios com -ed: /t/, /d/, /ɪd/

No causative, o particípio fica no fim e é bem audível. Lembre os três sons: **fixed** ("fikst"), **cleaned** ("klínd"), **painted** ("pêintid"). E **cut**, **done**, **made** são irregulares.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L1/pron-01.m4a) I got my phone fixed.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L1/pron-03.m4a) Where can I get this cleaned?

## 📖 Reading — The right to repair [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M4-L1/reading.m4a)

Have you ever had a phone repaired, only to be told that it's cheaper to buy a new one? You're not alone. For years, many manufacturers have made it difficult for customers to get their devices fixed: special screws, glued batteries, and parts that only official stores can buy. Now, a global movement called "Right to Repair" is fighting back. The European Union and several US states have passed laws requiring companies to sell spare parts and repair manuals for many products. Supporters say this will reduce electronic waste — one of the fastest-growing types of waste in the world — and save consumers money. In Brazil, small repair shops have always been part of the culture: we get our shoes resoled, our clothes altered and our appliances fixed. Maybe the rest of the world is finally catching up.

**Questions:**
1. How have manufacturers made repairs difficult?
2. What do the new laws require?
3. What are two benefits, according to supporters?
4. What does the text say about Brazil?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — DIY culture

Nos EUA e no Reino Unido, mão de obra é cara, então a cultura **DIY (Do It Yourself)** é muito forte: pessoas pintam a própria casa, montam móveis e consertam coisas com tutoriais. Lojas como Home Depot e IKEA vivem disso. No Brasil, é mais comum **get things done** — contratar alguém.

## ✅ Resumo
- get / have + objeto + particípio = alguém fez para mim.
- I cut my hair (eu mesmo) x I got my hair cut (no salão).
- Funciona em todos os tempos (got, 'm getting, will get...).
- at the mechanic's / dry cleaner's / tailor's.
