## 🧩 Grammar: Do you mind if...? / Would you mind if...?

Para pedir **permissão** de forma educada:
- **Do you mind if** I + **simple present**? → "Do you mind if I **call** you Ana?"
- **Would you mind if** I + **simple past**? (mais formal) → "Would you mind if I **called** you Ana?"

⚠️ **Atenção à resposta!** "Mind" significa "se importar". Então:
- **"No, not at all." / "No, go ahead."** = **pode sim** (não me importo) ✅
- **"Yes, I do mind." / "Actually, I'd rather you didn't."** = **não pode** ❌

Para pedir que **outra pessoa** faça algo: **Would you mind + verbo-ing?** → "Would you mind **spelling** your name?"

**Títulos:**
- **Mr.** (homens) + sobrenome: Mr. Smith
- **Ms.** (mulheres — neutro, o mais seguro no trabalho) + sobrenome: Ms. Lopez
- **Mrs.** (mulher casada) / **Miss** (jovem/solteira — pouco usado hoje)
- **Dr.** + sobrenome (médicos e doutores) · **Professor** + sobrenome (professores universitários)
- Nunca use título + primeiro nome: ❌ "Mr. John" (comum no Brasil, estranho em inglês) → ✅ "Mr. Smith" ou "John".

| Pedido | Sim, pode | Não, prefiro que não |
|---|---|---|
| Do you mind if I sit here? | No, not at all. / Go ahead. | Sorry, it's taken. |
| Would you mind if I opened the window? | No, of course not. | Actually, I'm a bit cold. |
| Would you mind waiting a minute? | Not at all. | Sorry, I'm in a hurry. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L4/example-01.m4a) Do you mind if I call you Bia? — Not at all, everyone does! — _Você se importa se eu te chamar de Bia? — De jeito nenhum, todo mundo chama!_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L4/example-02.m4a) Would you mind spelling your last name for me? — _Você se importaria de soletrar seu sobrenome para mim?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L4/example-03.m4a) Good morning, Dr. Patel. Thank you for seeing me. — _Bom dia, Dr. Patel. Obrigado por me receber._

> 💡 **Dica:** Se não sabe como tratar alguém num e-mail, use **nome completo**: "Dear Alex Morgan," — evita erros de gênero e de título. Muitas pessoas também indicam os **pronomes** na assinatura (she/her, he/him, they/them).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Hello, Mr. Paulo!  
  ✅ **Hello, Mr. Santos! / Hello, Paulo!**  
  Em inglês, títulos vão com o sobrenome, nunca com o primeiro nome.
- ❌ "Do you mind if I sit here?" "Yes, sit!"  
  ✅ **"No, not at all. Go ahead!"**  
  Mind = se importar. "Yes" significaria "sim, me importo"!
- ❌ Would you mind to wait?  
  ✅ **Would you mind waiting?**  
  Would you mind + -ing.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Mr. / Mrs. / Ms. / Miss

Diferencie bem: **Mr.** = "míster" · **Mrs.** = "mísiz" · **Ms.** = "míz" (com som de Z zumbindo) · **Miss** = "mís" (S chiado). Ms. e Miss são os mais confundidos!

**Teacher:** The way you open and close an email shows your tone. For a formal email to someone you don't know, start with "Dear Mr. Lee" or "Dear Ms. Santos". If you don't know the name, "Dear Hiring Manager" is better than "To whom it may concern", which sounds old-fashioned. For colleagues, "Hi Ana" is perfect. Close formal emails with "Kind regards" or "Best regards". "Cheers" is friendly, especially in the UK and Australia, but don't use it with a new client. And please — never write "Kisses" or "Hugs" in a business email, even if Brazilians do it with friends!

## 📖 Reading — Is "Mr." disappearing? [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L4/reading.m4a)

In many English-speaking workplaces, titles are becoming less common. Tech companies in Silicon Valley are famous for their informal culture: the CEO is "Mark" or "Sundar", and nobody wears a tie. Many universities and hospitals, however, still prefer titles like "Professor" and "Dr.", especially with students and patients. In the UK, older generations often expect "Mr." or "Mrs." until they say otherwise. Another change is the use of "Ms." instead of "Mrs." or "Miss": it doesn't reveal whether a woman is married, and it's now the standard in professional situations. Some people prefer gender-neutral titles like "Mx." The safest strategy? Follow the other person's lead. If someone signs an email "Jennifer", you can reply "Hi Jennifer". If they sign "Dr. Jennifer Adams", start with "Dear Dr. Adams".

**Questions:**
1. Where are titles becoming less common?
2. Where are titles still preferred?
3. Why is "Ms." the standard now?
4. What's the safest strategy?

## 📝 Writing

Write two short emails with the same message (asking to reschedule a meeting): one formal to a new client, one informal to a colleague (50–70 words each).

**Model:**

> Formal:  
> Dear Ms. Kerr,  
> I'm afraid I need to reschedule our meeting on Thursday due to an unexpected trip. Would it be possible to meet on Monday or Tuesday next week instead? Please let me know what time suits you best. I apologize for any inconvenience.  
> Best regards,  
> Bruna Carvalho  
>   
> Informal:  
> Hi Jake,  
> Sorry, something came up and I can't make Thursday's meeting. Do you mind if we move it to Monday or Tuesday? Whatever works for you!

> Bru

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Seu" e "Dona" não têm tradução direta

No Brasil, dizemos "Seu João" e "Dona Maria" — título + primeiro nome, com respeito e carinho. Em inglês não há equivalente: "Mr. John" soa errado. Com pessoas mais velhas, use **Mr./Mrs. + sobrenome** até que elas digam **"Please, call me..."**.

## ✅ Resumo
- Título + sobrenome (Mr. Smith), nunca + primeiro nome.
- Ms. = padrão profissional para mulheres.
- Do you mind if I + presente? / Would you mind if I + passado?
- Resposta "No, not at all" = pode sim!
