## 🧩 Grammar: Be supposed to · It's + adjetivo + to + verbo

**Be supposed to + verbo** = o que é **esperado** por regra, costume ou combinação ("é para...", "deveria"):
- In Japan, you**'re supposed to** bow when you greet someone.
- You**'re not supposed to** eat on the subway in Singapore.
- **Am I supposed to** tip here?
- No passado = o que deveria ter acontecido (e não aconteceu): "I **was supposed to** call her, but I forgot."

**It's + adjetivo + to + verbo** para falar de costumes:
- **It's polite to** bring flowers.
- **It's rude to** blow your nose at the table.
- **It's customary to** shake hands.
- **It's (not) acceptable to** arrive late.
- **It's common to** kiss on the cheek.

Para negar o costume, use **not**: "It's **not** polite **to**..." ou "You'**re not supposed to**...". Para opções: "You **don't have to** tip, but you **can**."

| Estrutura | Exemplo | Sentido |
|---|---|---|
| be supposed to | You're supposed to arrive on time. | regra/expectativa |
| be not supposed to | You're not supposed to touch the art. | proibido por regra |
| was supposed to | The train was supposed to leave at 8. | expectativa não cumprida |
| It's polite to | It's polite to say thank you. | educado |
| It's rude to | It's rude to interrupt. | grosseiro |
| It's customary to | It's customary to tip 15%. | costume |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L3/example-01.m4a) In many Asian countries, you're supposed to take off your shoes before entering a home. — _Em muitos países asiáticos, você deve tirar os sapatos antes de entrar numa casa._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L3/example-02.m4a) It's rude to check your phone during a business meal in Japan. — _É falta de educação olhar o celular durante uma refeição de negócios no Japão._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L3/example-03.m4a) The meeting was supposed to start at 9, but the boss was late. — _A reunião era para começar às 9, mas o chefe se atrasou._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Supposed** se pronuncia "sapôust" — o D quase some antes do "to": "sapôusta".

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ You are suppose to arrive early.  
  ✅ **You are supposed to arrive early.**  
  A forma é sempre supposed (com -d).
- ❌ Is polite to bring wine.  
  ✅ **It's polite to bring wine.**  
  A frase precisa do sujeito it.
- ❌ It's educated to say thank you.  
  ✅ **It's polite to say thank you.**  
  "Educado" (maneiras) = polite. Educated = que tem estudo, instruído.

## 📖 Reading — Tipping around the world [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L3/reading.m4a)

Tipping is one of the most confusing parts of traveling. In the United States, servers depend on tips because their salaries are low, so you're supposed to leave 15 to 20 percent in restaurants. Not tipping is seen as very rude. In Japan, it's the opposite: tipping can be considered strange or even insulting, because good service is simply expected. In most of Europe, a service charge is often included in the bill, and leaving a few extra coins is a nice gesture but not required. In Brazil, restaurants usually add 10% to the bill, and you can choose not to pay it — although most people do. The golden rule? Check the local custom before you travel, and when you're not sure, ask: "Is service included?"

**Questions:**
1. Why are you supposed to tip in the US?
2. How can tipping be seen in Japan?
3. What's the situation in most of Europe?
4. What question can you ask when you're not sure?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Gestos que mudam de sentido

O gesto de **"OK"** (polegar e indicador em círculo) é positivo nos EUA, mas ofensivo no Brasil — e em alguns países também. O **polegar para cima** é positivo na maioria dos lugares, mas pode ser rude em partes do Oriente Médio. Na dúvida, use palavras!

- be supposed to = é esperado / é para.
- was supposed to = era para (e não aconteceu).
- It's polite / rude / customary + to + verbo.
- polite = educado (educated = instruído).
