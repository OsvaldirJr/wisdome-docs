## 🧩 Grammar: Tag questions

**Tag questions** são as perguntinhas no fim da frase, como o nosso **"né?"**, **"não é?"**, **"certo?"**. Servem para **confirmar** algo ou **puxar conversa**.

**Regra de ouro:** frase **afirmativa** → tag **negativa**; frase **negativa** → tag **afirmativa**.

A tag repete o **auxiliar** (ou to be / modal) da frase e usa um **pronome**:
- It**'s** cold, **isn't it**?
- You **work** here, **don't you**? (simple present → do/does)
- She **went** home, **didn't she**? (simple past → did)
- They **can** swim, **can't they**?
- You **haven't** been to Japan, **have you**?
- He **won't** come, **will he**?

**Casos especiais:**
- **I am** → **aren't I**? ("I'm late, aren't I?")
- **Let's** → **shall we**? ("Let's go, shall we?")
- Imperativo → **will you?** ("Close the door, will you?")
- **There is** → **isn't there**?

**Entonação:** voz **descendo** ↘ = você tem certeza, só quer concordância (small talk). Voz **subindo** ↗ = pergunta real, você não tem certeza.

| Frase | Tag | Tempo / auxiliar |
|---|---|---|
| It's a great party, | isn't it? | to be |
| You live nearby, | don't you? | simple present |
| She doesn't eat meat, | does she? | simple present (neg.) |
| They met in college, | didn't they? | simple past |
| You've been here before, | haven't you? | present perfect |
| We can't park here, | can we? | modal |
| I'm on the list, | aren't I? | exceção |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L1/example-01.m4a) This coffee is really good, isn't it? ↘ — _Esse café é muito bom, né?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L1/example-02.m4a) You're Paula's brother, aren't you? ↗ — _Você é irmão da Paula, não é? (dúvida real)_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L1/example-03.m4a) We haven't met before, have we? — _A gente não se conhece, né?_

> 💡 **Dica:** Na dúvida, falantes nativos usam formas informais como **"..., right?"** ou **"..., yeah?"** — funcionam com qualquer frase e são ótimas para começar.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ You live in Recife, isn't it?  
  ✅ **You live in Recife, don't you?**  
  A tag repete o auxiliar da frase (simple present → do), não é sempre "isn't it".
- ❌ She can't drive, can't she?  
  ✅ **She can't drive, can she?**  
  Frase negativa pede tag afirmativa.
- ❌ I'm right, am not I?  
  ✅ **I'm right, aren't I?**  
  A tag de I am é a exceção: aren't I.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Entonação das tag questions

A mesma frase muda de sentido pela entonação. **"You're coming, aren't you? ↘"** (tenho certeza, confirmando). **"You're coming, aren't you? ↗"** (não tenho certeza, quero saber). No small talk, quase sempre é ↘.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L1/pron-01.m4a) Nice weather, isn't it? ↘
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L1/pron-02.m4a) You work in marketing, don't you? ↗
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L1/pron-03.m4a) We've met before, haven't we? ↘

## 📖 Reading — Why small talk matters [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L1/reading.m4a)

Many people hate small talk. "It's superficial," they say. But research in psychology suggests the opposite: short, friendly conversations with strangers — the barista, a neighbor, someone on the train — can make us happier and less lonely. In one study, commuters who talked to a stranger reported a more positive journey than those who sat in silence, even though most of them expected the opposite. Small talk is also the first step to deeper conversations: we rarely make friends or business contacts without it. The secret is curiosity. Ask open questions ("What brings you here?"), listen to the answer, and ask a follow-up. Safe topics include the weather, the place you're in, travel, food and work. Avoid politics, religion, money and personal appearance — at least until you know the person better.

**Questions:**
1. What does research suggest about small talk?
2. What happened in the study with commuters?
3. What is "the secret" of good small talk?
4. Name three topics to avoid.

## 📝 Writing

Write a short guide (100–120 words) for a foreign colleague: "How to make small talk in Brazil" — safe topics, topics to avoid and three useful phrases.

**Model:**

> Small talk in Brazil is easy — Brazilians love to chat! Good topics are food, football, music, travel and the weather (especially if it's very hot). People will probably ask where you're from and if you like Brazil — say yes! Brazilians are usually comfortable with personal questions about family, but avoid politics with people you've just met: it can get heated. Three useful phrases: "Tudo bem?" (How are you?), "Que calor, né?" (It's so hot, isn't it?) and "Você é de onde?" (Where are you from?). Finally, don't be surprised if people stand close or touch your arm when they talk — it's just friendliness.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Have a good one!"

Para encerrar conversas curtas nos EUA, são comuns **"Have a good one!"**, **"Take care!"** e **"Catch you later!"**. No Reino Unido, ouve-se **"Cheers!"** (obrigado/tchau) e **"Mind how you go!"**. E o famoso **"Nice weather, isn't it?"** é quase um esporte nacional britânico.

## ✅ Resumo
- Afirmativa → tag negativa; negativa → tag afirmativa.
- A tag repete o auxiliar: do/does/did, have, can, will, be.
- I am → aren't I? · Let's → shall we?
- ↘ confirmar · ↗ perguntar de verdade.
