## 🧩 Grammar: Present perfect: experiências

O **present perfect** liga o passado ao presente. O primeiro uso: **experiências de vida**, quando **não importa quando** aconteceu — só se aconteceu ou não.

**Estrutura:** **have / has** + **particípio passado**
- I**'ve** (I have) **been** to Chile.
- She**'s** (she has) **eaten** snails.
- I **haven't** seen that movie. / He **hasn't** flown before.
- **Have** you **ever** **tried** surfing?

**Particípio:** regulares = igual ao passado (-ed): worked, visited, tried. Irregulares têm uma **terceira forma** (go – went – **gone**, see – saw – **seen**, eat – ate – **eaten**). Revise a lista de verbos!

**Ever** (alguma vez) aparece em perguntas: Have you **ever**...? **Never** (nunca) em afirmativas com sentido negativo: I've **never**...

**Been x gone:**
- She's **been** to Paris. (foi e já voltou — experiência)
- She's **gone** to Paris. (foi e ainda está lá)

**Present perfect → simple past:** a pergunta de experiência usa present perfect, mas quando você dá **detalhes** (quando, onde, como foi), muda para o **simple past**:
"Have you ever been to Peru?" "Yes, I **have**. I **went** in 2022. It **was** amazing."

| Afirmativa | Negativa | Pergunta | Resposta curta |
|---|---|---|---|
| I've been to Japan. | I haven't been to Japan. | Have you ever been to Japan? | Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. |
| She's met a celebrity. | She's never met a celebrity. | Has she ever met a celebrity? | Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't. |
| They've tried acarajé. | They haven't tried acarajé. | Have they ever tried it? | Yes, they have. / No, never. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L2/example-01.m4a) Have you ever ridden a camel? — Yes, once, in Morocco. It was hilarious! — _Você já andou de camelo? — Sim, uma vez, no Marrocos. Foi hilário!_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L2/example-02.m4a) I've never lived abroad, but I've traveled a lot. — _Nunca morei fora, mas já viajei muito._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L2/example-03.m4a) My parents have been to Portugal three times. — _Meus pais já foram a Portugal três vezes._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Nunca** use present perfect com tempo passado definido (yesterday, in 2019, last year, when I was a child). Nesses casos, é simple past: "I **went** to Rio **in 2019**" (e não "I've been to Rio in 2019").

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Did you ever go to London?  
  ✅ **Have you ever been to London?**  
  Para experiências de vida, sem tempo definido, use present perfect.
- ❌ I've been to Bahia last year.  
  ✅ **I went to Bahia last year.**  
  Com tempo definido (last year), use simple past.
- ❌ I've never ate sushi.  
  ✅ **I've never eaten sushi.**  
  Present perfect usa o particípio (eaten), não o passado (ate).

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Contrações: I've, she's, haven't

Na fala, **have** quase sempre vira **'ve** ("áiv", "uív", "dêiv") e **has** vira **'s**. O **'ve** é bem curto — às vezes só um "v" leve: "I've been" = "áivbin". **Been** é fraco: "bin" (EUA), não "bíin".

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L2/pron-01.m4a) I've been to Chile.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L2/pron-02.m4a) She's never seen snow.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L2/pron-03.m4a) Have you ever tried it? — No, I haven't.

## 📖 Reading — Experiences vs. things [🔊](../../../audio/B1/B1-M1-L2/reading.m4a)

Have you ever wondered why a trip makes you happier than a new phone? Psychologists who study happiness have found that spending money on experiences — travel, concerts, courses, dinners with friends — usually brings more lasting happiness than spending on things. One reason is adaptation: we get used to new objects quickly, but memories of experiences often get better with time. Another reason is connection: experiences are usually shared with other people and become part of our identity and our stories. "I've climbed a volcano" says more about who you are than "I've bought a new TV." Of course, things matter too — but next time you have some extra money, consider buying a memory instead of an object.

**Questions:**
1. What brings more lasting happiness, according to psychologists?
2. What is "adaptation"?
3. What happens to memories of experiences?
4. Why does "I've climbed a volcano" say more about you?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Bucket list

A expressão **bucket list** vem de **"kick the bucket"** (gíria para morrer) e ficou famosa com o filme "Antes de Partir" (The Bucket List, 2007). Hoje é usada para qualquer lista de sonhos: "Seeing the pyramids is on my bucket list."

## ✅ Resumo
- have/has + particípio = experiência de vida.
- Have you ever...? / I've never...
- been (foi e voltou) x gone (foi e está lá).
- Detalhes (quando, como foi) → simple past.
