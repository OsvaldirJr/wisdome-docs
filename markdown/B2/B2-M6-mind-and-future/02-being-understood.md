## 🧩 Grammar: Advanced gerunds and infinitives

Você já sabe quais verbos pedem **-ing** (enjoy, avoid, deny, mind...) e quais pedem **to** (want, decide, seem, expect...). Agora vamos às formas **passivas** e **perfeitas**:

| | Gerúndio | Infinitivo |
|---|---|---|
| simples | doing | to do |
| **passivo** | **being done** | **to be done** |
| **perfeito** | **having done** | **to have done** |
| **perfeito passivo** | having been done | to have been done |

**1. Passivo** → o sujeito **recebe** a ação:
- "I hate **being interrupted**." (eu sou interrompido)
- "Nobody likes **being criticized** in public."
- "Everyone wants **to be taken** seriously."
- "The report needs **to be finished** today."

**2. Perfeito** → ação **anterior** ao verbo principal:
- "He denied **having taken** the money." (negou ter pegado)
- "She seems **to have forgotten** our meeting." (parece ter esquecido)
- "I regret **not having studied** more." (= I regret not studying more — também correto)
- "He's thought **to have left** the country." (formal)

**3. Verbos que mudam de sentido:**
- **remember / forget + -ing** = lembrar/esquecer de algo que **aconteceu** ("I remember **meeting** her.")
- **remember / forget + to** = lembrar/esquecer de **fazer** algo ("Remember **to call** her.")
- **stop + -ing** = parar uma atividade · **stop + to** = parar **para** fazer outra coisa
- **try + -ing** = experimentar ("Try **turning** it off and on.") · **try + to** = tentar com esforço ("I tried **to open** it, but I couldn't.")
- **regret + -ing** = arrepender-se · **regret + to** = lamentar informar (formal: "We regret **to inform** you...")

| Forma | Exemplo | Tradução |
|---|---|---|
| being + pp | I hate being ignored. | Odeio ser ignorado. |
| to be + pp | She expects to be paid. | Ela espera ser paga. |
| having + pp | He admitted having lied. | Ele admitiu ter mentido. |
| to have + pp | They seem to have left. | Parece que eles foram embora. |
| remember + -ing | I remember locking the door. | Lembro de ter trancado a porta. |
| remember + to | Remember to lock the door. | Lembre-se de trancar a porta. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/example-01.m4a) Most employees appreciate being given clear feedback. — _A maioria dos funcionários gosta de receber feedback claro._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/example-02.m4a) The suspect denies having been at the scene of the crime. — _O suspeito nega ter estado na cena do crime._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/example-03.m4a) I'm lucky to have had such good teachers. — _Tenho sorte de ter tido professores tão bons._

> 💡 **Dica:** Em português usamos "ter + particípio" ("negou **ter dito**") — em inglês, o paralelo é **having said** (depois de verbos com -ing) ou **to have said** (depois de verbos com to).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I don't like to being told what to do.  
  ✅ **I don't like being told what to do.**  
  like + being + particípio (não "to being").
- ❌ He denied to have taken the money.  
  ✅ **He denied having taken the money. / He denied taking the money.**  
  deny + -ing.
- ❌ I stopped to smoke last year. (= parei de fumar)  
  ✅ **I stopped smoking last year.**  
  stop + -ing = parar de; stop + to = parar para.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: being / been

Não confunda: **being** = "BÍ-in" (duas sílabas) e **been** = "bín" (uma sílaba; no americano às vezes "bin"). "I hate **being** interrupted" x "I've **been** interrupted".

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/pron-01.m4a) I hate BEING interrupted.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/pron-02.m4a) I've BEEN interrupted three times.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/pron-03.m4a) having BEEN told...

## 🎧 Listening — The art of listening

_Uma especialista em comunicação explica a escuta ativa._

**Listen first, without reading.** [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/listening-full.m4a)

**Questions:**
1. Why do we often listen, according to research?
2. Name two things active listening involves.
3. How do people feel when they are listened to this way?
4. What simple exercise does she suggest?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Speaker:** Most of us think we're good listeners. But research on communication suggests that we often listen in order to reply, not to understand. While the other person is talking, we're planning what to say next. Active listening is different. It means giving the person your full attention, avoiding being distracted by your phone, and trying not to interrupt. It also means checking that you've understood: "So what you're saying is..." People who are listened to in this way feel respected. They're more likely to trust you, and less likely to feel misunderstood. A simple exercise: in your next conversation, try waiting two seconds before you reply. You'll be surprised how much more you notice — including what's said between the lines.

## 📖 Reading — Why we want to be understood [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L2/reading.m4a)

Being understood is one of our deepest psychological needs. Researchers studying relationships have found that people who feel understood by their partners tend to report higher satisfaction, and in some studies even smaller increases in stress during conflict. Interestingly, it seems that what matters most is not having our ideas accepted, but being listened to. In one line of research, participants who were allowed to explain their point of view — even when the other person disagreed — reported feeling more respected than those who were simply agreed with quickly. Feeling misunderstood, on the other hand, can be painful. Many people describe being left out or not being taken seriously at work as one of the most frustrating experiences of their professional lives. Psychologists suggest a few habits for helping others feel understood: asking open questions, repeating key points in your own words and avoiding being too quick to give advice. Admitting to having made assumptions also helps: "I think I misunderstood you — can you explain again?" Sometimes, the most powerful thing we can do is not to have the right answer, but to have truly listened.

**Questions:**
1. What have researchers found about people who feel understood by their partners?
2. What matters most, according to the text?
3. What is one of the most frustrating experiences at work for many people?
4. Name two habits that help others feel understood.

## 🎤 Speaking

"Active listening practice": in pairs, one person talks for 2 minutes about something that matters to them. The other listens actively (no interrupting, open questions, "So what you're saying is..."). Then swap and discuss how it felt to be listened to.

1. What do you hate being asked at family gatherings?
2. When was the last time you felt really listened to?
3. Do you prefer to be given direct or gentle feedback?
4. What do you remember being told as a child that still influences you?
5. Are you a good listener? What could you improve?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short article (200–240 words) for a company newsletter titled "How to make your colleagues feel heard". Use at least 6 passive or perfect gerunds/infinitives and 2 verbs that change meaning (remember, stop, try).

**Model:**

> How to make your colleagues feel heard  
>   
> In our last staff survey, many of you said that you wanted to be listened to more in meetings. Nobody likes being interrupted or having their ideas ignored. Here are four simple habits that can help.  
>   
> 1. Stop multitasking. When a colleague is speaking, try putting your phone face down. Being given someone's full attention is rare these days — and people notice it.  
>   
> 2. Let people finish. Many of us interrupt without realizing it. If you remember interrupting someone, go back to them: "Sorry, I cut you off — please continue."  
>   
> 3. Give credit. Nothing is more frustrating than seeing your idea presented by someone else. If you build on a colleague's idea, mention them. Everyone deserves to be recognized for their contributions.  
>   
> 4. Check your understanding. Before you reply, try repeating the main point in your own words. If you realize you've misunderstood, admit having made an assumption. It shows respect.  
>   
> These habits take practice. Several team leaders who tested them last month said they were surprised to have noticed so many changes in their meetings. One said: "People seem to have become more confident about sharing ideas."  
>   
> Next time you're in a meeting, remember to listen as carefully as you'd like to be listened to.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Comunicação direta x indireta

Culturas variam no nível de **franqueza**. Holandeses e alemães são conhecidos por feedback direto; britânicos, por indiretas ("**That's an interesting idea**" pode significar "não gostei"). Brasileiros costumam suavizar críticas e valorizar a relação. Em equipes internacionais, **read between the lines** — e, se não tiver certeza, pergunte.
