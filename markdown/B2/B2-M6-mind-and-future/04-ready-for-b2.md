## 🧩 Grammar: B2 review and exam strategies

**Revisão das grandes estruturas do B2:**
- **Narrativa:** past perfect continuous, would / used to, inversão ("Never have I...")
- **Futuro:** future continuous / perfect ("By 2030, I'll have finished...")
- **Passiva e reporting:** "It is believed that...", "He is said to be..."
- **Condicionais:** third, mixed, wish / if only, alternativas ao if
- **Orações:** relative clauses avançadas, participle clauses
- **Coesão:** although / despite / whereas / however, linkers formais
- **Modais no passado:** needn't have, should have, could have, might have
- **Gerúndio / infinitivo:** being told, having done, to have done
- **Léxico:** collocations e idioms

**Formato das provas:**

**Cambridge B2 First** — 4 partes:
- **Reading & Use of English** (1h15): gramática, vocabulário e leitura
- **Writing** (1h20): essay obrigatório + uma tarefa à escolha (artigo, e-mail/carta, relatório ou resenha)
- **Listening** (cerca de 40 min)
- **Speaking** (cerca de 14 min, **em dupla**): entrevista, comparação de fotos, tarefa colaborativa e discussão

**IELTS Speaking** (11–14 min, individual):
- **Part 1**: perguntas sobre você (casa, trabalho, hobbies)
- **Part 2**: fale **1–2 minutos** sobre um tema de um cartão (1 min para preparar)
- **Part 3**: discussão mais abstrata sobre o tema da Part 2
- Critérios: fluência e coerência, vocabulário, gramática e pronúncia. No IELTS, B2 corresponde aproximadamente às bandas 5.5–6.5.

**Estratégias para o speaking:**
1. **Ganhe tempo** com naturalidade: "That's an interesting question...", "Let me think..."
2. **Desenvolva** (elaborate): responda + motivo + exemplo. Nunca só "yes" ou "no".
3. **Parafraseie** quando faltar uma palavra: "It's a kind of... / It's something you use to..."
4. **Autocorrija-se** rapidamente: "I goed — sorry, I went..."  — isso mostra controle.
5. Na prova em dupla, **interaja**: "What do you think?", "I see your point, but..."
6. **Mostre variedade**: use estruturas do B2 de propósito (conditionals, passivas, relative clauses).

| Estratégia | Frases úteis |
|---|---|
| Ganhar tempo | That's a good question. / Let me think about that for a second. |
| Parafrasear | I can't remember the word, but it's a kind of... / It's what you use to... |
| Desenvolver | The main reason is... For example, ... That's why... |
| Interagir | What do you think? / Would you agree? / I see your point, but... |
| Esclarecer | Sorry, could you repeat the question? / Do you mean...? |
| Concluir | So, all in all, I'd say... / To sum up, ... |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L4/example-01.m4a) That's an interesting question. I've never really thought about it, but I'd say that technology has made us more connected and, at the same time, lonelier. — _Que pergunta interessante. Nunca pensei muito nisso, mas eu diria que a tecnologia nos tornou mais conectados e, ao mesmo tempo, mais solitários._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L4/example-02.m4a) I can't remember the exact word, but it's a kind of tool you use to measure temperature. — _Não lembro a palavra exata, mas é um tipo de ferramenta que você usa para medir temperatura._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L4/example-03.m4a) If I had known how useful English would be, I would have started studying much earlier. — _Se eu soubesse o quanto o inglês seria útil, teria começado a estudar bem mais cedo._

> 💡 **Dica:** Na prova oral, **o examinador avalia o seu inglês, não as suas ideias**. Você não precisa dizer algo brilhante — precisa dizer algo **claro, desenvolvido e com variedade**. Se não tiver opinião, invente uma!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Examiner: Do you like reading? — Student: Yes.  
  ✅ **Yes, I do, especially novels. I usually read before bed, because it helps me relax.**  
  Sempre desenvolva: resposta + motivo + exemplo.
- ❌ I don't know how to say this in English, so... (silêncio)  
  ✅ **I can't think of the word, but it's a kind of...**  
  Parafraseie em vez de parar.
- ❌ Usar só estruturas simples para não errar.  
  ✅ **Arriscar estruturas do B2 (If I had..., It is said that..., Having...)**  
  A variedade conta pontos — pequenos erros com estruturas complexas são aceitáveis.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Fluência: chunks e pausas naturais

Fluência não é falar rápido — é falar **sem pausas longas no lugar errado**. Faça pausas **entre** blocos de sentido (não no meio de um chunk), use **fillers** naturais (well, actually, I mean, you know) com moderação e ligue as palavras dentro de cada bloco.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L4/pron-01.m4a) Well, | to be honest, | I've never really thought about it.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L4/pron-02.m4a) Actually, | I'd say that | it depends on the situation.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L4/pron-03.m4a) I mean, | if I had known, | I would have done it differently.

## 📖 Reading — What does B2 really mean? [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M6-L4/reading.m4a)

According to the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), a B2 user is an "independent user" who can understand the main ideas of complex texts, interact with native speakers with a degree of fluency and spontaneity, and produce clear, detailed texts on a wide range of subjects. In practical terms, this is a significant milestone. Many universities around the world accept B2 as a minimum level of English for some courses, and many international employers consider it the point at which someone can work effectively in English. However, reaching B2 does not mean that you will understand everything. You may still struggle with fast speech, strong regional accents, jokes and cultural references. You will continue to make mistakes — but, crucially, they will rarely prevent communication, and you will often be able to notice and correct them yourself. Perhaps the most important change is psychological. At B2, English stops being only a subject that you study and becomes a tool that you use: to read the news, watch series without subtitles, follow a TED talk or argue your point in a meeting. From here, progress depends less on courses and more on exposure. The more you read, listen and speak, the closer you will get to C1 — and, more importantly, the richer your life in English will become.

**Questions:**
1. How does the CEFR describe a B2 user?
2. Why is B2 a significant milestone?
3. What might you still struggle with at B2?
4. What does progress depend on after B2?

## 📝 Writing

Write a B2 First–style essay (140–190 words): "Some people believe that with translation apps and AI, learning foreign languages is no longer necessary. Do you agree?" Write about 1) communication and relationships, 2) work and careers, and 3) your own idea. Show a wide range of B2 structures.

**Model:**

> Is learning languages still necessary?  
>   
> Translation apps have improved enormously in recent years. As a result, it could be argued that learning a foreign language is no longer worth the effort. However, I believe this view is mistaken.  
>   
> Firstly, language is not only about exchanging information but also about building relationships. Although an app can translate words, it cannot easily capture humour, emotion or cultural references. Having made friends abroad, I know that real connection happens when you speak someone's language.  
>   
> Secondly, in the job market, speaking English is still seen as an essential skill. Employers want people who can lead meetings and negotiate, which is extremely difficult to do through an app.  
>   
> Finally, learning a language changes the way we think. It is widely believed that bilingual people are more flexible and open to other cultures.  
>   
> In conclusion, while translation technology is a useful tool, it is not a substitute for learning. If I had relied on apps, I would never have had the experiences that English has given me.
