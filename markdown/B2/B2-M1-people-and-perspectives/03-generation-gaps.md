## 🧩 Grammar: Advanced comparisons

**1. Modificar comparativos** (o quanto maior/menor):
- grande diferença: **much / far / a lot / considerably / significantly** + comparativo → "Rent is **far higher** than it was in 2010."
- pequena diferença: **slightly / a bit / a little / marginally** → "Gen Z is **slightly more** likely to..."
- também com superlativos: "**by far the** biggest change" / "**easily the** best"

**2. As... as (com modificadores):**
- **just as** (exatamente tão): "Young people are **just as** hard-working **as** their parents."
- **not quite as / not nearly as / nowhere near as** (não tão / nem de longe tão): "Buying a house is **nowhere near as** easy **as** it was."
- **twice / three times as... as** (o dobro / o triplo): "Houses cost **three times as much as** they did."
- **the same as / similar to / different from**

**3. Comparativos duplos (mudança progressiva / correlação):**
- **-er and -er / more and more** (cada vez mais): "Life is getting **more and more** expensive." / "Phones are getting **smaller and smaller**."
- **The + comparativo..., the + comparativo** (quanto mais..., mais...): "**The more** you read, **the better** you write." / "**The older** I get, **the less** I care about what people think." / "**The sooner, the better.**"

**4. Linguagem para evitar estereótipos:** use **tend to**, **on average**, **many / some (but not all)**, **compared with**: "**On average**, Gen Z **tends to** change jobs more often **than** previous generations."

| Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| far / much + comparativo | It's far more competitive now. |
| slightly / a bit + comparativo | Salaries are slightly higher. |
| just as... as | We're just as worried as they were. |
| nowhere near as... as | It's nowhere near as cheap as before. |
| twice as... as | It costs twice as much. |
| more and more / -er and -er | It's getting harder and harder. |
| the more..., the more... | The more I learn, the less I know. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L3/example-01.m4a) On average, young people today are far more likely to rent than to own a home. — _Em média, os jovens hoje têm muito mais chance de alugar do que de ter casa própria._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L3/example-02.m4a) My grandparents' generation had nowhere near as many choices as we do. — _A geração dos meus avós não tinha nem de longe tantas opções quanto nós._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L3/example-03.m4a) The more time I spend with my grandmother, the more I realize how much she's been through. — _Quanto mais tempo passo com minha avó, mais percebo o quanto ela passou._

> 💡 **Dica:** Em português dizemos "**cada vez mais caro**"; em inglês, **more and more expensive** ou, para adjetivos curtos, **cheaper and cheaper**. Nunca "each time more" ❌.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ It's each time more difficult.  
  ✅ **It's getting more and more difficult.**  
  "Cada vez mais" = more and more / -er and -er.
- ❌ More you study, more you learn.  
  ✅ **The more you study, the more you learn.**  
  A estrutura exige the nas duas partes.
- ❌ It's very more expensive.  
  ✅ **It's much/far more expensive.**  
  Use much/far (não very) com comparativos.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Ritmo de "The more..., the more..."

Nas frases "the more..., the more...", há duas partes com ritmo paralelo e uma pausa no meio: "The **MORE** you **READ** ↗ | the **BET**ter you **WRITE** ↘." A voz sobe na primeira parte e desce na segunda.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L3/pron-01.m4a) The more you read, the better you write.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L3/pron-02.m4a) The older I get, the wiser I feel.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L3/pron-03.m4a) The sooner, the better.

## 🎧 Listening — Generations at work

3. What do all generations want most?
4. What approach does she recommend?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Consultant:** Today, many workplaces have four or even five generations working side by side. Managers often ask me whether younger employees are really that different. My answer: slightly, but nowhere near as much as the headlines suggest. Surveys show that, on average, younger workers tend to value flexibility and feedback a bit more, while older workers tend to value stability. But when you look at what people want most — fair pay, respect and meaningful work — the generations are remarkably similar. The bigger problem is stereotypes. The more we assume that a 60-year-old can't learn new technology, or that a 25-year-old won't stay in a job, the worse our teams work. The best approach is mentoring in both directions: older employees share experience, younger ones share new tools. The more they learn from each other, the better.

## 📖 Reading — Are young people really so different? [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L3/reading.m4a)

Every generation seems to believe that the next one is lazier, more selfish and more addicted to whatever technology is new. Similar complaints about young people can be found in texts from centuries ago. Today, the target is often Generation Z, described in headlines as anxious, glued to their phones and unwilling to work hard. But how much of this is true? The data paint a more complex picture. On the one hand, young people today do spend considerably more time online than previous generations did at the same age, and surveys in several countries suggest that reported levels of anxiety among teenagers have risen. On the other hand, many members of Gen Z drink far less alcohol and smoke much less than their parents did as teenagers, and they're often just as interested in meaningful work. Economic conditions matter too. In many countries, housing has become far more expensive relative to income, making it nowhere near as easy for young people to reach traditional milestones like buying a home. Researchers increasingly argue that differences between generations are often less about character and more about circumstances. Put simply: the more you change the world young people grow up in, the more their choices will change too.

**Questions:**
1. What do many generations believe about the next one?
2. In what ways might Gen Z be different?
3. In what ways might they be "better" than their parents at the same age?
4. What do researchers increasingly argue?

## 📝 Writing

Write a comparison essay (200–250 words): "Young people today have a better life than their grandparents did." Discuss both sides and give your opinion. Use advanced comparatives and careful generalizations.

**Model:**

> It is often said that young people today have a far better life than their grandparents did. While there is some truth in this, the picture is much more complex.  
>   
> On the one hand, in many ways life has become considerably easier. Most young people today have access to far more information, healthcare and opportunities than their grandparents could have imagined. In Brazil, for example, the average life expectancy is significantly higher than it was in the 1960s, and many more people go to university. Technology also allows us to stay in touch with friends and family who live thousands of kilometers away.  
>   
> On the other hand, some aspects of life have become harder and harder. Housing is nowhere near as affordable as it was, and stable, lifelong jobs are much less common. Many young people also report higher levels of anxiety, partly because of the constant pressure of social media.  
>   
> In my opinion, it is misleading to say that one generation has it better than another. Each generation faces different challenges. My grandparents had fewer choices, but they had more stability; we have more freedom, but less security. Rather than competing over who suffered more, I believe that the more generations listen to each other, the better we will understand the world we share.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "OK, boomer"

Em 2019, a expressão **"OK, boomer"** viralizou como resposta irônica de jovens a pessoas mais velhas que criticam sua geração. Em contrapartida, termos como **"snowflake"** (floco de neve, frágil) foram usados para criticar jovens. Ambos são considerados desrespeitosos em contextos formais — use com cuidado (ou não use).
