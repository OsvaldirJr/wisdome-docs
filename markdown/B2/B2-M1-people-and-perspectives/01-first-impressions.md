## 🧩 Grammar: Talking about habits with nuance

No B2, além de **usually** e **used to**, você tem estruturas que mostram **atitude** sobre o hábito:

**1. Will + verbo (presente)** → hábito **característico** de alguém (muitas vezes com carinho ou resignação):
"My grandma **will** always **offer** you food, even if you've just eaten."
**Would + verbo (passado)** → o mesmo, no passado (só ações, não estados):
"When I was a kid, my dad **would** wake us up singing."

**2. Will / would com ênfase** (WILL, WOULD acentuados) → hábito **irritante**:
"He **WILL** leave his shoes in the middle of the room!" / "She **WOULD** say that — she always takes his side."

**3. Always / constantly / forever + present continuous** → hábito irritante ou surpreendente:
"You're **always losing** your keys!" / "He's **constantly checking** his phone."

**4. Keep (on) + -ing** → repetição (frequentemente irritante): "The printer **keeps jamming**." / "She **kept interrupting** me."

**5. Tend to + verbo** → tendência geral (mais neutro, ótimo para generalizar com cuidado):
"Introverts **tend to** prefer small groups." / "I **tend not to** trust first impressions." (negativa: tend not to / don't tend to)

**6. Be prone to / be inclined to** (mais formal): "He's **prone to** exaggerating."

**Come across as** + adjetivo/substantivo = passar a impressão de ser: "She **comes across as** arrogant, but she's just shy."

| Estrutura | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| will + verbo | hábito característico (presente) | He'll sit for hours reading. |
| would + verbo | hábito característico (passado) | We'd play outside until dark. |
| WILL (enfático) | hábito irritante | She WILL leave the lights on! |
| always + continuous | hábito irritante/surpreendente | You're always complaining! |
| keep + -ing | repetição | It keeps raining. |
| tend to + verbo | tendência geral | People tend to judge quickly. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L1/example-01.m4a) My boss will reply to emails at 2 a.m. — it's just how he is. — _Meu chefe é capaz de responder e-mails às 2 da manhã — é o jeito dele._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L1/example-02.m4a) She tends to come across as cold, but once you get to know her, she's incredibly warm. — _Ela costuma passar uma imagem fria, mas quando você a conhece, ela é extremamente calorosa._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L1/example-03.m4a) My roommate is forever leaving dirty dishes in the sink! — _Meu colega de casa vive deixando louça suja na pia!_

> 💡 **Dica:** **Tend to** é uma das palavras mais úteis do B2: ela **suaviza generalizações**. Em vez de "Brazilians are late" (estereótipo), diga "Brazilians **tend to** be more flexible about time".

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ He would be very tall when he was young.  
  ✅ **He used to be very tall... / He was very tall...**  
  Would não funciona com estados (be, have, know) — só com ações repetidas.
- ❌ She tends to be talking a lot.  
  ✅ **She tends to talk a lot.**  
  Tend to + verbo base.
- ❌ He seems arrogant, but he comes across as shy.  
  ✅ **He comes across as arrogant, but he's actually shy.**  
  Come across as = a impressão (aparente); depois vem a realidade.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Will / would enfáticos

Para mostrar irritação, os nativos **acentuam** will/would, que normalmente são fracos: "He **WILL** leave his socks everywhere!" ("rí UÍL lív..."). Compare com o neutro: "He'll sit for hours reading" ("ríl").

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L1/pron-01.m4a) He'll sit for hours reading. (neutro)
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L1/pron-02.m4a) He WILL leave his socks everywhere! (irritado)
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L1/pron-03.m4a) She WOULD say that! (irônico)

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Psychologist:** Research suggests that we form an impression of someone's face in a fraction of a second — some studies say about a tenth of a second. We tend to judge whether someone is trustworthy, competent or friendly almost instantly. The problem is that these snap judgements are heavily influenced by bias. For example, we tend to see people who look like us as more trustworthy, and we'll often assume that attractive people are also smarter — something psychologists call the halo effect. The good news is that first impressions aren't fixed. If we keep meeting someone and they keep behaving in a way that contradicts our first judgement, we'll eventually update our opinion. The key is to stay curious and to be aware of our own biases.

## 📖 Reading — Why we misjudge people [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M1-L1/reading.m4a)

Imagine meeting two job candidates. One walks in confidently, makes eye contact and tells a funny story. The other speaks quietly and looks down. Most of us would instinctively prefer the first — even though nothing we've seen tells us who would actually do the job better. Psychologists have identified several reasons why our judgements of other people tend to be unreliable. One is the halo effect: when someone has one attractive quality, we'll tend to assume they have others too. Another is the fundamental attribution error: when other people behave badly, we tend to blame their personality ("He's rude"), but when we behave badly, we blame the situation ("I was tired"). Culture also plays a role. In some cultures, direct eye contact signals honesty; in others, it can come across as disrespectful. What seems confident in one country may seem arrogant in another. None of this means we should ignore our instincts completely. But it does suggest that we should hold our first impressions lightly, look for evidence, and give people the same benefit of the doubt that we would want them to give us.

**Questions:**
1. Which candidate would most people instinctively prefer, and why is that a problem?
2. What is the fundamental attribution error?
3. How can eye contact be interpreted differently?
4. What does the writer recommend?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Diretos x indiretos

O que é "confiante" ou "educado" muda entre culturas. Nos EUA, autopromoção e contato visual forte costumam ser vistos como confiança; no Japão, modéstia é valorizada; no Reino Unido, o **understatement** (dizer menos do que se sente) é comum: "It's not bad" pode significar "é ótimo!". No B2, perceber essas nuances é parte da fluência.
