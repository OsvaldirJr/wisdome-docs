## 🧩 Grammar: Wish, if only, it's time, would rather

**1. Wish / If only + past simple** → desejo sobre o **presente** (revisão):
"I **wish** I **had** more free time." / "**If only** I **were** taller!"

**2. Wish / If only + past perfect** → arrependimento sobre o **passado**:
- "I **wish** I **had studied** harder at school." (não estudei — me arrependo)
- "**If only** I **hadn't said** that!"
- "She **wishes** she **had taken** the job in London."

**3. Wish + would / wouldn't** → **reclamação** sobre o comportamento de **outra pessoa** ou situação que queremos que mude:
- "I **wish** you **would** stop leaving your clothes on the floor!"
- "I **wish** it **would** stop raining."
⚠️ Não use "I wish I would..." (sobre você mesmo, use could: "I wish I **could** stop procrastinating").

**4. It's (high / about) time + sujeito + past simple** → já passou da hora de algo acontecer (crítica/urgência):
- "**It's time** we **went** home." / "**It's high time** the government **did** something about it."
- Também: **It's time to** + verbo (mais neutro): "It's time to go."

**5. Would rather + sujeito + past simple** → preferência sobre o que **outra pessoa** faz:
- "**I'd rather you didn't** smoke here." (prefiro que você não fume — educado!)
- "**I'd rather** we **met** tomorrow."
- Compare com **I'd rather + verbo base** (sobre você mesmo): "I'd rather stay home."

**Resumo do "backshift" para irrealidade:** presente → past simple · passado → past perfect.

| Estrutura | Uso | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| wish + past simple | desejo (presente) | I wish I lived by the sea. |
| wish + past perfect | arrependimento (passado) | I wish I hadn't bought that car. |
| wish + would | reclamação (outra pessoa) | I wish he would call me. |
| wish + could | habilidade desejada | I wish I could sing. |
| It's time + past simple | já passou da hora | It's time you got a job. |
| would rather + sujeito + past | preferência sobre o outro | I'd rather you stayed. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L3/example-01.m4a) I wish I had started investing when I was in my twenties. — _Queria ter começado a investir quando tinha uns vinte anos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L3/example-02.m4a) I wish my neighbours would turn their music down after midnight. — _Queria que meus vizinhos abaixassem a música depois da meia-noite._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L3/example-03.m4a) It's high time we talked about our budget — I'd rather we didn't leave it until the last minute. — _Já passou da hora de conversarmos sobre nosso orçamento — prefiro que não deixemos para a última hora._

> 💡 **Dica:** **I'd rather you didn't** é uma forma muito educada de dizer "não faça isso" em inglês: "Do you mind if I open the window?" — "**I'd rather you didn't**, actually. I'm a bit cold."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I wish I studied more when I was at school.  
  ✅ **I wish I had studied more when I was at school.**  
  Arrependimento sobre o passado = past perfect.
- ❌ I wish I would stop eating sugar.  
  ✅ **I wish I could stop eating sugar.**  
  Sobre você mesmo, use could (não would).
- ❌ It's time we go home.  
  ✅ **It's time we went home. / It's time to go home.**  
  It's time + sujeito + past simple.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: I wish I'd / I'd rather

**I wish I'd** soa "ái uísh áid" — o 'd é curto, mas essencial (sem ele, a frase vira presente). **I'd rather** = "áid réder". **If only** tem ênfase forte em **ONLY** e entonação emotiva: "If **ONLY** I'd known!"

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L3/pron-01.m4a) I wish I'd listened.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L3/pron-02.m4a) If ONLY I'd known!
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L3/pron-03.m4a) I'd rather you didn't.

## 📖 Reading — Learning from regret [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L3/reading.m4a)

Regret has a bad reputation. We're often told to "live with no regrets" and to avoid dwelling on the past. But some psychologists argue that regret, when handled well, is one of our most useful emotions. In his book The Power of Regret, Daniel Pink describes a large survey in which thousands of people around the world shared their regrets. He identified four core types: foundation regrets ("If only I'd saved more money / looked after my health"), boldness regrets ("I wish I'd asked her out / started that business"), moral regrets ("I wish I hadn't cheated / been unkind") and connection regrets ("I wish I had kept in touch with my brother"). Interestingly, regrets about inaction — the chances we didn't take — tend to last longer than regrets about actions. Pink suggests a three-step method for using regret productively. First, reframe it: talk about it or write it down, which reduces its emotional power. Second, be kind to yourself — treat your past self as you would treat a friend who made a mistake. Third, extract a lesson for the future: "Next time, I'll speak up." Regret, in this view, isn't something to avoid. It's information about what we value most.

**Questions:**
1. What are the four core types of regret Pink identified?
2. Which regrets tend to last longer?
3. What is the first step of Pink's method?
4. What does regret tell us, according to this view?

## 📝 Writing

Write a letter to your younger self (200–250 words). Talk about things you wish you had done (or hadn't done), what you've learned, what you'd rather they did differently and what it's time for them to start doing.

**Model:**

> Dear 17-year-old me,  
>   
> I know you won't listen to everything I'm about to say — I certainly wouldn't have — but I'll try anyway.  
>   
> First, I wish you had taken English more seriously at school. You thought you'd never need it. If only you'd known that, fifteen years later, you would be using it in meetings every day! You'll learn it eventually, but the hard way, in evening classes after long days at work.  
>   
> Secondly, I wish you hadn't worried so much about what other people thought. You spent so much energy trying to fit in. I'd rather you spent that energy on the things you actually love: drawing, music, the friends who accept you exactly as you are.  
>   
> I also wish you had told Grandpa how much he meant to you. He knew, I think — but I wish you'd said it out loud.  
>   
> Here's the good news: you turn out fine. Better than fine, actually. You find work you enjoy, and people who love you.  
>   
> So, it's high time you stopped waiting for the "perfect moment" to start things. It doesn't exist. Sign up for the art course. Call your friends. Say "I love you" more often.  
>   
> And please — start using sunscreen.  
>   
> With love,  
> Your older (and slightly wiser) self

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Wear sunscreen"

Em 1997, a colunista Mary Schmich escreveu um texto com conselhos para formandos que começava com **"Wear sunscreen"**. O texto viralizou (atribuído erroneamente a Kurt Vonnegut!) e virou a música **"Everybody's Free (To Wear Sunscreen)"**, de Baz Luhrmann — que no Brasil inspirou a famosa versão "Filtro Solar", de Pedro Bial.
