## 🧩 Grammar: Alternatives to if

**1. Suppose / Supposing / Imagine / What if** → introduzem **cenários hipotéticos** (no lugar de if). Funcionam com qualquer tipo de condicional:
- "**Suppose** you **won** the lottery — what **would** you do?" (second)
- "**Supposing** the flight **is** cancelled, what **will** we do?" (first)
- "**What if** we **had** missed the train?" (third)

**2. Otherwise / Or else** → "se não" — mostram a **consequência negativa** se algo não acontecer:
- "Save your work, **otherwise** you'll lose it." (= If you don't save it, you'll lose it.)
- "Leave now, **or else** you'll miss the bus."
- Também no passado: "I took a taxi; **otherwise** I would have been late."

**3. But for + substantivo** (formal) = "não fosse por" (if it weren't for / if it hadn't been for):
- "**But for** the rain, the festival would have been perfect."
- "**If it hadn't been for** your help, I would have failed."
- "**If it weren't for** my family, I wouldn't be here."

**4. In case** → **precaução** (fazer algo agora por causa de uma possibilidade futura):
- "Take an umbrella **in case** it rains." (leve agora — talvez chova)
- Compare: "Take an umbrella **if** it rains." (só leve se estiver chovendo)
- **In case of** + substantivo (avisos): "**In case of** fire, use the stairs."

**5. Even if** → mesmo que (a condição não muda o resultado): "**Even if** it rains, we'll go."

**6. Revisão B1+/B2:** **unless** (a menos que), **provided / providing (that)**, **as long as**, **on condition that**.

| Expressão | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| suppose / supposing / what if | cenário hipotético | Supposing you lost your phone, what would you do? |
| otherwise / or else | senão | Hurry, otherwise we'll miss it. |
| but for + noun | não fosse por | But for the traffic, we'd have arrived on time. |
| if it weren't / hadn't been for | se não fosse por | If it hadn't been for you, I'd have given up. |
| in case | por precaução | Bring a jacket in case it gets cold. |
| even if | mesmo que | Even if he apologizes, I won't forgive him. |
| unless / as long as / provided | condições | You can go as long as you're back by ten. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L4/example-01.m4a) Suppose there was a blackout for three days — would your family be prepared? — _Suponha que houvesse um apagão de três dias — sua família estaria preparada?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L4/example-02.m4a) Always back up your files, otherwise you might lose years of work. — _Sempre faça backup dos seus arquivos, senão você pode perder anos de trabalho._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L4/example-03.m4a) If it hadn't been for the seatbelt, he would have been seriously injured. — _Se não fosse o cinto de segurança, ele teria se ferido gravemente._

> 💡 **Dica:** **In case** é um falso amigo parcial: "caso" em português muitas vezes significa "se" ("caso você precise, me ligue" = **if** you need anything, call me). Use **in case** só quando é **precaução**: "Here's my number **in case** you need anything."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ In case you finish early, call me. (= se)  
  ✅ **If you finish early, call me.**  
  In case = por precaução, não "se".
- ❌ But for your help, I would fail. (passado)  
  ✅ **But for your help, I would have failed.**  
  Resultado passado = would have + particípio.
- ❌ Hurry up, otherwise we will be late it.  
  ✅ **Hurry up, otherwise we'll be late.**  
  Mantenha a frase simples: otherwise + consequência completa.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Supposing, otherwise

## 📖 Reading — Thinking in scenarios [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L4/reading.m4a)

In the early 1970s, the oil company Shell began using a technique called scenario planning. Instead of trying to predict a single future, its planners imagined several possible futures and asked: "Suppose this happened — how would we respond?" One of the scenarios they explored was a sudden, dramatic rise in oil prices. Most companies considered this unlikely. When the oil crisis of 1973 arrived, Shell was widely seen as being better prepared than many of its competitors, and the company's approach became famous in business schools. Today, scenario planning is used by governments, cities and businesses around the world. The idea is not to guess correctly, but to become more flexible. A city might ask: "What if sea levels rose faster than expected?" or "Supposing a pandemic closed schools for months, how would children learn?" The process often reveals weaknesses that nobody had noticed. Psychologists point out that individuals can benefit from the same thinking. Imagining a worst-case scenario — and planning how you would cope — can actually reduce anxiety, because the future feels less threatening once you have a plan. As the old saying goes: hope for the best, but prepare for the worst.

**Questions:**
1. What is scenario planning?
2. What scenario helped Shell before 1973?

## 🎤 Speaking

"Scenario workshop": in groups, choose a scenario (a 3-day blackout, losing your phone abroad, your company closing tomorrow, AI replacing your job). Discuss what you would do, what precautions you'd take now (in case), what would happen otherwise and the worst-case scenario.

1. Supposing you lost your phone right now, what would be your biggest problem?
2. What do you always carry in case of an emergency?
3. If it weren't for one person, where would you be today?
4. What would you do even if everyone told you not to?
5. Do you prefer to plan for everything or cross that bridge when you come to it?

## 📝 Writing

Write a practical guide (200–250 words) titled "What if...? Be prepared!" for one situation (a trip abroad, a job interview, a natural disaster, a wedding). Use at least 7 alternatives to if.

**Model:**

> What if...? Be prepared for your first trip abroad!  
>   
> Travelling abroad for the first time is exciting, but things don't always go as planned. Here's how to be ready for the most common problems.  
>   
> Supposing you lose your passport, what would you do? Before you leave, take a photo of it and save a copy in the cloud, in case you need to prove your identity at the embassy. Also, write down your country's embassy phone number — your phone might be stolen too!  
>   
> What if your bank card stops working? Always carry two different cards and some cash, otherwise you could be stuck without money on your first night. Tell your bank you're travelling, or else your card might be blocked for "suspicious activity".  
>   
> Travel insurance is essential. Even if you're young and healthy, a hospital bill abroad can be extremely expensive. Many travellers have told me that, but for their insurance, an accident would have ruined them financially.  
>   
> Finally, plan your first day carefully. Book your first night's accommodation in advance, as long as you can cancel it for free, and know how to get there from the airport.  
>   
> Of course, you can't predict everything. But if you prepare for the worst-case scenario, you'll be able to relax and enjoy the best parts of your adventure. Bon voyage!

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Hope for the best, prepare for the worst"

Culturas variam muito na forma de lidar com incerteza. O pesquisador Geert Hofstede chamou isso de **uncertainty avoidance**: países como Alemanha e Japão tendem a planejar tudo; outros, como o Brasil, costumam ser mais flexíveis e improvisar — o famoso **"jeitinho"**. Em equipes internacionais, combinar planejamento e flexibilidade é uma grande vantagem.
