## 🔥 Warm-up
"Se eu tivesse aceitado aquele emprego em São Paulo, **hoje estaria** morando lá." Essa frase mistura uma condição **no passado** com um resultado **no presente**. É um **mixed conditional** — uma das estruturas mais naturais (e elegantes) do inglês B2.

- What would your life be like now if you had chosen a different career?
- Where would you be living today if you hadn't moved to your current city?

## 🧩 Grammar: Mixed conditionals

Os condicionais "puros" mantêm o mesmo tempo nas duas partes. Mas a vida real mistura tempos! Os **mixed conditionals** combinam partes do **second** e do **third** conditional.

**Tipo 1: condição no PASSADO → resultado no PRESENTE**
**If + past perfect, would + verbo base (now)**
- "If I **had accepted** the job in Canada, I **would be living** there **now**." (não aceitei → não moro lá hoje)
- "If she **hadn't studied** medicine, she **wouldn't be** a doctor today."
- "If we **had saved** more, we **wouldn't have** these money problems **now**."

**Tipo 2: característica PRESENTE (permanente) → resultado no PASSADO**
**If + past simple, would have + particípio**
- "If I **were** more confident, I **would have spoken** in the meeting yesterday." (não sou confiante → não falei)
- "If he **didn't have** a fear of flying, he **would have come** to the wedding."
- "If I **spoke** French, I **would have understood** the film."

**Como escolher?** Pergunte-se: **quando** é a condição? **Quando** é o resultado?
- passado → passado = **third** (If I'd known, I'd have come)
- presente/geral → presente/futuro = **second** (If I knew, I'd come)
- passado → presente = **mixed tipo 1** (If I'd known, I'd be there now)
- presente → passado = **mixed tipo 2** (If I knew him, I'd have invited him)

**Palavras-pista:** now, today, still (presente) / yesterday, last year, in 2015 (passado).

| Tipo | Condição | Resultado | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second | presente (irreal) | presente | If I had money, I'd travel. |
| Third | passado (irreal) | passado | If I'd had money, I'd have travelled. |
| Mixed 1 | passado | presente | If I'd saved money, I'd be travelling now. |
| Mixed 2 | presente (permanente) | passado | If I weren't so shy, I'd have asked her out. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L2/example-01.m4a) If I hadn't moved to Curitiba, I wouldn't know my best friends. — _Se eu não tivesse me mudado para Curitiba, não conheceria meus melhores amigos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L2/example-02.m4a) If she were more organized, she wouldn't have missed the deadline. — _Se ela fosse mais organizada, não teria perdido o prazo._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L2/example-03.m4a) If we had bought that apartment in 2015, it would be worth twice as much now. — _Se tivéssemos comprado aquele apartamento em 2015, ele valeria o dobro agora._

> 💡 **Dica:** Mixed conditionals são muito comuns na **fala natural** — muitas vezes até mais que os puros. Ouvir "If I'd known, I wouldn't be here" num filme é muito frequente!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ If I had taken the job, I would have lived in Lisbon now.  
  ✅ **If I had taken the job, I would be living in Lisbon now.**  
  Resultado no presente (now) = would + verbo base.
- ❌ If I was more patient, I wouldn't shout at him yesterday.  
  ✅ **If I were more patient, I wouldn't have shouted at him yesterday.**  
  Resultado no passado (yesterday) = would have + particípio.
- ❌ If I would have studied, I would be a doctor.  
  ✅ **If I had studied, I would be a doctor.**  
  Nunca would na oração com if.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Ênfase nos marcadores de tempo

Em mixed conditionals, os marcadores de tempo (**now**, **today**, **yesterday**, **back then**) costumam receber ênfase, porque são eles que mostram a mistura: "If I'd taken the job... I'd be living in Lisbon **NOW**."

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L2/pron-01.m4a) If I'd taken the job, I'd be living there NOW.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L2/pron-02.m4a) If I were braver, I'd have spoken up YESTERDAY.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L2/pron-03.m4a) If we hadn't met, I'd be a different person TODAY.

## 📖 Reading — The road not taken [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L2/reading.m4a)

"Two roads diverged in a wood, and I — / I took the one less traveled by, / And that has made all the difference." These lines from Robert Frost's 1916 poem "The Road Not Taken" are among the most quoted in American literature, often printed on posters encouraging people to be brave and different. Yet many literary critics argue that the poem is widely misunderstood. Earlier in the poem, Frost actually describes the two roads as being worn "really about the same". The traveller only imagines that, years later, he will tell the story as if his choice had been dramatic and meaningful. In other words, the poem may be less about bravery and more about how we construct stories about our decisions after the fact. Psychologists have found something similar: we tend to justify our choices once they are made, convincing ourselves that the path we took was the right one. If we had chosen differently, we would probably be just as good at explaining why that choice was the best. Perhaps that's not a bad thing. If we didn't have this ability, we might spend our lives tormented by the roads we didn't take.

**Questions:**
1. How are Frost's lines usually interpreted?
2. Why do critics say the poem is misunderstood?
3. What have psychologists found about our choices?
4. Why might this ability be a good thing?

## 📝 Writing

Write an opinion essay (220–260 words): "It is better to regret something you have done than something you haven't done." Use examples from your own life or others' with at least 4 mixed conditionals and 2 third conditionals.

**Model:**

> Is it better to regret what you did, or what you didn't do?  
>   
> A famous saying claims that we regret the things we didn't do more than the things we did. Research by psychologists has offered some support for this idea: in the short term, people tend to regret their mistakes, but over a lifetime, they regret their missed opportunities more.  
>   
> My own experience supports this view. When I was 22, I was offered a scholarship to study in Germany, but I turned it down because I was afraid of leaving my family. If I had accepted it, I would probably speak German fluently now, and I might be working in a completely different field. If I weren't so attached to my home, I would have taken that chance without hesitation. Even today, I sometimes wonder what might have been.  
>   
> On the other hand, my brother took a risk that went wrong. He invested in a restaurant that closed after a year. If he hadn't opened it, he would have saved a lot of money. Yet he insists he has no regrets. If he hadn't tried, he says, he would still be dreaming about it today instead of having learned so much about business. Had he not failed, he wouldn't have started the successful catering company he runs now.  
>   
> In my opinion, regrets about actions usually teach us something, while regrets about inaction stay with us as unanswered questions. Therefore, I believe it is generally better to take reasonable risks than to play it safe forever.
