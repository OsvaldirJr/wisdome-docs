## 🧩 Grammar: Third conditional

O **third conditional** fala de situações **imaginárias no passado** — algo que **não aconteceu** e sua consequência também imaginária.

**Estrutura: If + past perfect, would have + particípio**
- "If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed** the exam." (Não estudei → não passei)
- "If she **hadn't missed** the bus, they **wouldn't have met**." (Ela perdeu → eles se conheceram)

**Contrações na fala (muito importantes):**
- **I'd** = I had (na parte do if) / I would (na consequência): "If **I'd** known, **I'd** have called."
- **would have** → **would've** ("uúdav"); **wouldn't have** → **wouldn't've**

**Outros modais na consequência:**
- **could have + pp** = poderia ter (possibilidade/habilidade): "If we'd left earlier, we **could have caught** the train."
- **might have + pp** = talvez tivesse: "If you'd asked, she **might have said** yes."

**Perguntas:** "What **would** you **have done** if you **had lost** your passport?"

**A ordem pode inverter** (sem vírgula): "I would have helped **if** you'd asked me."

**Inversão formal (sem if):** "**Had I known**, I would have come." (= If I had known) — comum em textos formais e no B2 First.

⚠️ **Nunca** use "would have" na oração com if: ❌ "If I would have known..."

| Realidade | Third conditional |
|---|---|
| I didn't study, so I failed. | If I had studied, I would have passed. |
| It rained, so we cancelled the trip. | If it hadn't rained, we wouldn't have cancelled the trip. |
| I didn't know, so I didn't help. | If I'd known, I could have helped. |
| She took the job in Lisbon and met her husband. | If she hadn't taken the job, she might not have met him. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L1/example-01.m4a) If I hadn't taken that English course, I would never have got this job. — _Se eu não tivesse feito aquele curso de inglês, nunca teria conseguido este emprego._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L1/example-02.m4a) We could have avoided the problem if we'd checked the numbers. — _Poderíamos ter evitado o problema se tivéssemos conferido os números._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L1/example-03.m4a) Had the firefighters arrived five minutes later, the whole building might have burned down. — _Se os bombeiros tivessem chegado cinco minutos depois, o prédio todo poderia ter pegado fogo._

> 💡 **Dica:** Em português, na fala, muitas vezes dizemos "se eu **soubesse**, eu **tinha** te contado" para o passado. Em inglês, use sempre a estrutura completa: **If I had known, I would have told you.**

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ If I would have known, I would have come.  
  ✅ **If I had known, I would have come.**  
  Na oração com if, use past perfect (had + pp).
- ❌ If I had studied, I would passed.  
  ✅ **If I had studied, I would have passed.**  
  Consequência = would have + particípio.
- ❌ If she didn't miss the bus, they wouldn't have met.  
  ✅ **If she hadn't missed the bus, they wouldn't have met.**  
  Hipótese sobre o passado = past perfect.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Would've / I'd've

Nativos quase nunca dizem "would have" por extenso: **would've** = "**uúdav**", **could've** = "**kúdav**", **wouldn't have** = "**uúdentav**". E "If I'd known, I'd have..." = "if áid nôun, áidav...". Treine o ouvido — e use na fala.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L1/pron-01.m4a) If I'd known, I would've told you.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L1/pron-02.m4a) We could've won!
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L1/pron-03.m4a) She wouldn't have come.

## 📖 Reading — History's great "what ifs" [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M4-L1/reading.m4a)

Historians are often cautious about "counterfactual" questions — imagining what would have happened if events had been different — but these questions can help us understand how fragile history really is. Consider the discovery of penicillin. In 1928, the Scottish scientist Alexander Fleming returned from a holiday to find that mould had contaminated one of his bacterial cultures — and that the bacteria around the mould had died. If he had cleaned his laboratory before leaving, or if the mould had landed on a different plate, he might never have noticed the effect. Penicillin, and the antibiotics that followed, have since saved millions of lives. Or take the Post-it note, which we saw in an earlier unit: if Spencer Silver hadn't "failed" to make a strong glue, one of the world's most famous office products would not have been invented. Of course, history is rarely shaped by a single moment. Had Fleming not noticed the mould, someone else might eventually have discovered penicillin. Still, these stories remind us that chance, curiosity and the ability to notice the unexpected have played a much larger role in human progress than we usually admit.

**Questions:**
1. What are "counterfactual" questions?
2. What did Fleming find when he returned from holiday?
3. What might have happened if he'd cleaned his lab before leaving?
4. What do these stories remind us?

## 🎤 Speaking

## 📝 Writing

Write a reflective story (200–250 words) about a turning point in your life. Describe what happened and imagine how your life would have been different, using at least 6 third conditional sentences (including could have / might have).

**Model:**

> The email I almost didn't send  
>   
> In 2019, I was working as an accountant in a job I didn't enjoy. One evening, I saw an online advert for a course in graphic design. The deadline for applications was midnight, and it was already eleven o'clock. I was tired, and I almost closed the laptop.  
>   
> If I had gone to bed that night, my life would have been completely different. I would probably still be working in accounting, and I might never have discovered how much I love design. I wouldn't have met Lucas, my teacher on the course, who later offered me my first freelance project. And if he hadn't recommended me to his clients, I couldn't have built my own studio, which now has four employees.  
>   
> Of course, things could have gone wrong. If the course had been terrible, I might have lost money and confidence. Had my family not supported me, I would never have had the courage to quit my stable job.  
>   
> With hindsight, I sometimes wonder what would have happened if I'd made the change earlier. But I don't really regret the years in accounting: they taught me how to manage a business. Sometimes, the turning point isn't a big event. It's an email you send at 11:57 p.m.
