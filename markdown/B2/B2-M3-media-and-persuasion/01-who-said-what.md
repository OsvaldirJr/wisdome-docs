## 🧩 Grammar: Reporting verb patterns

Cada reporting verb tem um (ou mais) **padrão** obrigatório. Aprenda em blocos:

**1. verbo + to + infinitivo:** agree, refuse, offer, promise, threaten, claim, decide
- "The company **refused to comment**." / "He **threatened to resign**."

**2. verbo + objeto + to + infinitivo:** ask, tell, urge, warn, advise, persuade, encourage, remind, invite, beg, order
- "The doctor **advised me to rest**." / "They **persuaded her to stay**." / "Police **warned drivers not to travel**."

**3. verbo + -ing:** deny, admit, suggest, recommend, regret, mention
- "He **denied stealing** the documents." / "She **admitted making** a mistake." / "I **suggest leaving** early."
- Para algo anterior, use **having + particípio**: "He **denied having seen** her."

**4. verbo + (objeto) + preposição + -ing:**
- accuse sb **of**, blame sb **for**, apologize **for**, insist **on**, congratulate sb **on**, thank sb **for**, criticize sb **for**, warn sb **against**
- "They **accused him of lying**." / "She **apologized for being** late." / "He **insisted on paying**."

**5. verbo + that + frase:** say, explain, claim, admit, deny, insist, announce, complain, point out, argue
- "She **insisted that** she was innocent." / "The CEO **announced that** the company would close."

⚠️ **Suggest** e **recommend** NÃO aceitam objeto + to: ❌ "He suggested me to go" → ✅ "He **suggested going**" / "He **suggested that I (should) go**".

O **backshift** do B1+ continua valendo quando o verbo introdutório está no passado.

| Padrão | Verbos | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| + to infinitive | agree, refuse, offer, promise, threaten | She refused to answer. |
| + object + to | advise, urge, warn, persuade, remind | He urged us to vote. |
| + -ing | deny, admit, suggest, recommend | He denied cheating. |
| + (object) + prep + -ing | accuse of, blame for, insist on, apologize for | They blamed her for losing. |
| + that | explain, claim, insist, announce, point out | She claimed that it was true. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L1/example-01.m4a) The CEO admitted making mistakes but denied knowing about the fraud. — _O CEO admitiu ter cometido erros, mas negou saber da fraude._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L1/example-02.m4a) Health officials urged people to get vaccinated before the winter. — _Autoridades de saúde pediram às pessoas que se vacinassem antes do inverno._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L1/example-03.m4a) The player apologized for criticizing the referee and promised not to do it again. — _O jogador pediu desculpas por criticar o juiz e prometeu não fazer de novo._

> 💡 **Dica:** Escolher o verbo certo também é escolher um **ponto de vista**. "She **said** she was innocent" (neutro) x "She **claimed** she was innocent" (há dúvida) x "She **insisted** she was innocent" (ênfase). Jornalistas sérios escolhem com cuidado.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ He suggested me to apply.  
  ✅ **He suggested that I (should) apply. / He suggested applying.**  
  Suggest não aceita objeto + to.
- ❌ She denied to steal the money.  
  ✅ **She denied stealing the money.**  
  Deny + -ing.
- ❌ They accused him to lie.  
  ✅ **They accused him of lying.**  
  Accuse sb of + -ing.

## 📖 Reading — The power of a single verb [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L1/reading.m4a)

Journalism students are often taught a simple rule: when in doubt, use "said". The reason is that reporting verbs are never neutral. Imagine a politician who makes a statement about the economy. A newspaper could report that she "said", "claimed", "admitted", "insisted" or "boasted" that unemployment had fallen. Each verb sends a different message to the reader. "Claimed" suggests the statement might not be true; "admitted" suggests she was forced to reveal something; "boasted" implies arrogance. Researchers who study media bias have found that the choice of reporting verbs can influence how readers judge the people being quoted, even when the facts are identical. This is one reason why many style guides, such as those of major news agencies, recommend using neutral verbs like "said" in news reports and saving more expressive verbs for when they're clearly justified. As readers, we can protect ourselves by paying attention not only to what people are reported to have said, but also to the verbs journalists choose to report it. Sometimes, a single word tells you more about the writer than about the person being quoted.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "No comment"

Em inglês, **"No comment"** é a resposta clássica de quem não quer se pronunciar à imprensa. Já **"off the record"** significa que a informação não deve ser publicada com o nome da fonte, e **"on the record"**, que pode. Saber essa diferença é essencial para quem lida com mídia.
