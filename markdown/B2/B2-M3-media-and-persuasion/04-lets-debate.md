## 🧩 Grammar: Language for debating

**1. Estruturar um argumento (PEEL / PRE):** **P**oint (ponto) → **R**eason (razão) → **E**xample/**E**vidence (exemplo/evidência) → **L**ink (volta ao tema)
"**Firstly**, a four-day week **would** improve productivity. **This is because** rested workers make fewer mistakes. **For instance**, a large trial in the UK found that... **Therefore**, ..."

**2. Sequenciar:** Firstly / First of all → Secondly / Furthermore / Moreover / In addition → Finally / Last but not least

**3. Conceder (reconhecer o outro lado) + contra-argumentar:**
- "**Admittedly**, ... **However**, ..." / "**It's true that**..., **but**..."
- "**While** it's true that..., we **must** also consider..." / "**Granted**, ... **Nevertheless**, ..."
- "**Even if** that were true, ..." (second conditional — hipótese para enfraquecer o argumento)

**4. Refutar com precisão (reporting verbs + hedging!):**
- "**My opponent claims that**..., **but** the evidence suggests otherwise."
- "**That argument overlooks** / **fails to take into account**..."
- "**That's an oversimplification.**" / "**That doesn't necessarily follow.**"
- "**I'd question** the idea that..."

**5. Concluir:** "**To sum up** / **In conclusion** / **All things considered** / **On balance**, we firmly believe that..."

**Falácias comuns para evitar e identificar:** **ad hominem** (atacar a pessoa), **straw man** (distorcer o argumento do outro), **slippery slope** ("se X, então inevitavelmente Z"), **false dilemma** ("ou A ou B", ignorando outras opções).

| Função | Frases |
|---|---|
| Sequenciar | Firstly, ... Furthermore, ... Finally, ... |
| Justificar | This is because... / The main reason is... |
| Exemplificar | For instance, ... / A case in point is... |
| Conceder | Admittedly, ... / It's true that..., but... |
| Contra-argumentar | However, ... / Nevertheless, ... / Even if... |
| Refutar | My opponent claims that..., but... / That overlooks... |
| Concluir | All things considered, ... / On balance, ... |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/example-01.m4a) Admittedly, a four-day week might not work for every industry. However, the evidence from several trials is very encouraging. — _É verdade que a semana de quatro dias pode não funcionar para todo setor. No entanto, as evidências de vários testes são muito animadoras._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/example-02.m4a) My opponent claims that social media connects people, but that argument overlooks the growing evidence of loneliness among teenagers. — _Meu oponente alega que as redes sociais conectam as pessoas, mas esse argumento ignora as crescentes evidências de solidão entre adolescentes._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/example-03.m4a) Even if online classes were cheaper, they couldn't replace the social experience of school. — _Mesmo que as aulas online fossem mais baratas, não poderiam substituir a experiência social da escola._

> 💡 **Dica:** Em debates em inglês, **reconhecer** um bom argumento do outro lado ("That's a valid point") **aumenta** sua credibilidade. Quem nunca concede nada parece radical — e perde o público.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ In the other hand, ...  
  ✅ **On the other hand, ...**  
  A expressão é on the other hand.
- ❌ Even if that is true, it would not work.  
  ✅ **Even if that were true, it wouldn't work. / Even if that is true, it won't work.**  
  Mantenha a coerência do tipo de condicional.
- ❌ You are stupid if you believe that.  
  ✅ **I'd question that idea, because...**  
  Ataque pessoal (ad hominem) é uma falácia e destrói sua credibilidade.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Signposting com pausas

Em debates, as palavras de transição (**Firstly**, **However**, **Admittedly**, **In conclusion**) são **seguidas de pausa** e ditas com **ênfase**. Elas funcionam como placas na estrada: ajudam o público e os juízes a acompanhar sua estrutura.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/pron-01.m4a) FIRSTLY... (pausa) a four-day week would...
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/pron-02.m4a) ADMITTEDLY... (pausa) there are risks. HOWEVER...
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/pron-03.m4a) ALL things considered... (pausa) we firmly believe...

## 💬 Dialogue — Debate: Should homework be banned?

_Num debate escolar, duas equipes discutem a moção "This house would ban homework in primary schools"._

🎧 Ouça o diálogo completo: [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-full.m4a)

|  |  | English |
|---|---|---|
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-01.m4a) | **Chair** | The motion is: "This house would ban homework in primary schools." Proposition, you have two minutes. |
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-02.m4a) | **Bia** | Thank you. Firstly, research suggests that homework has little benefit for young children's learning. Secondly, it takes away time for play, sleep and family. Children need to be children. |
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-03.m4a) | **Chair** | Opposition, your response. |
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-04.m4a) | **Theo** | Admittedly, too much homework can be stressful. However, banning it completely is an oversimplification. Small amounts of reading at home help build habits and involve parents in their children's education. |
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-05.m4a) | **Bia** | My opponent claims that homework involves parents, but that argument overlooks the fact that many parents work long hours and can't help. Homework actually increases inequality. |
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-06.m4a) | **Theo** | That's a valid point. But even if some parents can't help, wouldn't it be better to support those families rather than remove the opportunity from everyone? |
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-07.m4a) | **Bia** | In conclusion, what young children need most isn't more worksheets — it's time to rest, play and be curious. We urge you to support the motion. |
| [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/dialogue-08.m4a) | **Theo** | All things considered, the answer isn't a ban, but better homework: shorter, more meaningful and more flexible. We oppose the motion. |

## 🎧 Listening — Debate coach's advice

_Uma técnica de debate dá conselhos a alunos antes de uma competição._

**Listen first, without reading.** [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/listening-full.m4a)

**Questions:**
1. Why is structure important?
2. What is the worst thing you can do?
3. Which argument should you refute?
4. What does conceding a point show?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Coach:** Remember three things tomorrow. First, structure. Judges can't give you points for arguments they can't follow, so signpost clearly: "My first point is...", "My second point is...". Second, engage with the other side. The worst thing you can do is read a prepared speech and ignore what your opponents have just said. Listen, take notes, and refute their strongest argument, not their weakest. Third, stay calm and respectful. Attacking your opponent personally is a fallacy, and judges will penalize you for it. And one more thing: it's perfectly fine to concede a point. Saying "Admittedly, that's true, but..." shows confidence, not weakness. The best debaters aren't the loudest — they're the clearest.

## 📖 Reading — The four-day week: does it work? [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L4/reading.m4a)

In recent years, the idea of a four-day working week — with no reduction in pay — has moved from the fringes to the mainstream. Supporters argue that it improves employees' well-being, reduces burnout and can even increase productivity. They point to a large trial in the United Kingdom in 2022, coordinated by the non-profit 4 Day Week Global with researchers from several universities, in which around 60 companies reduced working hours. According to the organizers, the large majority of participating companies chose to continue with the policy after the trial, and many employees reported lower stress and better sleep. Similar pilots have been carried out in countries such as Iceland, Spain and, more recently, Brazil. Critics, however, raise several concerns. Admittedly, the results look impressive, but the companies that volunteered for trials may not be representative: they were probably already open to flexible work. Furthermore, a four-day week may be much harder to implement in sectors like healthcare, education or retail, where someone must be present at all times. Some economists also question whether productivity gains can be sustained in the long term. On balance, the evidence so far suggests that the four-day week can work well in some contexts — but it is unlikely to be a universal solution.

**Questions:**
1. What do supporters argue?
2. What did the UK trial find, according to the organizers?
3. Why might the trial companies not be representative?
4. What is the writer's conclusion?

## 🎤 Speaking

Formal debate (30 min): two teams of 3. Choose a motion (e.g., "This house would introduce a four-day week", "This house believes AI will do more good than harm", "This house would ban social media for under-16s"). Each speaker: 2 minutes. Use PEEL, concede at least once, refute the other team and conclude. The rest of the class judges.

1. Which motion would you most like to debate? Why?
2. Have you ever changed your mind after a good argument?
3. Which fallacy do you see most often online?
4. Is it possible to win an argument and still lose a friend?
5. What makes a debater convincing?

## 📝 Writing

Write a for-and-against essay (220–260 words) on one of the debate motions. Present both sides fairly with PEEL paragraphs, concede and counter-argue, and give your conclusion.

**Model:**

> Should social media be banned for children under 16?  
>   
> In recent years, several governments have proposed restricting social media for teenagers. While the idea has strong support, it also raises important questions.  
>   
> Those in favour argue that a ban would protect young people's mental health. Firstly, studies have linked heavy social media use to anxiety, poor sleep and low self-esteem among teenagers. Secondly, platforms are designed to be addictive, and it is unreasonable to expect a 13-year-old to resist techniques created by expert engineers. For instance, endless scrolling and constant notifications make it extremely difficult to stop. Therefore, supporters believe the responsibility should not fall on children alone.  
>   
> On the other hand, critics point out that a ban may be impossible to enforce. Admittedly, age-verification technology is improving; however, many teenagers would probably find ways around it. Furthermore, social media also has benefits: young people use it to stay in touch with friends, find communities and learn new skills. A complete ban, critics argue, could isolate those who rely on online support, such as LGBTQ+ teenagers in conservative environments.  
>   
> All things considered, I believe that the risks are serious enough to justify stronger regulation, but that a total ban is an oversimplification. What we need is a combination of measures: stricter rules for platforms, better age verification, and digital education in schools. Even if a ban were possible, it would not teach young people how to use technology wisely — and that is a skill they will need for the rest of their lives.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Debate como tradição

Em países como Reino Unido, EUA e Austrália, clubes de debate escolares e universitários são uma tradição forte — as **Oxford Union** e **Cambridge Union** recebem chefes de Estado. O formato "**This house believes/would...**" vem dessas sociedades. Participar de um clube de debate em inglês é um dos melhores treinos para o C1.
