## 🧩 Grammar: Passive reporting structures

Usamos estruturas passivas com verbos como **say, believe, think, report, know, expect, consider, estimate, claim** para relatar informações quando a fonte não é importante, é genérica ("as pessoas") ou a informação **não está confirmada**.

**Estrutura 1: It + is/was + particípio + that + frase**
- "**It is said that** the castle is haunted."
- "**It was believed that** the earth was flat."
- "**It is estimated that** 3 million people live there."

**Estrutura 2: Sujeito + is/was + particípio + to + infinitivo** (mais elegante e comum em notícias)
- "The castle **is said to be** haunted."
- "He **is believed to be** the richest man in the country."

**O infinitivo muda conforme o tempo da informação:**
- **to + verbo** → mesmo tempo (presente): "She is said **to live** in Paris." (Dizem que ela mora em Paris)
- **to be + -ing** → ação em andamento agora: "He is thought **to be hiding** in the forest." (Acredita-se que esteja se escondendo)
- **to have + particípio** → ação **anterior**: "The suspect is believed **to have left** the country." (Acredita-se que tenha deixado)
- **to have been + -ing** → ação contínua anterior: "She is reported **to have been working** on a new album."

**Advérbios equivalentes (mais informais):** **allegedly, reportedly, apparently, supposedly**
- "The singer has **reportedly** signed a new contract." / "He **allegedly** stole the car."

**Em português** esses sentidos são "dizem que", "acredita-se que", "supostamente", "segundo relatos".

| Informação | It is... that... | Sujeito + is... to... |
|---|---|---|
| Presente | It is said that she lives in Rome. | She is said to live in Rome. |
| Em andamento | It is thought that he is hiding. | He is thought to be hiding. |
| Passado | It is believed that they escaped. | They are believed to have escaped. |
| Passado contínuo | It is reported that she has been working on it. | She is reported to have been working on it. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L2/example-01.m4a) The painting is believed to have been stolen during the war. — _Acredita-se que o quadro tenha sido roubado durante a guerra._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L2/example-02.m4a) It is estimated that the festival brought over R$200 million to the city. — _Estima-se que o festival trouxe mais de R$200 milhões para a cidade._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L2/example-03.m4a) The actor is reported to be in talks to play the lead role in the new series. — _Segundo relatos, o ator está em negociações para estrelar a nova série._

> 💡 **Dica:** Estas estruturas são muito comuns em **notícias policiais, científicas e de celebridades** — e em provas como o **B2 First** (Key Word Transformation). Treine transformar uma estrutura na outra: "It is said that he is rich" ↔ "He is said to be rich".

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ He is said that he is rich.  
  ✅ **It is said that he is rich. / He is said to be rich.**  
  Não misture as duas estruturas.
- ❌ The suspect is believed to leave the country yesterday.  
  ✅ **The suspect is believed to have left the country yesterday.**  
  Ação anterior = to have + particípio.
- ❌ Is said that the house is haunted.  
  ✅ **It is said that the house is haunted.**  
  A estrutura impessoal precisa do sujeito it.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: To have been = "tâv bin"

Na fala, **to have** vira "**tâv**" e **to have been** vira "**tâvbin**": "He's believed tâv left." / "She's said tâvbin working...". A ênfase fica no verbo principal: "believed to have **LEFT** the **COUN**try".

## 📖 Reading — Urban legends [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M3-L2/reading.m4a)

Every culture has its urban legends — stories that are passed from person to person and are widely believed to be true, despite little or no evidence. One well-known example is the idea that the Great Wall of China can be seen from space with the naked eye. It was widely repeated in textbooks for decades, but astronauts have said they could not see it. Another is the claim that we only use 10% of our brains, which is often attributed to early psychologists but has been rejected by neuroscientists: brain scans show activity across virtually all regions. In Brazil, many people grew up hearing that mixing mango and milk could be fatal. The belief is thought to have originated during the colonial period, possibly to stop enslaved workers on plantations from drinking the milk produced on the farms. Today, nutritionists confirm that the combination is perfectly safe. Why do such stories survive? Psychologists suggest that legends spread because they are simple, surprising and emotionally engaging — and because once a belief is shared by many people, questioning it feels strange. The lesson? "Everyone knows" is not the same as "it's true".

**Questions:**
1. What is an urban legend?
2. What have astronauts said about the Great Wall?
3. Where is the mango-and-milk belief thought to have originated?
4. Why do legends survive, according to psychologists?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Alleged" e a presunção de inocência

Em notícias policiais em inglês, jornalistas usam **alleged/allegedly** ("the alleged thief", "he allegedly stole") até que a pessoa seja condenada. Isso protege o veículo de processos e respeita a **presumption of innocence** (presunção de inocência). Por isso, uma notícia que diz "the thief" antes do julgamento pode ser considerada problemática.
