## 🧩 Grammar: Advanced relative clauses

**1. Preposição + which / whom (formal)**
Na fala, a preposição vai para o final; em textos formais, ela vem **antes** do pronome:
- Informal: "the city (that) I grew up **in**"
- Formal: "the city **in which** I grew up"
- Informal: "the people I work **with**" → Formal: "the people **with whom** I work"
⚠️ Depois de preposição, use **which** (coisas) ou **whom** (pessoas) — **nunca** that ou who: ❌ "in that", ❌ "with who".

**2. Which para comentar a frase inteira** (= o que)
- "Rents have doubled, **which** means many families have had to move."
- "He didn't apologize, **which** I found rude."
Sempre com **vírgula** e sempre **which** (nunca that, nunca what): ❌ "..., what is a problem".

**3. Quantificador + of which / of whom**
**some / many / most / all / none / both / neither / half / one / a few + of which (coisas) / of whom (pessoas)**
- "The city has 30 parks, **most of which** are in rich areas."
- "I have two brothers, **both of whom** live abroad."
- "We received 200 complaints, **none of which** were answered."

**4. Whose** (posse — pessoas **e** coisas)
- "a neighbourhood **whose** residents are fighting gentrification"
- Formal: "the building, the roof **of which** collapsed" (= whose roof)

**5. Where / when / why** em vez de preposição + which:
- "the area **where** I live" (= in which) · "the year **when** the metro opened" · "the reason **why** I left"

**Lembrete:** relative clause **não restritiva** (informação extra) vem entre vírgulas e **não aceita that**.

| Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| preposição + which | the house in which she was born |
| preposição + whom | the colleague to whom I sent the report |
| , which (frase inteira) | The bus was late, which made me miss the meeting. |
| quantificador + of which | 200 flats, half of which are empty |
| quantificador + of whom | 50 students, most of whom are from Bahia |
| whose | a city whose population has doubled |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L1/example-01.m4a) Curitiba, whose bus rapid transit system became a model for other cities, is often praised for its urban planning. — _Curitiba, cujo sistema de BRT virou modelo para outras cidades, é frequentemente elogiada pelo seu planejamento urbano._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L1/example-02.m4a) Thousands of people commute every day, many of whom spend more than three hours in traffic. — _Milhares de pessoas se deslocam todo dia, muitas das quais passam mais de três horas no trânsito._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L1/example-03.m4a) The city closed the avenue to cars on Sundays, which made it one of the most popular leisure areas in town. — _A cidade fechou a avenida para carros aos domingos, o que a tornou uma das áreas de lazer mais populares._

> 💡 **Dica:** Em português dizemos "**o que** é um problema". Em inglês, para retomar a frase toda, use **which**, não what: "Prices went up, **which** is a problem."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Traffic is terrible, what makes people stressed.  
  ✅ **Traffic is terrible, which makes people stressed.**  
  Para comentar a frase inteira, use which.
- ❌ the people with who I work  
  ✅ **the people with whom I work / the people I work with**  
  Depois de preposição, use whom.
- ❌ I have two sisters, both of them live in Recife.  
  ✅ **I have two sisters, both of whom live in Recife.**  
  Para ligar as orações, use of whom (ou crie duas frases).

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Pausas nas relative clauses não restritivas

Nas orações com vírgula (informação extra), fazemos uma **pequena pausa** e o tom cai um pouco, como um parêntese: "My brother, | who lives in Recife, | is a doctor." Sem pausa, a frase significa que você tem **mais de um** irmão.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L1/pron-01.m4a) My brother, who lives in Recife, is a doctor.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L1/pron-02.m4a) The city has 30 parks, most of which are in rich areas.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L1/pron-03.m4a) The bus was late, which made me miss the meeting.

## 📖 Reading — The 15-minute city [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L1/reading.m4a)

Imagine a neighbourhood in which you could reach almost everything you need — work, school, shops, a doctor, a park — within a 15-minute walk or bike ride. This is the idea behind the "15-minute city", a concept popularized by the urbanist Carlos Moreno, whose work influenced the plans of Paris mayor Anne Hidalgo. Supporters argue that cities designed around cars have created long commutes, pollution and lonely suburbs, all of which damage our health and quality of life. By bringing services closer to people, the 15-minute city aims to cut emissions, revive local businesses and give residents more free time. Several cities, among which are Melbourne, Barcelona and Bogotá, have experimented with similar ideas. The concept is not without its critics, however. Some point out that in many cities, including those in Brazil, jobs and good services are concentrated in a few central areas, which means that a 15-minute city could easily become a privilege for the rich. Others fear that improving a neighbourhood will push up rents, forcing out the very people for whom the changes were intended. The challenge, then, is not only to design better neighbourhoods, but to ensure that everyone can afford to live in them.

**Questions:**
1. What is a 15-minute city?
2. What problems do supporters say car-based cities have created?
3. Why could it become a privilege for the rich in Brazil?
4. What risk do others fear?

## 📝 Writing

Write a proposal to your city council (220–260 words) suggesting one improvement for your neighbourhood. Describe the current situation, your proposal and its benefits. Use at least 6 advanced relative clauses (preposition + which/whom, which for a whole clause, quantifier + of which/whom, whose).

**Model:**

> Proposal: Turning the old market square into a community park  
>   
> Introduction  
> The purpose of this proposal is to suggest a new use for the old market square in Jardim Esperança, a neighbourhood in which more than 40,000 people live but where there is not a single public park.  
>   
> The current situation  
> The square, whose market closed in 2018, is now used as an informal car park. It is poorly lit, which makes many residents, especially women and older people, feel unsafe at night. Local children, most of whom live in small flats, have nowhere safe to play.  
>   
> The proposal  
> We propose transforming the square into a small park with trees, a playground, benches and an outdoor gym. A local architect, with whom our residents' association has already spoken, has offered to design the project at no cost. The area could also host a weekly farmers' market, the profits from which would help pay for maintenance.  
>   
> Benefits  
> The park would provide a green space for thousands of families, many of whom cannot afford to travel to the large parks in the city centre. It would also improve safety and support local producers. Studies in several cities have found that access to green spaces is associated with better physical and mental health.  
>   
> Conclusion  
> We strongly believe that this project, the costs of which are relatively low, would make a real difference to our community. We would be happy to present our ideas at the next council meeting.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Curitiba e o BRT

Nos anos 1970, Curitiba criou um sistema de ônibus com faixas exclusivas e estações-tubo que ficou conhecido no mundo como **BRT (Bus Rapid Transit)**. O modelo inspirou cidades como Bogotá (TransMilenio) e dezenas de outras. Em inglês, Curitiba aparece com frequência em livros e artigos sobre **urban planning**.

- , which = "o que" (comenta a frase inteira).
- most of which / all of whom / none of which.
- whose = cujo(a) — pessoas e coisas.
