## 🔥 Warm-up
Compare: "Because she **had grown up** in a small town, she found São Paulo overwhelming." e "**Having grown up** in a small town, she found São Paulo overwhelming." A segunda é mais curta e elegante. As **participle clauses** são típicas de textos jornalísticos, acadêmicos e de redações B2.

- How has society changed since your grandparents were young?

## 🧩 Grammar: Participle clauses

Participle clauses **encurtam** orações quando o **sujeito é o mesmo**. Há três formas:

**1. Present participle (-ing)** → sentido **ativo** / ações simultâneas
- Relative clause reduzida: "People **who live** in cities..." → "People **living** in cities..."
- Tempo: "**While I was walking** home, I saw..." → "**Walking** home, I saw..."
- Causa: "**Because I didn't know** the answer, I stayed quiet." → "**Not knowing** the answer, I stayed quiet."
- Resultado: "The storm hit the coast, **causing** serious damage."

**2. Past participle (-ed / 3ª forma)** → sentido **passivo**
- "The houses **which were built** in the 1950s..." → "The houses **built** in the 1950s..."
- "**Built** in 1960, Brasília was designed by Lúcio Costa and Oscar Niemeyer."
- "**Asked** about the problem, the mayor refused to comment."

**3. Perfect participle (having + particípio)** → ação **anterior** à principal
- "**After she had finished** university, she moved to Lisbon." → "**Having finished** university, she moved to Lisbon."
- Passiva: "**Having been** rejected twice, he finally got the job."

**Negativa:** **not** antes do particípio: "**Not having** a car, I take the bus."

⚠️ **Dangling participle (erro clássico):** o sujeito da participle clause **tem que ser** o mesmo da oração principal.
❌ "**Walking** down the street, the rain started." (a chuva estava andando?)
✅ "**Walking** down the street, **I** got caught in the rain."

| Forma | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| -ing | ativo / simultâneo / causa | Living abroad, I learned to cook. |
| not + -ing | causa negativa | Not knowing the way, we got lost. |
| particípio (-ed) | passivo | Founded in 1554, São Paulo is now a megacity. |
| having + pp | ação anterior | Having saved enough, they bought a house. |
| having been + pp | anterior + passivo | Having been warned, we left early. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L2/example-01.m4a) Having grown up without the internet, my parents find social media confusing. — _Tendo crescido sem internet, meus pais acham as redes sociais confusas._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L2/example-02.m4a) Life expectancy in Brazil has risen considerably in recent decades, creating new challenges for the pension system. — _A expectativa de vida no Brasil aumentou consideravelmente nas últimas décadas, criando novos desafios para a previdência._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L2/example-03.m4a) Most of the jobs created in the last decade require digital skills. — _A maioria dos empregos criados na última década exige habilidades digitais._

> 💡 **Dica:** "**Having said that**" é uma expressão fixa muito usada para introduzir um contraste depois de concordar com algo: "Remote work has many benefits. **Having said that**, it can be very lonely."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Walking to work, the rain started.  
  ✅ **Walking to work, I got caught in the rain.**  
  O sujeito das duas orações precisa ser o mesmo (dangling participle).
- ❌ Having finish the report, she went home.  
  ✅ **Having finished the report, she went home.**  
  Having + particípio (finished).
- ❌ The people living in 1900 had no electricity. (= viviam naquela época)  
  ✅ **People who lived in 1900 had no electricity.**  
  Para ações passadas encerradas, a relative clause completa costuma soar melhor.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Ritmo de frases longas

Participle clauses no começo da frase formam um **bloco de entonação** próprio, com uma pequena pausa antes da oração principal: "Having grown up in a small town, | she found São Paulo overwhelming." Isso ajuda o ouvinte a acompanhar.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L2/pron-01.m4a) Having grown up in a small town, | she found the city overwhelming.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L2/pron-02.m4a) Not knowing what to do, | I called my mother.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L2/pron-03.m4a) Built in the 1960s, | Brasília was a bold experiment.

## 📖 Reading — The remote work revolution [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L2/reading.m4a)

Before 2020, remote work was a privilege enjoyed by a small minority of workers. Then, almost overnight, the COVID-19 pandemic forced millions of office employees to work from their kitchens and living rooms. Having been told for years that remote work was impossible, many companies discovered that it worked surprisingly well. Freed from long commutes, employees gained hours of free time each week. Some left expensive capital cities altogether, moving to smaller towns or the coast and taking their salaries with them. Several countries, including Portugal and Costa Rica, began offering special visas designed to attract "digital nomads". Not everyone has benefited equally, however. Jobs requiring physical presence — in hospitals, shops, factories and construction — cannot be done from home, which means that remote work has, in some ways, increased the gap between different groups of workers. Managers, too, have expressed concerns, arguing that teams working only online lose creativity and that young employees miss out on learning from experienced colleagues. Having experimented with every possible model, many organizations have now settled on a hybrid approach. Having said that, the debate is far from over.

**Questions:**
1. What did many companies discover during the pandemic?
2. What did some employees do, freed from commuting?
3. Why has remote work increased the gap between workers?
4. What model have many organizations settled on?

## 📝 Writing

Write an article for a magazine (220–260 words) titled "How my generation is different". Compare your generation with your parents' and use at least 6 participle clauses, including "Having said that".

**Model:**

> How my generation is different  
>   
> Born in the early 1990s, my parents' generation grew up in a very different Brazil. Having lived through years of high inflation, they learned to save every penny and to value job security above everything else. My father, having worked for the same company for 32 years, still finds it strange that I've had four jobs in eight years.  
>   
> My generation, raised with the internet, sees the world differently. Connected to people all over the planet, we tend to be more open to new ideas, cultures and ways of living. Many of my friends, not wanting to spend three hours a day in traffic, have chosen jobs that allow them to work remotely. Others, inspired by influencers and entrepreneurs, have started their own businesses.  
>   
> We also talk more openly about topics that our parents rarely mentioned, such as mental health, gender equality and the environment. Asked about therapy, my mother once told me that "in my time, we just got on with life". My friends, on the other hand, see therapy as something completely normal.  
>   
> Having said that, our generation has its own problems. Faced with high rents and unstable jobs, many of us still live with our parents well into our thirties. And, having grown up with smartphones, we often find it difficult to switch off.  
>   
> In the end, every generation is shaped by the world in which it grows up. Perhaps the most important thing is to keep listening to each other.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z...

Em inglês, as gerações têm nomes populares: **Baby Boomers** (aprox. 1946–64), **Gen X** (65–80), **Millennials** ou **Gen Y** (81–96), **Gen Z** (97–2012) e **Gen Alpha** (depois de 2012). ## ✅ Resumo

- particípio = passivo (built in 1960; asked about...).
- having + pp = ação anterior (having finished...).
- Mesmo sujeito nas duas orações! Having said that = dito isso.
