## 🧩 Grammar: Contrast and concession linkers

**1. Although / Even though / Though + oração** (sujeito + verbo)
- "**Although** the city is rich, many people live in poverty."
- **Even though** = mais enfático ("mesmo que, ainda que"): "**Even though** he earns a lot, he never saves."
- **Though** = mais informal; também no fim da frase: "It's expensive. I love it, **though**."

**2. Despite / In spite of + substantivo ou -ing** (sem sujeito + verbo!)
- "**Despite** the rain, we went out." / "**In spite of** being tired, she kept working."
- Para usar uma oração: **despite the fact that** + oração: "**Despite the fact that** it was raining, we went out."
- ⚠️ ❌ "despite **of**" — não existe. ❌ "Despite it was raining".

**3. Whereas / While** → **contraste direto** entre duas coisas (comparação):
- "Rich families have fast internet at home, **whereas** many poor families rely on mobile data."
- "**While** some see AI as a threat, others see it as an opportunity."

**4. However / Nevertheless / On the other hand** → ligam **duas frases** (vêm depois de ponto ou ponto e vírgula, seguidos de vírgula):
- "The internet has created opportunities. **However**, not everyone can access them."
- **Nevertheless / Nonetheless** (formal) = "mesmo assim": "The project was expensive. **Nevertheless**, it was a success."
- **On the other hand** → apresenta o outro lado de um argumento: "On the one hand, ... **On the other hand**, ..."
- **Yet** (formal, = but): "simple **yet** effective"

⚠️ **However não liga duas orações com vírgula** como "but": ❌ "It's cheap, however it's bad." ✅ "It's cheap. However, it's bad." / "It's cheap; however, it's bad."

| Conector | Seguido de | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| although / even though | oração | Although it was late, we kept talking. |
| despite / in spite of | substantivo / -ing | Despite the cost, they bought it. |
| despite the fact that | oração | Despite the fact that it was late, we kept talking. |
| whereas / while | oração (contraste) | She's tall, whereas her brother is short. |
| however / nevertheless | nova frase + vírgula | It's cheap. However, it's slow. |
| on the other hand | nova frase (outro lado) | It's fast. On the other hand, it's expensive. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L3/example-01.m4a) Although internet access has expanded in Brazil, millions of people still have only limited mobile data. — _Embora o acesso à internet tenha se expandido no Brasil, milhões de pessoas ainda têm apenas dados móveis limitados._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L3/example-02.m4a) Despite having a smartphone, my grandmother rarely uses apps. — _Apesar de ter um smartphone, minha avó raramente usa aplicativos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L3/example-03.m4a) Automation can increase productivity. Nevertheless, it may leave many workers behind. — _A automação pode aumentar a produtividade. Mesmo assim, pode deixar muitos trabalhadores para trás._

> 💡 **Dica:** Truque para não errar: **although** = embora (+ frase completa) · **despite** = apesar de (+ substantivo/-ing). Se em português você diria "apesar **de que**", use **although** ou **despite the fact that**.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Despite of the traffic, we arrived on time.  
  ✅ **Despite the traffic, we arrived on time. / In spite of the traffic...**  
  Despite não leva of. In spite of leva.
- ❌ Although the rain, we played football.  
  ✅ **Despite the rain, we played football. / Although it was raining...**  
  Although + oração; despite + substantivo.
- ❌ It's a good idea, however it's expensive.  
  ✅ **It's a good idea. However, it's expensive. / It's a good idea, but it's expensive.**  
  However começa nova frase (ou vem depois de ponto e vírgula).

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Contraste com entonação

Em frases de contraste, as palavras que se opõem recebem **ênfase**: "**SOME** people love it, whereas **OTHERS** hate it." / "It's **CHEAP**. However, it's **SLOW**." Depois de **However,** faça uma pausa curta.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L3/pron-01.m4a) SOME people love it, whereas OTHERS hate it.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L3/pron-02.m4a) It's CHEAP. However, | it's SLOW.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L3/pron-03.m4a) On the ONE hand... | on the OTHER hand...

## 📖 Reading — Will AI make us more equal? [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M5-L3/reading.m4a)

Artificial intelligence has been described as the most important technology of our time. Yet economists disagree sharply about whether it will reduce or increase inequality. On the one hand, optimists argue that AI tools could empower people who have traditionally been left behind. A small farmer can now get advice about crops in her own language, while a student without access to private tutors can ask a chatbot to explain a difficult maths problem. Although these tools are not perfect, they are often free or cheap. On the other hand, pessimists point out that previous waves of technology tended to benefit those who already had capital and education. Despite creating new jobs, automation has often replaced routine tasks performed by lower-paid workers, whereas highly skilled professionals have seen their productivity — and salaries — increase. There is also the question of who owns the technology: a handful of large companies, most of which are based in the United States and China. In spite of these concerns, most experts agree on one point: the outcome is not inevitable. Whether AI narrows or widens the gap will depend largely on political choices, including investment in education, regulation and public access to digital tools.

**Questions:**
1. What do economists disagree about?
2. Give one example of how AI could empower people.
3. What have previous waves of technology tended to do?
4. What will the outcome depend on?

## 📝 Writing

Write a balanced "for and against" paragraph pair (200–240 words) on the question: "Has technology reduced inequality?" Use at least 7 different contrast linkers and finish with your own opinion.

**Model:**

> Has technology reduced inequality?  
>   
> On the one hand, technology has opened doors for millions of people. Although many Brazilians still cannot afford a computer, most now have a smartphone, which gives them access to free courses, banking apps and job opportunities. Instant payment systems, for instance, have made it much easier for small businesses and informal workers to receive money, whereas in the past many of them had no bank account at all. Despite its limitations, a phone with an internet connection can be a powerful tool for learning and earning.  
>   
> On the other hand, technology has also created new forms of inequality. While wealthy families have fast broadband and several devices, many poor families share one phone with a limited data plan. During the pandemic, this difference meant that some children kept learning, whereas others were left behind. Furthermore, automation is replacing routine jobs, yet the new jobs being created usually require skills that low-income workers have had little opportunity to develop.  
>   
> In my opinion, technology itself is neither good nor bad; it is a double-edged sword. It has certainly reduced some inequalities. Nevertheless, without public investment in internet access and digital education, it may end up widening the gap rather than narrowing it.
