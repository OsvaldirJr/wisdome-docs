## 🧩 Grammar: Conditionals and language for negotiating

**1. Condições** (alternativas mais precisas ao if):
- **provided (that) / providing (that)** = desde que (formal) → "We can deliver by Friday, **provided that** you confirm the order today."
- **as long as / so long as** = contanto que → "You can work from home **as long as** you attend the Monday meeting."
- **on condition that** = sob a condição de que (muito formal)
- **unless** = a menos que → "We can't lower the price **unless** you order more units."
⚠️ Depois dessas expressões, use **presente simples** para o futuro (como com if).

**2. Propostas hipotéticas — second conditional** (soa mais flexível e menos ameaçador que o first):
- "**If we ordered** 500 units, **would** you **give** us a 10% discount?"
- "**What if we offered** a two-year contract?"
- "**Suppose** we paid upfront — **could** you deliver earlier?"

**3. Linguagem diplomática** (would, could, might, a bit, slightly, I'm afraid):
- "**We'd** like to propose..." / "**We'd be willing to**..."
- "That's **slightly higher than** our budget." (em vez de "too expensive")
- "**I'm afraid** we **wouldn't be able to** accept that."
- Perguntas negativas como sugestão: "**Wouldn't it make sense to**...?"

**4. Concessão em troca (if... then):**
"**If you could** extend the deadline, **we could** reduce the price by 5%." — nunca dê algo sem pedir algo em troca!

| Função | Frases |
|---|---|
| Propor | We'd like to propose... / What if we...? |
| Condição | provided that / as long as / unless |
| Hipótese flexível | If we ordered more, would you...? |
| Recusar com educação | I'm afraid that's slightly beyond our budget. |
| Concessão + troca | We could..., if you could... |
| Meio-termo | Could we meet halfway? / How about splitting the difference? |
| Fechar | I think we've got a deal. / Let's put that in writing. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L3/example-01.m4a) We'd be happy to extend the contract, provided that the price stays the same for two years. — _Ficaríamos felizes em estender o contrato, desde que o preço se mantenha por dois anos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L3/example-02.m4a) If we paid the full amount upfront, would you be able to offer a better rate? — _Se pagássemos o valor total adiantado, vocês conseguiriam oferecer um preço melhor?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L3/example-03.m4a) I'm afraid we can't go below R$8,000 unless you sign for at least a year. — _Infelizmente não conseguimos baixar de R$8.000, a menos que vocês assinem por pelo menos um ano._

> 💡 **Dica:** Na cultura de negócios anglo-saxã, um "**not**" seco soa agressivo. Prefira: "**I'm afraid that might be difficult**" ou "**That would be a challenge for us**" — o outro lado vai entender que é um não.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Provided that you will pay today, we'll deliver tomorrow.  
  ✅ **Provided that you pay today, we'll deliver tomorrow.**  
  Depois de provided that / as long as, presente simples.
- ❌ This is too expensive.  
  ✅ **This is slightly higher than our budget.**  
  Em negociação, suavize: slightly / a bit + comparativo.
- ❌ We give you 10% if you buy more.  
  ✅ **We could give you 10% if you ordered more.**  
  Modais e second conditional tornam a proposta mais flexível e educada.

## 📖 Reading — Getting to Yes [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L3/reading.m4a)

In 1981, Roger Fisher and William Ury of the Harvard Negotiation Project published Getting to Yes, a book that changed how many people think about negotiation. Instead of seeing negotiation as a battle where one side wins and the other loses, they proposed a method called "principled negotiation". It has four main ideas. First, separate the people from the problem: you can be tough on the issue while being respectful to the person. Second, focus on interests, not positions. A position is what someone says they want ("I want a 20% raise"); an interest is why they want it ("I need to pay for my children's school"). When you understand interests, creative solutions often appear — perhaps a smaller raise plus an education allowance. Third, invent options for mutual gain before deciding. Finally, insist on objective criteria, such as market prices or expert standards, rather than pure willpower. The authors also introduced the concept of BATNA — your Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement. If you know what you'll do if the deal fails, you'll be much less likely to accept a bad one. Decades later, these ideas remain central to negotiation training around the world.

**Questions:**
1. How did Getting to Yes change the view of negotiation?
2. What's the difference between a position and an interest?
3. What are "objective criteria"?
4. What is a BATNA and why does it matter?

## 📝 Writing

Write an email (180–220 words) responding to a job offer. Thank the company, show enthusiasm and negotiate one or two points (salary, start date, remote work) diplomatically, using conditional language.

**Model:**

> Subject: Re: Job offer – Senior Data Analyst  
>   
> Dear Ms. Ferreira,  
>   
> Thank you very much for offering me the position of Senior Data Analyst. I'm genuinely excited about the opportunity to join your team, especially the international expansion project we discussed.  
>   
> Before accepting, I'd like to discuss two points. First, regarding the salary, the proposed figure is slightly lower than I had expected, based on my research for similar roles in the market and my eight years of experience. I was hoping for something closer to R$15,000. If that isn't possible, would you be willing to consider a review after six months, provided that I meet the agreed performance targets?  
>   
> Second, I'm currently required to give 30 days' notice to my employer. Would it be possible to set my start date for March 3rd instead of February 15th? This would allow me to complete my current projects properly.  
>   
> I'm confident that we can find an arrangement that works for both of us, and I look forward to your reply.  
>   
> Kind regards,  
> Igor Santos

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Negociar em culturas diferentes

Estilos variam muito: americanos tendem a ir direto aos números; japoneses e chineses valorizam construir relacionamento antes de negociar; alemães esperam dados detalhados; brasileiros costumam valorizar confiança pessoal e flexibilidade. Antes de uma negociação internacional, pesquise a cultura do outro lado — o livro **The Culture Map** (Erin Meyer) é uma boa referência.
