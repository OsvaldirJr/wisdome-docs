## 🔥 Warm-up
Reuniões em inglês são onde muitos profissionais brasileiros ficam em silêncio — não por falta de ideias, mas por não saber **como entrar na conversa**. Hoje você vai aprender as frases exatas para participar e até liderar reuniões.

- Do you speak up in meetings? Why or why not?
- What makes a meeting productive (or a waste of time)?

## 🧩 Grammar: Meeting language by function

A linguagem de reuniões é altamente **funcional**: cada momento tem suas frases. Repare em como elas usam estruturas que você já domina (modais, perguntas indiretas, present perfect).

**ABRIR (chair):** "Shall we get started?" / "The **purpose** of today's meeting is to..." / "We have three **items** on the agenda." / "We've got 30 minutes, so let's keep it brief."

**DAR A PALAVRA:** "Ana, **would you like to** start?" / "**What are your thoughts on** this, Leo?" / "**We haven't heard from** Pedro yet."

**INTERROMPER com educação:**
- "**Sorry to interrupt, but**..." / "**Can I just come in here?**" / "**If I could just add something**..."
- Em reuniões online: "**Sorry, go ahead.**" (quando duas pessoas falam juntas)

**MANTER A PALAVRA:** "**If I could just finish** my point..." / "**Just one more thing**..." / "**Bear with me**..."

**DISCORDAR (com hedging do B2-M1!):** "**I see your point, but**..." / "**I'm not sure that would work because**..." / "**Have we considered**...?"

**CONTROLAR O RUMO E O TEMPO:**
- "**I think we're getting off track.** Let's get back to..."
- "**That's a good point, but maybe we could take it offline.**" / "**Let's park that for now.**"
- "**We're running out of time**, so let's move on."

**RESUMIR E DEFINIR AÇÕES (present perfect + will/going to):**
- "**So, to sum up**, we**'ve agreed** to... / we**'ve decided** that..."
- "**Action points:** Ana **will** send the report by Friday; Leo **is going to** contact the client."
- "**Who's going to** follow up on this?" / "**Let's touch base** next week."

| Momento | Frases-chave |
|---|---|
| Abrir | Shall we get started? / The purpose of this meeting is... |
| Dar a palavra | What are your thoughts on this? / We haven't heard from... |
| Interromper | Sorry to interrupt, but... / Can I just come in here? |
| Manter a palavra | If I could just finish... / Just one more thing... |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/example-01.m4a) Sorry to interrupt, but before we move on, could we clarify the budget for the second phase? — _Desculpe interromper, mas antes de seguirmos, poderíamos esclarecer o orçamento da segunda fase?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/example-02.m4a) That's an important issue, but I think it deserves its own meeting. Shall we take it offline? — _É um assunto importante, mas acho que merece uma reunião própria. Vamos discutir à parte?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/example-03.m4a) So, to sum up, we've agreed to launch in May. Paula will update the timeline, and I'll inform the client. — _Então, resumindo, concordamos em lançar em maio. A Paula vai atualizar o cronograma, e eu vou informar o cliente._

> 💡 **Dica:** Em reuniões online com estrangeiros, usar o botão **"raise hand"** e dizer "**Could I add something?**" é uma ótima estratégia para quem tem dificuldade de interromper. E sempre termine com **action points** claros: quem faz o quê até quando.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Wait, I want to speak!  
  ✅ **Sorry to interrupt, but could I just add something?**  
  Interrupções devem ser suavizadas com Sorry / Could I...
- ❌ Let's start the meeting. The objective is discuss the budget.  
  ✅ **Let's get started. The objective is to discuss the budget.**  
  The objective/purpose is + to + verbo.
- ❌ We agreed that Ana sends the report.  
  ✅ **We've agreed that Ana will send the report.**  
  Action points usam will / going to para o futuro.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Entrar na conversa com confiança

Para interromper, **aumente levemente o volume** na primeira palavra ("**SORRY** to interrupt...") e depois fale em ritmo normal. Uma interrupção hesitante e baixa é ignorada; uma alta demais soa agressiva. Pratique o equilíbrio!

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/pron-01.m4a) SORRY to interrupt, but...
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/pron-02.m4a) Can I just COME IN here?
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/pron-03.m4a) So, to SUM UP...

## 🎧 Listening — Why meetings fail

_Um consultor de produtividade explica os erros mais comuns em reuniões._

**Listen first, without reading.** [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/listening-full.m4a)

**Questions:**
1. What is the first reason meetings fail?
2. What problem is there with the people invited?
3. What happens when nobody controls the discussion?
4. What three answers should every meeting end with?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Consultant:** Surveys suggest that many professionals feel a large share of their meetings are unproductive. Why? In my experience, there are four main reasons. First, there's no clear purpose — people come without knowing what decision needs to be made. Second, the wrong people are invited: too many people, or not the ones who can actually decide. Third, nobody controls the discussion, so the conversation goes off track and the loudest voices dominate. And fourth — the most common — the meeting ends without clear action points. Everyone leaves thinking someone else will do the work. My rule is simple: no agenda, no meeting. And no meeting should end without three answers: what have we decided, who will do what, and by when.

</details>

## 📖 Reading — The silent participants [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L4/reading.m4a)

In multinational companies, managers often notice a pattern: some team members — frequently non-native speakers — rarely speak up in meetings, even when they have excellent ideas. Research on international teams points to several reasons. One is language anxiety: the fear of making mistakes in front of colleagues, especially native speakers who talk quickly and use idioms. Another is cultural. In some cultures, interrupting is considered rude, and people wait for a pause that, in a fast-paced meeting, never comes. In others, junior employees are expected not to contradict senior ones publicly. The result is that companies lose valuable perspectives. Some organizations have started to change their meeting culture. Chairs are trained to invite quieter participants directly ("Marta, what's your view?"), agendas are shared in advance so people can prepare, and some meetings use a "round" format where everyone speaks in turn. Native speakers are encouraged to slow down and avoid slang. Non-native speakers, in turn, can benefit from learning a small set of phrases for entering a discussion. Ultimately, the goal is simple: the best idea should win — not the fastest speaker.

**Questions:**
1. Who often stays silent in multinational meetings?
2. What are two reasons for this?
3. What changes have some organizations made?
4. What is the ultimate goal, according to the text?

## 🎤 Speaking

Meeting simulation (20 min): groups of 4–5. One person chairs; the others receive role cards with different opinions on a real issue (e.g., the office moving to a new city, a four-day week, a new budget). Follow the agenda, interrupt politely, refocus, and end with clear action points.

1. What's the worst meeting you've ever been in? Why?
2. How do you feel about interrupting people in English?
3. Should every meeting have an agenda?
4. Are online meetings more or less productive than in-person ones?
5. What's one rule you would introduce for meetings at your workplace or school?

## 📝 Writing

Write the follow-up email (150–200 words) after a meeting: thank participants, summarize the main decisions, list the action points (who, what, when) and mention any issues parked for later.

**Model:**

> Subject: Summary and action points – App launch meeting (May 6)  
>   
> Hi everyone,  
>   
> Thank you all for your input in today's meeting. Here's a quick summary of what we discussed and agreed.  
>   
> Decisions  
> • We've agreed on a launch date of June 15th.  
> • The first version will include the payment and booking features only; the chat feature will be added in the second phase.  
> • Weekly progress meetings will take place every Thursday at 10 a.m.  
>   
> Action points  
> • Kate will resend the final designs to the new shared folder by the end of today.  
> • Leo's team will start development on Monday, May 12th.  
> • Marta is going to prepare a testing plan by May 20th.  
> • I'll update the client and share the revised timeline by Wednesday.  
>   
> Parked for later  
> • The marketing team's proposal to change the logo. I'll set up a separate meeting with them next week.  
>   
> If I've missed anything or if anything is unclear, please let me know by tomorrow.  
>   
> Best regards,  
> Paula
