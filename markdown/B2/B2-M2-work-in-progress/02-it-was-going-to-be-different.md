## 🧩 Grammar: Future in the past

Quando contamos uma história no passado, às vezes precisamos falar do que era **futuro naquele momento**. O inglês usa as formas de futuro "no passado":

**1. was/were going to + verbo** → plano ou intenção passada (geralmente **não** aconteceu):
- "I **was going to** call you, but I forgot." (eu ia te ligar)
- "We **were going to** move to Portugal, but then the pandemic happened."

**2. would + verbo** → previsão ou expectativa vista do passado (o "will" do passado):
- "I **knew** you **would** understand." (eu sabia que você entenderia)
- "She **promised** she **would** help." / "Nobody **thought** the company **would** survive."
- Também para narrar o destino: "He moved to London in 1990. He **would** never return to Brazil." (nunca mais voltaria)

**3. was/were about to + verbo** → algo **iminente** no passado (estava prestes a):
- "I **was about to** give up when I got the job offer."
- Mais formal: **was on the point of** + -ing: "She **was on the point of** resigning."

**4. was/were supposed to + verbo** → o que estava **combinado/esperado** (e geralmente não aconteceu):
- "The package **was supposed to** arrive yesterday."

**5. was/were + -ing** (present continuous no passado) → compromisso marcado no passado: "I couldn't meet him because I **was flying** to Recife the next day."

**6. was/were to + verbo** (formal/narrativo) → algo destinado a acontecer: "It was 2005. She **was to become** one of Brazil's greatest athletes."

| Estrutura | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| was going to | plano passado (não cumprido) | I was going to quit, but I stayed. |
| would | previsão vista do passado | I knew it would rain. |
| was about to | iminente no passado | I was about to call you! |
| was supposed to | combinado/esperado | The train was supposed to leave at 8. |
| was + -ing | compromisso marcado (passado) | I was meeting Ana that evening. |
| was to + verbo | destino (narrativo) | He was to become president. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L2/example-01.m4a) I was going to accept the job in São Paulo, but at the last minute I had second thoughts. — _Eu ia aceitar o emprego em São Paulo, mas no último minuto fiquei em dúvida._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L2/example-02.m4a) When I started my business, I never imagined it would grow so fast. — _Quando comecei meu negócio, nunca imaginei que cresceria tão rápido._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L2/example-03.m4a) We were just about to sign the contract when the investor backed out. — _Estávamos prestes a assinar o contrato quando o investidor desistiu._

> 💡 **Dica:** "Eu **ia** fazer" em português tem duas traduções: **was going to** (plano) ou **would** (expectativa/rotina passada — lembre do B2-M1!). "Eu ia à praia todo domingo" = **I would go / used to go** (hábito) — não "was going to"!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I knew you will like it.  
  ✅ **I knew you would like it.**  
  Futuro visto do passado: will → would.
- ❌ I was going to the beach every Sunday as a child. (hábito)  
  ✅ **I used to go / I would go to the beach every Sunday.**  
  Hábito passado = used to / would, não was going to.
- ❌ I was about to leaving.  
  ✅ **I was about to leave.**  
  Be about to + verbo base.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Was gonna

**Was going to** vira "**uâz gâna**" na fala informal: "I was gonna call you!". **Were going to** = "uâr gâna". **Was about to** = "uâzabáuta". **Was supposed to** = "uâz sapôusta".

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L2/pron-01.m4a) I was gonna call you, but I forgot.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L2/pron-02.m4a) I was about to leave.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L2/pron-03.m4a) We were supposed to meet at eight.

## 📖 Reading — The plans that failed — and the success that followed [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L2/reading.m4a)

Many famous success stories begin with a plan that went wrong. In the early 2000s, a small company called Odeo was going to become a major podcasting platform. Then Apple added podcasts to iTunes, and the founders realized their product would never compete. Instead of giving up, they held a brainstorming session, and one employee suggested a simple service for sharing short status updates. That idea became Twitter. Similarly, the founders of Slack were originally building an online game called Glitch. The game failed, but the internal chat tool the team had built to communicate turned out to be far more valuable than the game itself. Nobody at the time thought it would become one of the most widely used workplace tools in the world. Psychologists say that people who handle these moments well tend to share two qualities: they're flexible enough to change direction, and they see failure as information rather than as a final verdict. In other words, the plan you were going to follow might not be the plan you were supposed to follow. Sometimes, the most important skill is knowing when to let go.

**Questions:**
1. What was Odeo going to become?
2. Why did its founders change direction?
3. What were Slack's founders originally building?
4. What two qualities do people who handle failure well tend to share?
