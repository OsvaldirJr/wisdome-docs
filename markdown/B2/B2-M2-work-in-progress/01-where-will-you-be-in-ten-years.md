## 🧩 Grammar: Future continuous & future perfect

**Future continuous: will be + -ing** → ação **em andamento** num momento do futuro:
- "This time tomorrow, I**'ll be flying** to Lisbon." (estarei voando)
- "Don't call at 8 — we**'ll be having** dinner."
- **Perguntas educadas** sobre planos (sem pressionar): "**Will you be using** the car tonight?" (soa menos invasivo que "Will you use...?")
- Coisas que vão acontecer **naturalmente / como rotina**: "I**'ll be seeing** Ana at work anyway, so I'll give her the book."

**Future perfect simple: will have + particípio** → ação **concluída até** um ponto do futuro:
- "**By** 2030, I**'ll have finished** my master's." (terei terminado)
- "By the time you arrive, we**'ll have eaten**."
- Negativa: "I **won't have finished** by Friday."
- Com **by**: by then, by the time, by next year, by the end of the month

**Future perfect continuous: will have been + -ing** → **duração** até um ponto do futuro:
- "In June, I**'ll have been working** here **for** five years." (farei cinco anos)
- "By the time she retires, she**'ll have been teaching for** 35 years."

**Lembre:** em time clauses (when, by the time, as soon as), use o **presente**: "By the time you **arrive** (❌ will arrive), we'll have left."

| Tempo | Forma | Exemplo | Ideia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Future continuous | will be + -ing | At 9 a.m. I'll be presenting. | em andamento no futuro |
| Future perfect | will have + pp | By 2028 I'll have saved R$50k. | concluído até um ponto |
| Future perfect continuous | will have been + -ing | By May I'll have been studying for 3 years. | duração até um ponto |
| Pergunta educada | Will you be + -ing? | Will you be joining us? | sem pressionar |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L1/example-01.m4a) This time next year, I'll be working remotely from a small town by the sea. — _A esta altura do ano que vem, estarei trabalhando remotamente de uma cidadezinha à beira-mar._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L1/example-02.m4a) By the time I'm 40, I hope I'll have set up my own business. — _Quando eu tiver 40 anos, espero ter aberto meu próprio negócio._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L1/example-03.m4a) Next month, we'll have been living in this apartment for ten years. — _Mês que vem, faremos dez anos morando neste apartamento._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Will you be + -ing?** é uma forma muito educada de perguntar sobre planos sem parecer um pedido: "**Will you be coming** to the meeting?" (só quer saber) x "**Will you come** to the meeting?" (soa como convite/pedido).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ By 2030, I will finish my degree.  
  ✅ **By 2030, I will have finished my degree.**  
  Concluído até um ponto futuro (by) = future perfect.
- ❌ By the time you will arrive, I'll have left.  
  ✅ **By the time you arrive, I'll have left.**  
  Em time clauses, presente simples.
- ❌ Tomorrow at 10, I will work.  
  ✅ **Tomorrow at 10, I'll be working.**  
  Ação em andamento num momento futuro = future continuous.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Formas fracas: will have / will have been

Na fala, **will have** vira "**'ll've**" ("áiláv"): "I'll have finished" = "áilav FÍ-nisht". **Will have been** vira "**áilavbin**". A força fica no verbo principal e no tempo: "By **JUNE**, I'll've been **WORK**ing here for **FIVE** years."

- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L1/pron-01.m4a) I'll have finished by Friday. ("áilav")
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L1/pron-02.m4a) I'll be working at nine.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L1/pron-03.m4a) By June, I'll have been working here for five years.

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Futurist:** Let me paint a picture of 2035. By then, many of the jobs we know today will have changed dramatically — and some will have disappeared altogether. Millions of people will be working in roles that don't exist yet. Most of us won't be doing the same job for forty years; instead, we'll be retraining several times throughout our careers. By the time today's teenagers retire, they'll probably have had five or six different careers, not just different jobs. AI tools will be handling many routine tasks, which means humans will be focusing more on creativity, empathy and complex problem-solving. The question isn't whether your job will change. It's whether you'll have learned how to keep learning.

## 📖 Reading — The 100-year life [🔊](../../../audio/B2/B2-M2-L1/reading.m4a)

In their book The 100-Year Life, Lynda Gratton and Andrew Scott argue that as life expectancy increases, the traditional three-stage life — education, work, retirement — will no longer make sense for many people. If you're likely to live to 90 or 100, you probably won't be able to stop working at 60 and live on your savings for forty years. Instead, the authors predict a multi-stage life. People will be studying, working, taking breaks and retraining in different combinations throughout their lives. Someone might work in finance until 40, take a year off to travel, retrain as a teacher and then set up a small business at 60. By the time they finally retire, they will have had several careers, not one. This has big implications. Governments will need to rethink pensions and education. Companies will have to stop seeing older workers as a cost and start seeing them as an asset. And individuals will need to invest in what the authors call "intangible assets": skills, health, relationships and the ability to adapt. The good news? A longer life means more time to try new things. The challenge is that most of our institutions were designed for a much shorter one.

**Questions:**
1. What is the traditional three-stage life?
2. Why won't it make sense for many people?
3. What is a multi-stage life?
4. What are "intangible assets"?

## 📝 Writing

Write a personal development plan (200–250 words) for the next five years. Describe your goals, milestones and what you'll have achieved by specific dates. Use all three future forms from the lesson.

**Model:**

> My five-year plan  
>   
> Five years from now, I hope I'll be working as a product manager at an international tech company, ideally in a hybrid role that allows me to spend part of the year in Brazil.  
>   
> To get there, I've set myself several milestones. By the end of this year, I'll have completed this English course and taken the B2 First exam. Over the next twelve months, I'll be taking an online certificate in product management, which I'll be studying for every evening after work.  
>   
> By the middle of 2027, I'll have been working in my current company for four years, and I'll have led at least two product launches. That experience should make my CV much stronger. I also plan to start applying for international positions at that point.  
>   
> Of course, plans change. If I don't get an international job by 2028, I won't see it as a failure. I'll have gained valuable skills anyway, and I'll be in a better position than I am today.  
>   
> Outside work, I want to look after my health. By the time I turn 35, I'd like to have run my first half marathon. In five years' time, I'll have been running regularly for almost a decade — which is a goal in itself!
