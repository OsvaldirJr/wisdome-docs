## 🧩 Grammar: Complex noun phrases and reporting verbs

**1. Sintagma nominal complexo** = núcleo + **pré-modificadores** + **pós-modificadores**:

[the] [**recent**] [**large-scale**] [**rural-to-urban**] **migration** [**of young people**] [**in north-eastern Brazil**] [**over the past two decades**]

- **Pré-modificação:** adjetivos, substantivos como adjetivos (*labour market*, *health policy*), adjetivos compostos (*low-income*, *evidence-based*), particípios (*the growing demand*)
- **Pós-modificação:** preposições (*of, in, for, on*), orações relativas reduzidas (*the data collected in 2020*, *students attending public schools*), infinitivos (*the decision to expand*), orações relativas (*a policy which...*)

**Dica:** substantivos como adjetivos ficam no **singular**: *a three-year study*, *a labour market policy* (não "labours market").

**2. Verbos de citação (reporting verbs) — posição do autor original:**
- **Neutros:** state, note, report, describe, observe, suggest, propose
- **Argumentativos (o autor defende):** argue, contend, maintain, claim, assert, posit, insist
- **Concessão:** acknowledge, concede, admit, recognize
- **Discordância:** dispute, challenge, question, reject, refute (= provar que é falso!), dismiss
- **Evidência:** demonstrate, show, find, establish, confirm

**3. Sua posição (stance) em relação à fonte:**
- **Aceitando:** "As Silva **rightly** points out..." / "Silva **convincingly** demonstrates..."
- **Distanciando:** "Silva **claims** that..." (dúvida) / "**According to** Silva..."
- **Rejeitando:** "Silva's argument **fails to account for**..." / "This view **overlooks**..."

**4. Tempo verbal nas citações:**
- **Presente** para ideias/argumentos ainda relevantes: "Smith (2020) **argues** that..."
- **Passado** para estudos específicos e resultados: "Smith (2020) **found** that 40% of..."
- **Present perfect** para panorama: "Several studies **have shown**..."

| Função | Verbos |
|---|---|
| Neutro | state, note, observe, report, suggest |
| Defender | argue, contend, maintain, assert, posit |
| Admitir | acknowledge, concede, recognize |
| Discordar | dispute, challenge, question, refute, reject |
| Evidência | demonstrate, show, establish, confirm |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M3-L2/example-01.m4a) Drawing on interviews with 120 low-income families in São Paulo, Oliveira (2021) contends that access to public transport is a key determinant of educational outcomes. — _Com base em entrevistas com 120 famílias de baixa renda em São Paulo, Oliveira (2021) sustenta que o acesso ao transporte público é um fator-chave para os resultados educacionais._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M3-L2/example-02.m4a) While several studies have demonstrated a link between screen time and poor sleep, the direction of causality remains disputed. — _Embora vários estudos tenham demonstrado uma ligação entre tempo de tela e sono ruim, a direção da causalidade continua contestada._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M3-L2/example-03.m4a) The rapid expansion of private higher education institutions in Brazil since the late 1990s has received considerable scholarly attention. — _A rápida expansão das instituições privadas de ensino superior no Brasil desde o fim dos anos 1990 recebeu considerável atenção acadêmica._

> 💡 **Dica:** Cuidado: **refute** significa **provar** que algo é falso — não apenas discordar. Se você só discorda, use **dispute, challenge** ou **reject**. E evite começar parágrafos com "According to" o tempo todo — varie com verbos de citação.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ A three-years study of labours markets...  
  ✅ **A three-year study of labour markets...**  
  Substantivos usados como adjetivo ficam no singular.
- ❌ Smith refuted the theory. (só discordou)  
  ✅ **Smith challenged / disputed the theory.**  
  refute = provar que é falso.
- ❌ Many studies said that sleep is important.  
  ✅ **Many studies have shown / suggested that sleep is important.**  
  Use verbos de citação precisos e present perfect para panoramas.

## 🎧 Listening — Why is academic English so dense?

_Um linguista explica a densidade da escrita acadêmica._

**Listen first, without reading.** [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M3-L2/listening-full.m4a)

**Questions:**
1. What dominates conversation, according to corpus research?
2. What dominates academic prose?
3. What is the advantage of turning processes into nouns?
4. What do many style guides now encourage?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Linguist:** If you've ever struggled with an academic article, you're not alone. Corpus research led by linguists such as Douglas Biber has shown that academic writing differs from conversation in a striking way. Conversation is dominated by verbs and clauses: "We went out, and then it started raining, so we came back." Academic prose, by contrast, is dominated by nouns and noun phrases: "The sudden onset of heavy rainfall necessitated an early return." Information is packed into long, complex noun phrases, which allows writers to express a great deal very concisely — but also places heavy demands on the reader. This density isn't simply pretentiousness. It allows academics to treat processes as objects that can be discussed, compared and measured. Once "people moved to the cities" becomes "urbanization", you can ask about the rate of urbanization, the causes of urbanization, the effects of urbanization. That said, many style guides now encourage academics to balance density with clarity — and to remember that a paper nobody can read is a paper nobody will cite.

</details>

## 📖 Reading — The replication crisis [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M3-L2/reading.m4a)

Over the past fifteen years, a growing body of research has raised uncomfortable questions about the reliability of published scientific findings. The concern, often referred to as the "replication crisis", emerged most visibly in psychology. In a large-scale collaborative project published in Science in 2015, the Open Science Collaboration attempted to reproduce one hundred studies previously published in leading psychology journals. While nearly all the original studies had reported statistically significant results, only around a third to a half of the replications, depending on the criteria used, produced comparable findings. The causes of this problem are widely debated. A number of researchers contend that the incentive structures of academic publishing bear a considerable share of the responsibility. Journals have traditionally favoured novel, positive results over replications or null findings, thereby encouraging what critics describe as "questionable research practices": analysing data in multiple ways until a significant result emerges, or reporting only the outcomes that support the original hypothesis. Others emphasize methodological factors, including small sample sizes and insufficient statistical power. Not all scholars accept the more pessimistic interpretations, however. Some have argued that the replication studies themselves suffered from methodological limitations, and that differences between original and replication findings may reflect variations in populations or procedures rather than flawed original research. These objections notwithstanding, the episode has prompted significant reforms. Pre-registration of hypotheses and analysis plans, the sharing of data and materials, and the publication of registered reports — in which journals commit to publishing a study before its results are known — have become increasingly common. Whether these measures will substantially improve the reliability of the scientific record remains, at present, an open empirical question.

**Questions:**
1. What did the 2015 Open Science Collaboration attempt to do?
2. What proportion of replications produced comparable findings?
3. What incentive problem do some researchers identify?
4. Name two reforms prompted by the crisis.

>   
> (Note: the sources cited below are fictional and created for this exercise.)  
>   
> The sudden shift to remote learning during the COVID-19 pandemic has generated a substantial body of research on its consequences for educational inequality. Much of this literature points in a similar direction, although important disagreements remain regarding both the scale and the duration of the effects.  
>

>

>

>   
> Taken together, these studies suggest that the effects of remote learning cannot be attributed to any single cause. Further longitudinal research is needed to establish whether the observed learning losses have been recovered, and which interventions have proved most effective in narrowing the resulting gaps.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Publish or perish" e o inglês acadêmico

Grande parte da produção científica mundial é publicada em **inglês**, o que coloca pesquisadores de outros países — inclusive brasileiros — em desvantagem. Muitas universidades oferecem cursos de **English for Academic Purposes (EAP)**, e revistas científicas brasileiras de destaque publicam cada vez mais artigos em inglês. Dominar a voz acadêmica é, hoje, uma **habilidade profissional** para pesquisadores.

## ✅ Resumo
- Sintagma nominal complexo: pré-modificação + núcleo + pós-modificação.
- Substantivo como adjetivo no singular: a three-year study.
- Reporting verbs: argue/contend (defender), acknowledge (admitir), challenge/dispute (discordar), refute (provar falso).
- Stance: As X rightly points out / X claims / This view overlooks...
