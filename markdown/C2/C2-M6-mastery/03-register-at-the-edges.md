## 🧩 Grammar: Registers at the extremes

**1. Arcaico / literário** — aparece em Shakespeare, na Bíblia King James, em hinos, rituais, juramentos e em **uso irônico**:
- **thou** (tu, sujeito), **thee** (te, objeto), **thy/thine** (teu): "**Thou art** my friend." / "I love **thee**."
- Verbos com -st/-th: **thou hast, thou dost, he hath, she doth**
- **wherefore** (por quê — não "onde"!), **whence** (de onde), **hither/thither** (para cá/lá), **ere** (antes), **betwixt** (entre), **'tis** (it is), **pray** (por favor)
- Uso moderno **irônico**: "**Pray, do tell!**" / "**Whence** this sudden interest in cooking?"

**2. Técnico / jurídico** (revisão C2-M2-L3 e M5-L3): hereinafter, notwithstanding, shall, the party of the first part...

**3. Gíria** — muda rápido e varia por região e idade:
- **Britânica:** knackered (exausto), gutted (arrasado), chuffed (contente), skint (sem grana), cheeky (atrevido), mate, innit
- **Americana:** awesome, bummed (chateado), dude, my bad (foi mal), hang out
- **Gíria rimada cockney:** apples and pears = stairs; dog and bone = phone; loaf (of bread) = head
- **Internet / Gen Z** (envelhece rápido!): no cap (sério), lowkey/highkey, slay, it's giving..., rizz (charme), touch grass (vá viver a vida real), main character energy

**4. Choque de registro (register clash)** — misturar registros **sem querer** soa estranho; **de propósito**, é humor:
- Sem querer: "Dear Sir, your product is lowkey trash." ❌
- De propósito (humor): "Lo, the pizza hath arrived!" / "The committee has reached a decision, and the decision is: nah."

**5. Conselhos para o C2:**
- **Entenda** gírias para compreender filmes, séries e redes sociais.
- **Use** gírias com cuidado: só com quem você conhece, no contexto certo. Gíria de outra geração ou região pode soar **forçada** ("cringe").
- O arcaico, hoje, é usado quase sempre com **ironia** ou em **citações**.

| Registro | Exemplo | Quando |
|---|---|---|
| Arcaico | Thou art... / Wherefore...? / 'Tis... | literatura, citações, ironia |
| Formal elevado | I am most grateful for your kind invitation. | cartas muito formais, discursos |
| Neutro | Thanks for inviting me. | quase sempre |
| Informal | Cheers for the invite! | amigos, colegas próximos |
| Gíria | That party was sick, no cap. | grupo de amigos (cuidado!) |
| Técnico | The party of the first part shall... | contratos |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L3/example-01.m4a) In Romeo and Juliet, "Wherefore art thou Romeo?" means "Why are you Romeo?", not "Where are you?" — _Em Romeu e Julieta, "Wherefore art thou Romeo?" significa "Por que você é Romeu?", não "Onde você está?"_
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L3/example-02.m4a) I was absolutely knackered after the trip, and completely skint too. — _Eu estava completamente morto de cansaço depois da viagem, e completamente sem grana também._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L3/example-03.m4a) Using Gen Z slang in a job interview is a register clash that's unlikely to impress. — _Usar gíria da geração Z numa entrevista de emprego é um choque de registro que dificilmente vai impressionar._

> 💡 **Dica:** Gíria envelhece rápido — o que é "cool" hoje pode soar "cringe" amanhã. Para **compreender**, consulte dicionários de gíria e de uso informal; para **usar**, observe como seus amigos falantes nativos falam e imite com moderação. E lembre: um brasileiro usando "innit" num sotaque americano pode soar engraçado (às vezes de propósito!).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ "Wherefore art thou?" = "Where are you?"  
  ✅ **wherefore = why**  
  Um dos falsos amigos mais famosos do inglês arcaico.
- ❌ Dear Hiring Manager, I'm lowkey the perfect candidate, no cap.  
  ✅ **Dear Hiring Manager, I believe I am an excellent fit for this position.**  
  Choque de registro involuntário.
- ❌ Using "thou" as a formal "you" (like "o senhor").  
  ✅ **Historically, thou was informal/intimate; you was the polite form.**  
  Ironia histórica: thou, hoje arcaico, era a forma íntima.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Sotaque e registro

Registro também se ouve: o **arcaico** costuma ser lido com ritmo lento e solene; a **gíria** britânica vem com sotaques regionais (o "t" glotal de "innit" = "í'it"; "mate" = "mái'"); a americana, com reduções ("gonna", "kinda"). Imitar sotaque **não** é obrigatório — entender é.

- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L3/pron-01.m4a) (solemn) Wherefore art thou Romeo?

- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L3/pron-03.m4a) (American slang) That was kinda awesome, dude.

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Linguist:** Slang has a curious life cycle. It usually begins within a specific group — teenagers, musicians, gamers, a particular neighbourhood — as a way of signalling belonging. Using the right words shows you're an insider. But slang is a victim of its own success. As it spreads, through music, films and especially social media, outsiders start using it too: parents, advertisers, politicians trying to sound young. And the moment that happens, it loses its value as a marker of the group, so the group moves on to something new. That's why slang can seem to change almost every year, and why words that sounded cool to one generation sound embarrassing to the next. Interestingly, a few slang words escape this cycle and become standard English. "Mob", "bus", "cool" and "OK" were all once considered informal or slang. Others, like "groovy" or "rad", become firmly attached to a particular decade. For learners, the lesson is simple: understand slang, enjoy it — but be careful about adopting it, because you may be wearing last year's fashion without realizing it.

</details>

## 📖 Reading — Thou, you and the politics of politeness [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L3/reading.m4a)

Modern English has one second-person pronoun: you. Whether we are addressing a child, a stranger, a president or a crowd of thousands, the same word serves. It was not always so. In Middle and Early Modern English, speakers could choose between two forms, much as speakers of Portuguese, French or German still can. "Thou" (with its forms thee, thy and thine) was used in the singular, while "you" (or "ye") was originally plural. Over time, influenced partly by French usage, "you" also came to be used as a polite form for addressing a single social superior, while "thou" was reserved for intimates, children, inferiors — or for deliberate insult. Shakespeare's characters switch between the two with considerable subtlety. A lover may move from the respectful "you" to the tender "thou" at a moment of intimacy; a master may address a servant as "thou" while expecting "you" in return; and to "thou" a stranger could be a calculated provocation. Audiences of the time would have registered these shifts instantly, much as a Brazilian notices the difference between "você" and "o senhor". By the eighteenth century, however, "thou" had largely disappeared from standard English, surviving mainly in regional dialects, in religious language and among groups such as the Quakers, who deliberately used it for everyone as a statement of equality. The irony is that "thou", once the form of intimacy and familiarity, now sounds to most English speakers solemn, distant and archaic — the language of prayers and old poetry. The loss of the distinction has had an interesting consequence. Since English can no longer signal social distance through its pronouns, it does so through other means: titles, first names versus surnames, modal verbs, indirectness and the choice of vocabulary. Politeness in English, in other words, did not disappear with "thou". It simply moved elsewhere — into the subtleties of register that every advanced learner must eventually master.

**Questions:**
1. What was the original difference between thou and you?
2. How did the usage later develop?
3. Why did the Quakers use thou for everyone?
4. How does English signal social distance now?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Você" e "thou": uma história paralela

O português também viveu uma transformação dos pronomes: **"vossa mercê"** (tratamento respeitoso) virou **"vosmecê"** e depois **"você"**, que hoje, no Brasil, é a forma comum e neutra — enquanto o **"tu"** sobrevive em várias regiões. No inglês, aconteceu algo parecido com **you** e **thou**, mas com o resultado oposto: o íntimo **thou** desapareceu e o polido **you** ficou para todos.

- Arcaico: thou/thee/thy, art, hast, wherefore (= why), 'tis — hoje em citações e ironia.

- Choque de registro: erro se involuntário, humor se intencional.
- Gíria envelhece rápido: entenda tudo, use com cuidado.
