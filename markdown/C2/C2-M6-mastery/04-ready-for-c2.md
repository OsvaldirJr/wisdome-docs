## 🧩 Grammar: C2 review and exam strategies

**Revisão do C2 — o que diferencia este nível:**
- **Precisão lexical:** quase-sinônimos, conotação, collocations, intensidade (M1-L1)
- **Implícito:** litotes, negação enfática, implicatura (M1-L2); binômios e expressões fixas (M1-L3)
- **Estrutura e estilo:** orações absolutas (M1-L4), inversão literária e fronting (M2-L1), figuras retóricas (M2-L4), pontuação expressiva (M4-L3)
- **Leitura crítica:** spin, eufemismo e agente oculto (M2-L2); conectores formais e linguagem jurídica (M2-L3, M5-L3)
- **Certeza e argumentação:** modalidade epistêmica fina (M3-L1), voz acadêmica (M3-L2), hipóteses complexas (M3-L3), comparações sofisticadas (M3-L4)
- **Literatura e crítica:** discurso indireto livre (M4-L1), som e ritmo (M4-L2), linguagem avaliativa (M4-L4)
- **Língua no mundo:** neologismos (M5-L1), variedades (M5-L2), resumo e síntese (M5-L4)
- **Maestria comunicativa:** mediação (M6-L1), gerenciamento da interação (M6-L2), registros extremos (M6-L3)

**Cambridge C2 Proficiency** (4 partes):
- **Reading and Use of English** (1h30, 7 partes): vocabulário em contexto, lacunas abertas, **formação de palavras**, **key word transformations** (3–8 palavras), textos longos, texto com parágrafos removidos e correspondência múltipla
- **Writing** (1h30, 2 partes): **Part 1** — essay resumindo e avaliando **dois textos** (240–280 palavras); **Part 2** — uma tarefa à escolha (artigo, carta, relatório ou review, 280–320 palavras)
- **Listening** (cerca de 40 min, 4 partes), com variedade de sotaques
- **Speaking** (cerca de 16 min, **em dupla**, 3 partes): entrevista, tarefa colaborativa com imagens, e um **long turn** individual seguido de discussão

**IELTS:** o C2 corresponde aproximadamente às bandas **8.5–9**, que exigem uso "totalmente flexível e preciso" da língua, com erros raríssimos.

**Estratégias de alto nível:**
1. **Reading:** a resposta raramente usa as mesmas palavras do texto — procure **paráfrases** e cuidado com **distratores** (palavras iguais, sentido diferente).
2. **Use of English:** em key word transformations, identifique a **estrutura testada** (inversão, passiva de relato, expressão idiomática, phrasal verb) e **não altere** a palavra dada.
3. **Writing:** **planeje** (10 min), **responda tudo**, mostre **variedade natural** (não forçada), **parafraseie** os textos-base (Part 1), **revise** (5 min).
4. **Listening:** atenção a **atitude, opinião e propósito** dos falantes, não só a fatos.
5. **Speaking:** **desenvolva**, **interaja**, **mostre nuance** ("it depends", "to some extent"), **gerencie** hesitações com naturalidade.

| Parte | Formato | Dica C2 |
|---|---|---|
| Reading & Use of English | 1h30, 7 partes | Paráfrases e estrutura testada |
| Writing Part 1 | Essay: 2 textos, 240–280 palavras | Resumir + avaliar, sem copiar |
| Writing Part 2 | Artigo/carta/relatório/review, 280–320 | Registro e gênero adequados |
| Listening | ~40 min, 4 partes | Atitudes e opiniões |
| Speaking | ~16 min, em dupla | Desenvolver, interagir, nuançar |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L4/example-01.m4a) It's not so much a question of knowing more words as of knowing exactly when to use them. — _Não é tanto uma questão de saber mais palavras, mas de saber exatamente quando usá-las._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L4/example-02.m4a) Had I known at the beginning how long the journey would be, I might never have started — and that would have been the greatest mistake of my life. — _Se eu soubesse no início quanto tempo a jornada duraria, talvez nunca tivesse começado — e esse teria sido o maior erro da minha vida._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L4/example-03.m4a) Mastery, I have come to believe, is less a destination than a way of travelling. — _Domínio, passei a acreditar, é menos um destino do que uma forma de viajar._

> 💡 **Dica:** No C2, **a perfeição não é exigida** — mas a **precisão** e a **flexibilidade** sim. O examinador busca alguém que use a língua como um **instrumento**: adequando registro, tom, estrutura e vocabulário a cada situação, com naturalidade. Você chegou aqui. Confie no caminho.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Forcing advanced structures into every sentence.  
  ✅ **Use C2 structures where they add precision or effect.**  
  Variedade forçada soa artificial; o C2 valoriza naturalidade.
- ❌ Writing Part 1: copying phrases from the two texts.  
  ✅ **Paraphrase consistently and add your evaluation.**  
  Cópia é penalizada; avaliação é obrigatória.
- ❌ Key word transformation: changing the given word or using more than eight words.  
  ✅ **Keep the given word unchanged and use 3–8 words.**  
  As regras da tarefa são estritas.

## 📖 Reading — What it means to master a language [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M6-L4/reading.m4a)

The Common European Framework of Reference describes the C2 user in a handful of carefully chosen phrases: someone who can understand with ease virtually everything heard or read, summarize information from different sources, reconstructing arguments and accounts in a coherent presentation, and express themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in the most complex situations. Each phrase rewards closer attention. "With ease" suggests that comprehension is no longer an effort but a habit. "Reconstructing arguments" implies not merely understanding a text but being able to rebuild its logic in one's own words for someone else. "Spontaneously" means without preparation — in the meeting, at the dinner table, in the unexpected moment. And "finer shades of meaning" points to the domain in which C2 differs most from every level below: nuance. A B2 learner knows that "stubborn" and "determined" are related. A C1 learner knows that one is negative and one is positive. A C2 user knows when to choose "determined" in a reference letter in order to describe, diplomatically, someone who was in fact rather stubborn — and understands the same move when others make it. It is worth noting what the descriptors do not say. They do not require the C2 user to sound like a native speaker, to have no accent, or never to make a mistake. Many C2 users retain a clear accent; many native speakers would struggle with C2 reading tasks. Mastery, in the framework's terms, is defined not by origin but by capability — by what one can do with the language. This, perhaps, is the most encouraging message for anyone who has followed the long road from "Hello, my name is..." to the subtle machinery of litotes and inverted conditionals. The goal was never to become someone else. It was to become oneself, more fully, in another language. For those who have arrived, that goal has been reached. For everyone else, it is waiting — one word, one conversation, one shade of meaning at a time.

**Questions:**
1. What does "reconstructing arguments" imply?
2. In what domain does C2 differ most from other levels?
3. What do the descriptors NOT require?
4. According to the final paragraph, what was the goal of learning?

## 📝 Writing

C2 Proficiency Writing Part 2 — Article (280–320 words): "An international magazine has invited readers to submit articles titled 'What learning a language has taught me — beyond the language'. Write your article, reflecting on your experience and drawing broader conclusions." Show the full range of C2 language naturally.

**Model:**

> What learning a language has taught me — beyond the language  
>   
> I began learning English, like many Brazilians, with a vague sense that it would be "useful". It was. But had anyone told me then what it would really teach me, I would scarcely have believed them.  
>   
> The first lesson was humility. Nothing exposes the limits of one's intelligence quite like trying to order a coffee in a language one barely speaks. For an adult accustomed to being articulate, the experience of sounding, in effect, like a child was deeply uncomfortable. Yet it taught me something no textbook could: how it feels to be misunderstood — and, by extension, how many people around me must feel it every day.  
>   
> The second lesson was patience. Language learning is, by its nature, a slow burn. There were months, particularly at the intermediate stage, when I seemed to make no progress at all. Only in hindsight did I see that the plateau was not a wall but a change of terrain, requiring a different kind of effort.  
>   
> The third, and perhaps most profound, lesson concerned perspective. Words are not simply labels for things that exist independently of them; they are ways of seeing. Learning that English has no exact equivalent of "saudade", or that British understatement can transform "not bad" into high praise, made me realize how much of what I had considered "natural" was, in fact, cultural. I began to look at Portuguese — and at Brazil — with new eyes.  
>   
> None of this means that the language itself was unimportant. It opened professional doors and introduced me to friends I would otherwise never have met. But the deepest benefits were not linguistic at all.  
>   
> If I had to sum up the journey, I would say this: learning another language does not make you someone else. It makes you more fully yourself — only now, you have two windows through which to see the world.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Depois do C2

O CEFR termina no C2, mas o aprendizado não. A partir daqui, o caminho é **especializar-se** (o inglês da sua profissão, da literatura, da diplomacia), **criar** (escrever, ensinar, traduzir, palestrar) e **viver** na língua. Muitos falantes C2 se tornam tradutores, intérpretes, professores ou mediadores interculturais. Seja qual for o seu próximo passo: **congratulations — you have come full circle. Now, keep going.**
