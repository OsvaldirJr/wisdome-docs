# All things considered
**C2-M1-L4 · Precision and Nuance** — Orações absolutas: Weather permitting, The meeting over, With prices rising...  
⏱ 80 min

## 🎯 Nesta aula você vai
- Usar orações absolutas com sujeito próprio (The meeting over, we left)
- Usar with/without + objeto + particípio ou adjetivo (With prices rising, ...)
- Usar expressões absolutas fixas (weather permitting, all things considered, generally speaking)
- Evitar participles "pendurados" (dangling) e usar o estilo em narrativa e descrição

## 🔥 Warm-up
"**Terminada a reunião**, fomos embora." "**Se o tempo permitir**, faremos a trilha." "**Com os preços subindo**, as famílias cortam gastos." Estas são **orações absolutas**: pequenas orações com **sujeito próprio** e sem verbo conjugado, que dão ao texto um ritmo **literário e condensado**. Tema: **viagens e narrativa**.

- What's the most memorable journey you've ever made?

## 🧩 Grammar: Absolute constructions

Uma **oração absoluta** tem **sujeito próprio** (diferente do da oração principal) e um particípio, adjetivo, advérbio ou locução — **sem verbo conjugado**. Ela acrescenta circunstância (tempo, causa, condição, descrição).

**1. Substantivo + particípio (-ing / -ed)**
- "**The work done**, we went home." (= When the work was done...)
- "**The sun having set**, the temperature dropped."
- "**Weather permitting**, the ceremony will be held outdoors."
- "**Other things being equal**, the cheaper option is better."

**2. Substantivo + adjetivo / advérbio / locução** (muito comum em descrições literárias):
- "**The meeting over**, they left."
- "She stood at the window, **her face pale**."
- "He walked in, **hands in his pockets**."
- "The children ran out, **their schoolbags forgotten**."

**3. With / Without + objeto + particípio/adjetivo/locução** (mais natural na fala):
- "**With prices rising**, families are cutting back."
- "**With the deadline approaching**, nobody slept."
- "She fell asleep **with the light on**."
- "**Without anyone noticing**, he slipped out."

**4. Expressões absolutas fixas** (sem sujeito explícito, aceitas como "comentário"):
- **generally / strictly / broadly / frankly speaking** · **all things considered** · **considering** + noun · **given** + noun · **judging by / from** · **assuming** · **that said / that being said** · **talking of** · **taking everything into account**

**5. Cuidado com o dangling participle** (erro de sujeito):
- ❌ "**Walking** to the station, **the rain** started." (a chuva estava andando?)
- ✅ "**Walking** to the station, **I** got caught in the rain." (mesmo sujeito)
- ✅ "**I was walking** to the station **when** the rain started."
- As **expressões fixas** do item 4 são exceções aceitas.

| Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| Noun + -ing | Time permitting, we'll visit the museum. |
| Noun + -ed | The decision made, there was no going back. |
| Noun + adj/adv | The holiday over, they returned to work. |
| Noun + locução | He sat, head in hands. |
| With + noun + -ing/-ed | With inflation falling, rates may drop. |
| Expressão fixa | Generally speaking, it's safe. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M1-L4/example-01.m4a) The last ferry gone, we had no choice but to spend the night on the island. — _Tendo partido a última balsa, não tivemos escolha a não ser passar a noite na ilha._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M1-L4/example-02.m4a) She sat on the steps of the old church, her notebook open on her knees, watching the market come to life. — _Ela estava sentada nos degraus da velha igreja, o caderno aberto sobre os joelhos, observando o mercado ganhar vida._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M1-L4/example-03.m4a) With tourism booming and rents soaring, many locals can no longer afford to live in the historic centre. — _Com o turismo em alta e os aluguéis disparando, muitos moradores já não conseguem morar no centro histórico._

> 💡 **Dica:** Português tem o equivalente exato: "**Terminada a aula**, saímos", "**Feito isso**, ...", "**Dadas as circunstâncias**...". Em inglês, use orações absolutas com **moderação**: na escrita literária e formal ficam elegantes; na fala informal, prefira **with** + objeto + -ing.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Arriving at the hotel, the receptionist welcomed us.  
  ✅ **When we arrived at the hotel, the receptionist welcomed us. / Arriving at the hotel, we were welcomed by the receptionist.**  
  Dangling participle: o sujeito precisa ser o mesmo.
- ❌ The work was done, we went home.  
  ✅ **The work done, we went home. / When the work was done, we went home.**  
  Duas orações completas exigem conjunção; a absoluta não tem verbo conjugado.
- ❌ With the prices rise, people buy less.  
  ✅ **With prices rising, people buy less.**  
  with + substantivo + particípio (-ing).

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Pausas e entonação

Orações absolutas formam um **bloco de entonação** separado, como um parêntese, geralmente com tom **levemente ascendente** antes da oração principal: "The meeting over ↗, | we went home ↘." No meio ou no fim da frase, use uma pausa e tom mais baixo: "She sat there, | her eyes closed."

- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M1-L4/pron-01.m4a) The meeting over ↗, | we went home ↘.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M1-L4/pron-02.m4a) Weather permitting ↗, | we'll climb the peak ↘.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M1-L4/pron-03.m4a) She sat on the beach, | her eyes closed.

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Editor:** What separates great travel writing from a guidebook? Generally speaking, it's the shift from information to experience. A guidebook tells you the museum opens at nine. A travel writer puts you on the steps outside at ten to nine, the street still wet from the night's rain, a stray dog asleep in a patch of sunlight. Notice the grammar there — those little phrases with their own subjects, "the street still wet", "a dog asleep". They let a writer add layers of detail without stopping the flow of the sentence. Writers like Jan Morris, Bruce Chatwin and Paul Theroux used them constantly. The other ingredient is honesty. The best travel writers aren't afraid to describe disappointment, boredom or their own prejudices — and, all things considered, that's what makes us trust them when they describe wonder.

## 📖 Reading — Dawn in Ouro Preto [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M1-L4/reading.m4a)

I arrived in Ouro Preto after midnight, the bus from Belo Horizonte having been delayed by fog on the mountain road, and I remember almost nothing of that first night except the cold and the sound of rain on a tiled roof. It was the morning that stayed with me. I woke before six, the room still dark, and, unable to sleep, went out to walk. The town, built on hills so steep that some streets seem to defy gravity, was entirely silent. Mist lay in the valleys between the churches, their towers rising out of it like the masts of ships at anchor. The cobblestones, polished by three centuries of feet and that night's rain, shone under the last of the streetlamps. Somewhere below, a dog barked once, then thought better of it. I climbed towards the Church of São Francisco de Assis, my breath visible in the air, my legs protesting at every step. With the light growing stronger, details began to emerge: the soapstone carvings above the doorway, the blue and white of colonial façades, a woman in a doorway sweeping her front step, her radio murmuring a morning programme. She nodded to me without a word, as if strangers at dawn were part and parcel of her day. History in Ouro Preto is not a subject but an atmosphere. Gold made the town rich in the eighteenth century, its wealth flowing to Lisbon and beyond, and the beauty that remains was built, in large part, by enslaved labour — a fact no honest visitor should forget. Yet that morning, the streets empty and the mist slowly lifting, it felt less like a museum than a place still dreaming. By eight, the first tour buses had arrived. The spell broken, I went in search of coffee. All things considered, I have rarely spent two hours better.

**Questions:**
1. Why did the writer arrive late?
2. Find three absolute constructions in the text.
3. What uncomfortable historical fact does the writer mention?
4. What broke the "spell"?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Literatura de viagem em inglês

A **travel writing** é um gênero literário prestigiado em inglês, com autores como **Jan Morris**, **Bruce Chatwin** (*In Patagonia*), **Paul Theroux** e **Pico Iyer**. O Brasil também inspirou clássicos, como *Brazilian Adventure* (1933), de Peter Fleming, sobre uma expedição em busca do explorador desaparecido Percy Fawcett. Ler esses autores é uma ótima forma de absorver estilo C2.

## ✅ Resumo
- Oração absoluta: sujeito próprio + particípio/adjetivo/locução (The meeting over, ...).
- With + objeto + -ing/-ed: With prices rising, ...
- Fixas: weather permitting, all things considered, generally speaking, that being said.
- Evite dangling participles: o sujeito do particípio deve ser o da oração principal.
