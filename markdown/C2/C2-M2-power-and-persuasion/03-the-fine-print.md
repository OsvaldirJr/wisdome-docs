## 🧩 Grammar: Formal connectives

**1. Concessão formal:**
- **notwithstanding** (= despite / however) — antes ou depois do substantivo: "**Notwithstanding** the delay, the project was completed." / "The delay **notwithstanding**, ..."; também advérbio: "It was expensive; it was, **notwithstanding**, a success."
- **albeit** (= although) + adjetivo/advérbio/frase sem verbo: "a significant, **albeit** temporary, improvement" / "He agreed, **albeit** reluctantly."
- **be that as it may** (C1) / **that said** / **nonetheless**

**2. Extensão / grau:**
- **insofar as / in so far as** (= to the extent that): "The plan is fair **insofar as** everyone pays the same."
- **inasmuch as** (= since / to the extent that, formal): "He is responsible **inasmuch as** he signed the document."

**3. Meio / relação:**
- **whereby** (= by which, através do qual): "a system **whereby** employees can buy shares"
- **wherein / whereupon**: "the document **wherein** the terms are stated" / "He signed, **whereupon** the meeting ended." (= depois do que)

**4. Resultado / consequência:**
- **hence** (= portanto / daí) — muitas vezes sem verbo: "The data were incomplete, **hence** the delay."
- **thus** (= assim, portanto): "Costs fell; **thus**, prices could be reduced."
- **thereby** + -ing (= com isso, desse modo): "The bank raised rates, **thereby** reducing inflation."

**5. Finalidade negativa:**
- **lest** + (should) + verbo base (= para que não, formal/literário): "Keep a copy **lest** the original **be** lost."
- **for fear that / for fear of** + -ing: "He kept quiet **for fear of** losing his job."

**6. Linguagem jurídica (here- / there-):**
- **hereby** (por meio deste), **herein** (neste documento), **hereinafter** (doravante denominado), **thereof** (dele/disso), **therein** (nele)
- "The Company (**hereinafter** "the Provider") **hereby** agrees..."

⚠️ **Use com critério:** em e-mails comuns, "hereby" e "lest" soam arcaicos. São adequados em **textos jurídicos, acadêmicos e muito formais**.

| Conector | Equivale a | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| notwithstanding | despite | Notwithstanding the risks, they proceeded. |
| albeit | although | a small, albeit important, change |
| insofar as | to the extent that | Insofar as I know, it's true. |
| whereby | by which | a scheme whereby... |
| hence | therefore / that's why | It was late, hence the rush. |
| thereby | by doing this | ...thereby saving money. |
| lest | so that... not | Lest we forget. |
| hereby | by this document | I hereby resign. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M2-L3/example-01.m4a) Notwithstanding any other provision of this agreement, the user remains responsible for the security of their password. — _Não obstante qualquer outra disposição deste acordo, o usuário permanece responsável pela segurança de sua senha._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M2-L3/example-02.m4a) The government introduced a scheme whereby low-income families receive a monthly payment, thereby reducing extreme poverty. — _O governo introduziu um programa pelo qual famílias de baixa renda recebem um pagamento mensal, reduzindo assim a pobreza extrema._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M2-L3/example-03.m4a) The results were encouraging, albeit based on a small sample; hence the need for further research. — _Os resultados foram encorajadores, embora baseados numa amostra pequena; daí a necessidade de mais pesquisa._

> 💡 **Dica:** Muitos desses conectores têm equivalente direto no português jurídico: **notwithstanding** = não obstante; **hereby** = por meio deste / pelo presente; **whereby** = pelo qual; **hence** = daí; **albeit** = ainda que. Saber isso ajuda muito na tradução de contratos.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Albeit it was expensive, it was worth it.  
  ✅ **Although it was expensive, it was worth it. / It was expensive, albeit worth it.**  
  albeit normalmente não introduz uma oração completa com sujeito e verbo.
- ❌ The data was incomplete, hence we delayed the report.  
  ✅ **The data was incomplete; hence the delay. / ..., and hence we delayed the report.**  
  hence costuma vir com substantivo ou depois de ponto e vírgula/and.
- ❌ Write it down lest you will forget.  
  ✅ **Write it down lest you forget / lest you should forget.**  
  lest + verbo base (ou should).

<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Researcher:** Almost everyone clicks "I agree" without reading. In one well-known experiment, published in 2016, researchers Jonathan Obar and Anne Oeldorf-Hirsch created a fictitious social network and asked students to sign up. Buried in the terms of service was a clause whereby users agreed to give up their first-born child as payment. The overwhelming majority of participants agreed — most of them, apparently, without noticing. The study was, admittedly, an exaggeration, albeit a revealing one. Real terms of service rarely demand children, but they frequently contain provisions whereby users waive important rights: allowing companies to share data with third parties, to change the terms at any time, or to resolve disputes through private arbitration rather than in court. Several jurisdictions, including the European Union, have introduced rules requiring that key information be presented clearly, thereby giving consumers a better chance of understanding what they're signing. Notwithstanding these efforts, the basic problem remains: terms are long, readers are busy, and the default is to trust.

## 📖 Reading — In defence of legalese? [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M2-L3/reading.m4a)

Few styles of writing are as widely disliked as "legalese": the dense, archaic and seemingly impenetrable language of contracts, statutes and terms of service. Critics have long argued that it serves mainly to exclude ordinary readers, thereby protecting the interests of lawyers and the powerful. Movements for "plain language" in law have won significant victories, and in many countries, government documents and consumer contracts must now be written in a way that the average reader can understand. Yet legal language is not merely the product of tradition or self-interest. Many of its peculiarities exist for a reason. Words such as "notwithstanding", "hereinafter" and "whereby" allow drafters to establish precise relationships between different parts of a document — which clause overrides which, how a term is defined, by what mechanism an obligation arises. Ordinary words, by contrast, tend to be vague, and vagueness in a contract can lead to years of costly litigation. Repetition, too, has a purpose: a lawyer who writes "the Buyer" fifty times rather than switching to "they" or "the customer" ensures that no court can later argue that the parties meant different people. Some features, admittedly, are harder to defend. Doublets and triplets such as "null and void", "cease and desist" and "give, devise and bequeath" are largely historical relics, dating from a time when English law drew on English, French and Latin and drafters used all three to be safe. Long sentences with multiple embedded clauses serve the convenience of the writer far more than the comprehension of the reader. The challenge, then, is not to abolish legal language but to distinguish precision from obscurity. A good contract should be as exact as necessary — albeit no more complicated than it needs to be. Insofar as plain language can achieve that, it is not the enemy of the law but its ally.

**Questions:**
1. What do critics say legalese is mainly for?
2. Why do words like "notwithstanding" exist?
3. Why do lawyers repeat "the Buyer" instead of using pronouns?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Lest we forget"

A expressão **"Lest we forget"** (para que não esqueçamos) aparece em memoriais de guerra em todo o mundo de língua inglesa, especialmente no Reino Unido, Austrália, Nova Zelândia e Canadá. Vem de um poema de **Rudyard Kipling** (1897) e é repetida todo ano no **Remembrance Day** (11 de novembro) e no **Anzac Day** (25 de abril). É um dos poucos usos de **lest** ainda vivos no dia a dia.

## ✅ Resumo
- Concessão: notwithstanding (= despite), albeit (+ adj/adv).
- Extensão: insofar as, inasmuch as. Meio: whereby. Resultado: hence, thus, thereby + -ing.
- lest + verbo base (= para que não); for fear of + -ing.
- Jurídico: hereby, herein, hereinafter, thereof — só em textos formais.
