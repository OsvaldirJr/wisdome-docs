## 🧩 Grammar: Modals and expressions of obligation in legal texts

**1. Shall** — em leis e contratos, expressa **obrigação** ou **determinação legal** (não futuro):
- "The tenant **shall** pay the rent on the first day of each month." (= deve pagar)
- "Everyone **shall** have the right to..." (= terá/tem garantido)
- **Shall not** = proibição: "No one **shall** be held in slavery."
- ⚠️ Muitos especialistas em *plain English* recomendam substituir **shall** por **must** (obrigação) ou pelo presente ("Everyone **has** the right..."), pois "shall" pode ser ambíguo.

**2. Must / must not** — obrigação / proibição clara.

**3. May / may not** — em textos legais:
- **may** = **permissão** ou **possibilidade**: "The landlord **may** inspect the property with 24 hours' notice."
- **may not** = **proibição** (= is not allowed to): "Tenants **may not** sublet the property."

**4. Expressões com be + adjetivo/particípio:**
- **be entitled to** + substantivo/verbo (ter direito a): "Workers **are entitled to** thirty days' leave." / "...entitled to receive..."
- **be liable for** (ser responsável por) / **be liable to** (estar sujeito a — pena, multa): "Offenders **are liable to** a fine of up to..."
- **be obliged / required to** (ser obrigado a)
- **be prohibited / barred / forbidden from** + -ing: "Members **are prohibited from** sharing..."
- **be subject to** (estar sujeito a): "**Subject to** approval" / "Prices **are subject to** change."
- **be eligible for / to** (ser elegível)
- **be exempt from** (ser isento de)

**5. Passiva e linguagem impessoal** — típicas de leis: "It **is hereby** enacted...", "Any person who... **shall be guilty of** an offence."

**6. Explicando em linguagem simples:** "The tenant shall not keep pets" → "You're not allowed to have pets."

| Texto legal | Significado | Linguagem simples |
|---|---|---|
| shall | obrigação legal | must / has to |
| shall not / may not | proibição | is not allowed to |
| may | permissão / possibilidade | can / is allowed to |
| be entitled to | ter direito a | have the right to |
| be liable for | ser responsável por | be responsible for |
| be liable to (a fine) | estar sujeito a | can be punished with |
| be subject to | depender de / sujeito a | depends on / can change |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L3/example-01.m4a) Under the law, consumers are entitled to cancel online purchases within seven days. — _Segundo a lei, os consumidores têm direito a cancelar compras online em até sete dias._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L3/example-02.m4a) Any person who fails to comply with this order shall be liable to a fine. — _Qualquer pessoa que descumprir esta ordem estará sujeita a multa._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L3/example-03.m4a) Employees may not disclose confidential information to third parties. — _Os funcionários não podem divulgar informações confidenciais a terceiros._

> 💡 **Dica:** Atenção: **shall** em contratos **não** é futuro! E **may not** em regulamentos é **proibição**, não "talvez não". Ao ler um contrato em inglês, pergunte sempre: isto é um **direito** (entitled, may), um **dever** (shall, must, required) ou uma **proibição** (shall not, may not, prohibited)?

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Reading "The tenant shall pay..." as future ("will probably pay").  
  ✅ **It means "The tenant must pay...".**  
  Em textos legais, shall expressa obrigação.
- ❌ You are entitled for a refund.  
  ✅ **You are entitled to a refund.**  
  entitled TO.
- ❌ Visitors are prohibited to take photos.  
  ✅ **Visitors are prohibited from taking photos.**  
  prohibited FROM + -ing.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Leitura de textos formais

<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Professor:** On 10 December 1948, the General Assembly of the United Nations adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Drafted in the aftermath of the Second World War, it set out, for the first time, a list of rights that all human beings are entitled to, regardless of nationality, race, sex, religion or any other status. The committee that drafted it was chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt, and it included representatives from many countries and traditions. Its thirty articles cover civil and political rights — such as the right to life, liberty and a fair trial — as well as economic, social and cultural rights, including the right to education and to work. Notice the language: "No one shall be held in slavery"; "Everyone has the right to education". Technically, the Declaration itself is not a binding treaty. But it has inspired many later legally binding agreements and national constitutions, including Brazil's 1988 Constitution. It has also been translated into more languages than almost any other document in the world.

## 📖 Reading — Rights in the digital age [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L3/reading.m4a)

When the Universal Declaration of Human Rights was drafted in 1948, its authors could not have imagined smartphones, social media or artificial intelligence. Yet many of the most urgent debates about rights today concern precisely these technologies. Article 12 of the Declaration states that no one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with their privacy. What does that mean in a world where vast amounts of personal data are collected, analysed and sold, often without users fully understanding what they have agreed to? In response, a growing number of jurisdictions have adopted data protection laws. The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation, which came into force in 2018, has been particularly influential; Brazil's own General Data Protection Law (LGPD) followed shortly afterwards. Under such laws, individuals are typically entitled to know what data is held about them, to request its correction and, in certain circumstances, to have it deleted. Organizations that process data are obliged to have a legal basis for doing so and may be liable to substantial fines if they fail to protect it. New questions continue to emerge. Should people have a right to an explanation when an algorithm denies them a loan or a job? Is internet access itself becoming a basic right, given that so many essential services now depend on it? To what extent should platforms be held liable for harmful content posted by their users? These debates are far from settled. What is clear is that rights are not fixed for all time. They are interpreted, extended and sometimes contested as societies change. The language of the law may sound timeless — "shall", "entitled", "liable" — but the questions it must answer are constantly new.

## 📝 Writing

Write a "charter of rights and responsibilities" (250–300 words) for a community you belong to (a school, a workplace, an online community, a residents' association). Use legal-style modals and expressions, then add a plain English summary (60–80 words).

**Model:**

> Charter of Rights and Responsibilities — Riverside Online Learning Community  
>   
> Preamble  
> This Charter sets out the rights and responsibilities of all members of the Riverside Online Learning Community ("the Community"). By joining the Community, members agree to abide by its provisions.  
>   
> Article 1 — Respect  
> Every member shall have the right to participate in discussions without being subjected to harassment, discrimination or personal attacks. Members shall treat one another with courtesy, regardless of nationality, age, gender, religion or level of English.  
>   
> Article 2 — Freedom of expression  
> Members are entitled to express their opinions freely, subject to the limits set out in Article 1. Disagreement is welcome; abuse is not.  
>   
> Article 3 — Privacy  
> Members may not share other members' personal information, including photographs, messages or contact details, without their explicit consent. The Community shall not sell or disclose members' data to third parties.  
>   
> Article 4 — Academic integrity  
> Members shall submit only their own work. Members found to have plagiarised may be liable to suspension from the Community.  
>   
> Article 5 — Moderation  
> Moderators may remove content that breaches this Charter. Any member whose content has been removed shall be entitled to request an explanation and to appeal the decision within fourteen days.  
>   
> Article 6 — Amendments  
> This Charter may be amended by a majority vote of members, provided that all members are notified at least one month in advance.  
>   
> — Plain English summary —  
> Be respectful: no harassment or discrimination. You can say what you think, but don't attack people. Don't share other people's personal information; we won't sell yours. Only submit your own work — if you copy, you may be suspended. Moderators can delete posts that break the rules, but you can ask why and appeal. Rules can change only if most members vote for it, with a month's notice.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — A Constituição Cidadã

A **Constituição brasileira de 1988**, conhecida como "**Constituição Cidadã**", é uma das mais extensas do mundo e garante uma ampla lista de direitos sociais, como saúde, educação e moradia. Em inglês, é frequentemente descrita como **"one of the most rights-oriented constitutions in the world"**. Comparar sua linguagem com a de constituições anglófonas (muito mais curtas, como a dos EUA) é um ótimo exercício de vocabulário jurídico.
