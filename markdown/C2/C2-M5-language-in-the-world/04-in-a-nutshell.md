É a habilidade de **mediação** em nível máximo: sintetizar, parafrasear e posicionar-se.

## 🧩 Grammar: Paraphrasing and synthesizing

**1. Técnicas de paráfrase** (combine várias!):
- **Sinônimos:** "children" → "young people"; "harmful" → "detrimental"
- **Mudar a classe da palavra:** "Technology **has transformed** education" → "Education **has undergone a transformation** because of technology"
- **Mudar a estrutura (ativa/passiva, ordem):** "Schools should ban phones" → "Phones ought to be prohibited in schools"
- **Generalizar / abstrair:** "teachers, parents and pupils" → "the whole school community"
- **Usar um verbo de citação que mostra a posição do autor:** "The writer **contends / warns / dismisses the idea that**..."
⚠️ **Não copie** sequências de 3+ palavras do texto original (exceto termos técnicos).

**2. Resumir:** identifique **a tese** e **2–3 argumentos de cada texto**; ignore exemplos e detalhes.

**3. Sintetizar (comparar os textos):**
- **Concordância:** "Both writers **agree / concur** that..." / "The second text **echoes** this view..."
- **Divergência:** "**Whereas** the first writer..., the second..." / "The second writer **takes issue with**..." / "**Where they diverge** is..."
- **Complemento:** "The second text **adds** a further dimension..." / "...**goes further**, arguing that..."

**4. Avaliar (sua opinião):**
- "I find the first argument **more convincing** because..."
- "**While** the second writer **rightly points out**..., she **overlooks**..."
- "**In my view**, the most persuasive point is..."

**5. Estrutura do essay C2 Part 1 (240–280 palavras):**
1. **Introdução:** tema geral + o que os dois textos discutem
2. **Texto 1:** ideias principais (parafraseadas) + sua avaliação
3. **Texto 2:** ideias principais + relação com o texto 1 + sua avaliação
4. **Conclusão:** sua posição geral
(Alternativa: organizar por **temas**, comparando os dois textos em cada parágrafo.)

| Original | Paráfrase |
|---|---|
| Social media is harmful to teenagers. | Young people's well-being may be damaged by online platforms. |
| Schools should ban phones. | Mobile devices ought to be prohibited in classrooms. |
| Technology has transformed education. | Education has undergone a profound transformation as a result of technology. |
| Many experts disagree. | This view is far from universally accepted. |
| It costs a lot of money. | It entails considerable expense. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L4/example-01.m4a) Both writers concur that remote work offers greater flexibility; where they diverge is in their assessment of its long-term impact on collaboration. — _Os dois autores concordam que o trabalho remoto oferece mais flexibilidade; onde divergem é na avaliação de seu impacto de longo prazo na colaboração._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L4/example-02.m4a) The second writer takes issue with the idea that technology alone can solve educational inequality. — _O segundo autor contesta a ideia de que a tecnologia sozinha pode resolver a desigualdade educacional._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L4/example-03.m4a) In a nutshell, the first text is optimistic, the second cautious — and the truth, I would argue, lies somewhere in between. — _Em poucas palavras, o primeiro texto é otimista, o segundo cauteloso — e a verdade, eu diria, está em algum ponto intermediário._

> 💡 **Dica:** Na prova, **planeje 10 minutos**: sublinhe a tese e os argumentos de cada texto, anote **sinônimos** antes de escrever e decida sua opinião. O erro mais comum é **copiar frases inteiras** do texto — isso é penalizado. O segundo mais comum é **esquecer a avaliação**.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Copying full sentences from the input texts.  
  ✅ **Paraphrase using synonyms, new structures and reporting verbs.**  
  Na prova, cópia de trechos é penalizada.
- ❌ Summarizing without evaluating.  
  ✅ **Include your opinion on each text and an overall position.**  
  A tarefa exige resumo E avaliação.
- ❌ Including examples and minor details from the texts.  
  ✅ **Focus on the main arguments only.**  
  Com 240–280 palavras, não há espaço para detalhes.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Resumir oralmente (mediação)

Ao resumir um texto em voz alta (numa reunião, por exemplo), use **marcadores claros** e **pausas** entre as ideias: "So, in a nutshell, | the first report says... | The second, by contrast, | argues that... | Where they agree is...". Acentue as palavras de **contraste**.

## 📖 Reading — C2 Part 1 practice: two texts on tourism [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M5-L4/reading.m4a)

Text 1 — The case for tourism  
For many regions, tourism is not merely an industry but a lifeline. It brings income to places where few other opportunities exist, creates jobs for young people who might otherwise be forced to migrate, and provides funding for the preservation of historic buildings and natural landscapes. Moreover, travel broadens the mind: visitors who encounter other cultures at first hand often return home more tolerant and curious. To condemn tourism wholesale, as some critics now do, is to ignore the livelihoods of millions and the very real benefits of human contact across borders.  
  
Text 2 — The cost of popularity  
Across the world, a growing number of cities and natural sites are struggling under the weight of their own popularity. In some historic centres, short-term rentals have driven up housing costs so steeply that local residents can no longer afford to live there; neighbourhoods once full of life now resemble theme parks. Fragile ecosystems are damaged by crowds, and much of the money generated by tourism flows to international companies rather than local communities. This is not an argument against travel itself, but against an unregulated model that treats places as products. Without limits, tourism risks destroying the very things that make destinations worth visiting.  
  
Model essay  
Both texts address the impact of tourism on the places that receive it, but they reach rather different conclusions.  
  
The first writer presents tourism as vital for many communities, arguing that it generates income and employment in areas with few alternatives and helps finance the conservation of heritage and nature. The text also emphasizes the cultural value of travel, suggesting that contact with other ways of life fosters open-mindedness. While these points are undoubtedly valid, the writer arguably overstates the case by dismissing critics so readily.  
  
The second text, by contrast, focuses on the consequences of excessive tourism. Its author contends that soaring demand for holiday accommodation has priced residents out of their own neighbourhoods, that natural environments are suffering, and that the economic benefits often bypass local people. Crucially, however, the writer does not reject travel as such, but rather a model that lacks regulation.  
  
In my view, the second text offers the more nuanced analysis. It acknowledges, at least implicitly, the advantages highlighted in the first text, while identifying real problems that a purely positive account ignores. The challenge, as I see it, is not to choose between tourism and its critics, but to strike a balance: to welcome visitors while ensuring that communities retain control over their homes, their environment and their economy.

**Questions:**
1. What are the main arguments of Text 1?
2. What are the main arguments of Text 2?
3. Find two paraphrases in the model essay.
4. What is the writer's own evaluation?

## 📝 Writing

C2 Proficiency Writing Part 1 (240–280 words): Read the two texts on homework from the listening (or two short texts on another topic). Write an essay summarizing and evaluating the key points from both, using your own words throughout and including your own view.

**Model:**

> Homework: essential or outdated?  
>   
> The value of homework has long divided educators and parents, and the two contributions considered here illustrate the debate well.  
>   
> The first speaker defends homework as a fundamental component of education. In her view, tasks completed at home consolidate classroom learning, encourage pupils to work independently and allow families to follow their children's progress. Importantly, she acknowledges that much homework is poorly designed, attributing the problem not to the practice itself but to repetitive, time-consuming assignments that add little to students' understanding. This distinction seems to me a sensible one, although she offers little evidence to support her confidence in homework's benefits.  
>   
> The second speaker takes a considerably more sceptical position. Drawing on research, he argues that the advantages of homework are, at best, uncertain, particularly in primary education. Furthermore, he raises the important issue of fairness: pupils from more privileged homes, he contends, are far better placed to benefit from work set outside school, so homework may actually widen educational gaps. His proposal — to eliminate homework for younger children and greatly reduce it for older ones — follows logically from this analysis.  
>   
> On balance, I find the second argument more persuasive, largely because it addresses the question of equity, which the first speaker overlooks. That said, both contributions converge on one crucial point: the quantity and quality of homework matter far more than whether it exists at all. A reasonable compromise, I would suggest, might be to limit homework to short, purposeful tasks — such as reading — for younger pupils, while ensuring that schools provide quiet study spaces for those who lack them at home.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Resumo no ENEM e no exterior

No Brasil, a habilidade de resumir aparece em provas como o ENEM (interpretação de textos) e em vestibulares. Em universidades anglófonas, o **summary** e o **critical review** são tarefas acadêmicas constantes, e **plágio** (inclusive copiar frases sem aspas) é tratado com muita seriedade — podendo levar à reprovação ou expulsão. Parafrasear bem é, portanto, uma questão de **integridade acadêmica**.
