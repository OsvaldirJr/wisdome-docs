## 🧩 Grammar: Evaluative language and review structure

**1. Vocabulário avaliativo preciso** — substitua "good / bad / interesting":
- **Positivo:** accomplished, assured, compelling, luminous, nuanced, haunting, exhilarating, beautifully observed, a tour de force
- **Negativo:** derivative, formulaic, self-indulgent, overwrought, heavy-handed, laboured, uneven, patchy, forgettable
- **Neutro/descritivo:** understated, ambitious, sprawling, experimental, slow-paced, unconventional

**2. Avaliação com nuance** — o crítico C2 raramente é totalmente positivo ou negativo:
- **if / albeit** concessivo: "an **accomplished, if uneven**, debut" / "a **powerful, albeit flawed**, film"
- **never quite / not entirely / falls short of**: "The ending **never quite** convinces." / "It **falls short of** its ambitions."
- **what it lacks in X, it makes up for in Y**: "**What it lacks in** originality, **it makes up for in** charm."
- **for all its flaws / for all its brilliance** (apesar de): "**For all its flaws**, it's a remarkable achievement."
- **the film / book is at its best when...**: "The novel **is at its best when** it focuses on the family."

**3. Estrutura de uma review C2 (280–320 palavras):**
1. **Abertura com gancho + contexto** (autor/diretor, gênero, expectativa)
2. **Resumo breve** (sem spoilers!), no **presente**
3. **Análise** de 2–3 aspectos (atuação, roteiro, estilo, temas) com exemplos
4. **Pontos fracos** com nuance
5. **Avaliação final + recomendação** (para quem?)

**4. Registro:** semiformal, com personalidade; pode usar humor e opinião pessoal, mas sempre **justificada**.

| Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| adj, if adj | an ambitious, if flawed, novel |
| never quite | The plot never quite comes together. |
| falls short of | It falls short of the original. |
| What it lacks in X... | What it lacks in depth, it makes up for in style. |
| For all its... | For all its length, it never drags. |
| is at its best when | The series is at its best when it's quiet. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M4-L4/example-01.m4a) For all its visual brilliance, the film never quite finds an emotional centre. — _Apesar de todo o brilho visual, o filme nunca chega a encontrar um centro emocional._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M4-L4/example-02.m4a) What the album lacks in innovation, it more than makes up for in warmth and craftsmanship. — _O que falta ao álbum em inovação, ele compensa com sobra em calor humano e apuro técnico._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M4-L4/example-03.m4a) The novel is at its best when it abandons the main plot and simply observes the life of the village. — _O romance está no seu melhor quando abandona a trama principal e simplesmente observa a vida da aldeia._

> 💡 **Dica:** Na prova C2, o examinador procura **vocabulário avaliativo variado** e **opinião justificada**. Evite "very good" e "very interesting". Uma boa técnica: para cada adjetivo avaliativo, dê **um exemplo concreto** da obra que o justifique.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ The film is very interesting and very good.  
  ✅ **The film is compelling and beautifully shot.**  
  No C2, use vocabulário avaliativo preciso.
- ❌ It's an ambitious, but uneven film. (sem vírgula de fechamento)  
  ✅ **It's an ambitious, if uneven, film. / It's ambitious but uneven.**  
  Na estrutura adj, if adj, use as duas vírgulas.
- ❌ In the end, the hero dies and his wife marries his brother.  
  ✅ **Avoid spoilers: "The ending is likely to divide audiences."**  
  Reviews não devem revelar o final.

## 🎧 Listening — What makes a good critic?

**Questions:**
1. What is the least interesting part of a critic's job, according to the editor?
2. What are the three things a good critic does?
3. What kinds of reviews does she consider the worst?
4. Where does a critic's real value lie?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Editor:** People sometimes assume that a critic's job is simply to tell you whether something is good or bad. That's the least interesting part of it. A good critic does three things. First, they describe the work vividly enough that you can imagine it, without giving away what shouldn't be given away. Second, they analyse — they explain how the work achieves its effects, or why it fails to. And third, they place it in context: what is this artist trying to do, how does it relate to their earlier work, or to the tradition it belongs to? The verdict comes last, and it should feel earned. The worst reviews, in my experience, are the ones that are either entirely gushing or entirely dismissive. Real works of art are rarely perfect or worthless. Even a masterpiece has weaknesses; even a failure may contain moments of genuine brilliance. A critic's real value lies in noticing both — and in helping readers see what they might otherwise have missed.

</details>

## 📖 Reading — Review: The House of the Two Rivers [🔊](../../../audio/C2/C2-M4-L4/reading.m4a)

(The novel reviewed below is fictional and was created for this lesson.)  
  
Few debut novels arrive with as much expectation as The House of the Two Rivers, the first book by the Manaus-born writer Helena Barros. Shortlisted for a major prize before its publication in English, it has been described by its publishers as "the great Amazonian family saga". It is not quite that — but it is something more interesting.  
  
The novel follows three generations of the Andrade family, rubber merchants whose fortunes rise and fall with the boom and collapse of the trade in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. At its centre is the family's crumbling mansion, built where two rivers meet, and Dona Clara, the formidable matriarch who refuses to leave it even as the city around her decays.  
  
Barros is at her best when she slows down. Her descriptions of the river at dawn, of the humid stillness of the house in the afternoon, of the small rituals of a family pretending nothing has changed, are luminous. Her prose is understated yet precise, and her use of free indirect speech allows us to inhabit Clara's mind while quietly exposing her illusions. The scenes between Clara and her granddaughter, in particular, are a masterclass in what goes unsaid.  
  
The novel is less assured when it tries to cover too much ground. A subplot involving a foreign engineer feels heavy-handed, and the final section, which jumps abruptly to the 1970s, reads more like an epilogue than a conclusion. At over five hundred pages, the book occasionally sags, and a firmer editorial hand might have produced a tighter, more powerful novel.  
  
For all its flaws, however, this is an accomplished and haunting debut. What it lacks in structural discipline, it more than makes up for in atmosphere, emotional intelligence and sheer beauty of language. Readers who enjoy slow, immersive fiction will find much to admire. Barros is a writer to watch; her second novel may well be a masterpiece.

**Questions:**
1. How does the reviewer respond to the publisher's description?
2. When is the writer "at her best"?
3. What weaknesses does the reviewer identify?
4. Who does the reviewer recommend the novel to?

## 📝 Writing

C2 Proficiency–style review (280–320 words): "An international cultural website has asked readers to review a book, film or album by an artist from their country that deserves to be better known abroad. Describe the work, evaluate its strengths and weaknesses, and explain why international audiences should discover it."

**Model:**

> Vidas Secas (Barren Lives), by Graciliano Ramos  
>   
> Few novels manage to say so much in so few pages. First published in 1938 and available in English as Barren Lives, Graciliano Ramos's portrait of a family of migrants fleeing drought in the Brazilian sertão is barely 150 pages long, yet it remains one of the most powerful works of Brazilian literature.  
>   
> The novel follows Fabiano, his wife Sinha Vitória, their two sons and their dog, Baleia, as they wander across the parched landscape of the Northeast in search of work and water. Very little happens, in the conventional sense. The family finds shelter on an abandoned farm, endures the cruelty of a landowner and a soldier, and is eventually forced to move on again.  
>   
> What makes the book extraordinary is its form. Each chapter focuses on a different character, and Ramos uses free indirect speech to enter minds that have almost no words of their own. Fabiano struggles to express his anger; the children ask questions nobody can answer; and in the most famous chapter, the narrative enters the consciousness of the dying dog with a tenderness that is almost unbearable. Ramos's prose is spare, precise and utterly unsentimental — as dry, one might say, as the landscape it describes.  
>   
> The novel is not without its challenges for international readers. Its episodic structure can feel fragmentary, and some cultural and historical references may be unfamiliar without a good introduction. Its bleakness, too, will not be to everyone's taste.  
>   
> For all that, it is a masterpiece that has stood the test of time remarkably well. In an age of climate migration and growing inequality, its portrait of people driven from their land by forces beyond their control feels anything but dated. Readers who admire the restraint of writers such as Steinbeck or Coetzee will find in Ramos a kindred spirit — and a voice the world should hear far more often.  
>   
> Rating: ★★★★★
