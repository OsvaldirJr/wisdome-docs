## 🧩 Grammar: Cohesion in writing

**Coesão** = os mecanismos que **ligam** frases e parágrafos. Um texto C1 usa vários, **sem exagerar nos conectores**.

**1. Referência (para trás):**
- **this / that / these / those** + substantivo: "Many cities have introduced bike lanes. **This policy** has..."
- **such** + substantivo (= desse tipo): "**Such measures** are expensive." / "**Such a policy**..."
- **the former / the latter** (o primeiro / o último de **dois**): "Investing in schools or hospitals? **The former** has long-term benefits; **the latter** saves lives now."
- **it / they / which** (pronomes — cuidado com ambiguidade!)

**2. "Umbrella nouns"** — substantivos gerais que resumem uma ideia anterior:
**this issue / problem / trend / approach / development / situation / view / claim / argument / measure / phenomenon**
"Young people are increasingly leaving rural areas. **This trend** has serious consequences..."

**3. Sinônimos e variação lexical** — evite repetir a mesma palavra:
public transport → the transport network → buses and trains → the system

**4. Substituição e elipse** (revisão C1-M1-L2): do so, one(s), so/not.

**5. Transições entre parágrafos** (ligam ideia anterior + nova):
- "**Turning now to** healthcare, ..."
- "**Another area which deserves attention is**..."
- "**While** improving transport would clearly benefit commuters, **investing in** education might have more far-reaching effects."
- "**Having considered** X, **it is worth examining** Y."

**6. Estrutura do essay C1 Advanced (avaliar 2 de 3 opções):**
1. **Introdução:** apresente o tema e diga quais duas opções vai discutir.
2. **Parágrafo 1:** opção A — vantagens, exemplos, possíveis limitações.
3. **Parágrafo 2:** opção B — idem.
4. **Conclusão:** diga **qual é mais importante/eficaz** e **por quê** (obrigatório!).

⚠️ **Dica:** um conector por frase é muito. Use **referência e vocabulário** para criar coesão — isso é o que distingue C1.

| Recurso | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| this / these + umbrella noun | This trend is worrying. |
| such + noun | Such policies have worked elsewhere. |
| the former / the latter | The former is cheaper; the latter is fairer. |
| Sinônimos | the elderly → older people → senior citizens |
| Transição | Turning now to housing, ... |
| Comparação final | Of the two, the former would be more effective. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L4/example-01.m4a) Several countries have introduced free public transport. Such schemes reduce traffic, but they are expensive to maintain. — _Vários países introduziram transporte público gratuito. Esquemas desse tipo reduzem o trânsito, mas são caros de manter._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L4/example-02.m4a) Governments can either invest in renewable energy or subsidize electric cars. The former tends to have a broader impact, whereas the latter mainly benefits wealthier citizens. — _Os governos podem investir em energia renovável ou subsidiar carros elétricos. A primeira opção tende a ter impacto mais amplo, enquanto a segunda beneficia principalmente os cidadãos mais ricos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L4/example-03.m4a) Youth unemployment has risen sharply in recent years. This development is particularly worrying because it affects long-term earnings. — _O desemprego juvenil aumentou acentuadamente nos últimos anos. Esse fenômeno é particularmente preocupante porque afeta a renda de longo prazo._

> 💡 **Dica:** Use **the former / the latter** apenas quando houver **exatamente dois** itens. Para três ou mais, use **the first / the last** ou repita o substantivo.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Such measure would be expensive.  
  ✅ **Such a measure would be expensive. / Such measures would be expensive.**  
  such + a + substantivo singular contável.
- ❌ There are three options: the latter is the best.  
  ✅ **There are three options: the last is the best.**  
  the former / the latter só para dois itens.
- ❌ Moreover, ... Furthermore, ... Moreover, ... In addition, ...  
  ✅ **Use referência e umbrella nouns: This approach... Such a policy...**  
  Conectores demais prejudicam o texto; varie os recursos de coesão.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: former / latter

**Former** = "FÓR-mer" e **latter** = "LÉ-ter" (no americano, o tt soa quase como um "r" rápido: "LÉ-rer"). Não confunda **latter** (o último) com **later** ("LÊI-ter", mais tarde)!

## 🎧 Listening — What examiners look for

_Uma examinadora explica os critérios de correção do essay C1._

**Listen first, without reading.** [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L4/listening-full.m4a)

**Questions:**
1. What is often the difference between good and excellent essays at C1?
2. What mistake do many candidates make with linking words?
3. What really impresses examiners?
4. What part of the task do candidates often forget?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Examiner:** At C1 level, the difference between a good essay and an excellent one is often not the ideas but the organization. We assess writing in four areas: content, communicative achievement, organization and language. Under organization, we're looking for a text that is well structured and cohesive, using a variety of devices — not just linking words. Many candidates think that the more "furthermore" and "moreover" they use, the higher their score will be. In fact, overusing linking words can make a text feel mechanical. What really impresses examiners is when ideas flow naturally: when a sentence refers back to the previous one using "this problem" or "such an approach", and when paragraphs are connected by a clear line of argument. The other common mistake is forgetting part of the task. In the essay, you must clearly state which option you consider more important, and give reasons. If you don't, your content score will suffer, however good your English is.

</details>

## 📖 Reading — Model essay: Improving life in cities [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L4/reading.m4a)

Over half of the world's population now lives in cities, and this proportion is expected to keep rising. As a result, the question of how to make urban life healthier and more sustainable has become increasingly urgent. Of the various measures available to governments, investment in public transport and the provision of affordable housing are two of the most frequently discussed.  
  
An efficient public transport system can transform daily life. When buses and trains are frequent, safe and affordable, fewer people depend on cars, which in turn reduces congestion, air pollution and carbon emissions. Such a network also widens access to jobs and education, particularly for those living on the outskirts. However, this kind of infrastructure is costly and can take years to build, and its benefits depend heavily on good planning and maintenance.  
  
Affordable housing, by contrast, addresses a more fundamental need. In many cities, soaring rents have pushed low-income families into informal settlements or distant suburbs, where they face long commutes and limited services. Providing decent homes closer to city centres would not only improve living conditions but also reduce the very travel demands that transport investment seeks to meet. Admittedly, such programmes can be poorly managed and may concentrate poverty if they are badly designed.  
  
On balance, although both measures are valuable, I believe the latter should be the priority. Without a secure, affordable place to live, people cannot fully benefit from any other improvement a city has to offer. Moreover, well-located housing reduces the need for long journeys in the first place, making it, in the long term, the more far-reaching solution.

**Questions:**
1. Which two measures does the essay discuss?
2. What benefits of public transport are mentioned?
3. Find two examples of cohesive devices in paragraph 2.
4. Which option does the writer prioritize, and why?

## 📝 Writing

C1 Advanced essay (220–260 words): "Your class has discussed three ways governments could reduce inequality: free university education, a guaranteed minimum income, and investment in early childhood education. Write an essay discussing two of these, explaining which is more effective and why." Use a range of cohesive devices.

**Model:**

> Reducing inequality: where should governments begin?  
>   
> Inequality remains one of the most persistent challenges facing countries such as Brazil. Of the many policies proposed to address this issue, a guaranteed minimum income and investment in early childhood education are among the most widely debated.  
>   
> A guaranteed minimum income offers immediate relief. By ensuring that every household has enough to cover its basic needs, such a scheme can reduce extreme poverty almost overnight. Cash transfer programmes have been credited with lowering poverty rates in several countries, including Brazil, and they allow families to decide for themselves how best to spend the money. Nevertheless, this approach is expensive and, on its own, does little to change the conditions that cause poverty in the first place.  
>   
> Early childhood education, by contrast, targets those underlying conditions. Research suggests that the first years of life are crucial for cognitive and social development, and that children from poorer families who attend high-quality nurseries and pre-schools are more likely to succeed later on. The benefits of this investment, however, take many years to appear, which makes it politically less attractive.  
>   
> On balance, although both measures have clear merits, I believe the latter is ultimately more effective. While a minimum income alleviates the symptoms of inequality, early education addresses one of its root causes, giving children a fairer start regardless of their background. Ideally, the two policies would work together; if only one can be prioritized, however, investing in the youngest citizens is likely to have the most far-reaching impact.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Coesão em inglês x português

Textos acadêmicos em português costumam aceitar frases longas, com muitas orações subordinadas. Em inglês, a tradição valoriza frases mais **curtas e diretas**, ligadas por **referência** (this, such, these) em vez de conectores longos. Uma dica prática: se sua frase em inglês tem mais de 35 palavras, provavelmente vale dividir em duas.
