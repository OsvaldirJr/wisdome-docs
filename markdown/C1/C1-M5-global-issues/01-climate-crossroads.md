## 🎯 Nesta aula você vai
- Usar be set to / be poised to / be expected to para previsões jornalísticas
- Usar be on the verge / point / brink of para algo iminente

## 🧩 Grammar: Formal future forms

**1. Previsões (jornalismo, relatórios):**
- **be set to** + verbo: muito provável, já encaminhado — "Renewables **are set to** overtake coal."
- **be expected / likely / predicted to**: "Temperatures **are expected to** rise by..."
- **be poised to**: pronto para agir, prestes a — "The country **is poised to** become a leader in green hydrogen."
- **be bound to**: certeza (revisão C1-M2)

**2. Iminência:**
- **be about to** + verbo: prestes a (muito em breve) — "The meeting **is about to** start."
- **be on the verge / point / brink of** + substantivo ou -ing: à beira de
  "on the **verge of** a breakthrough" / "on the **point of** leaving" / "on the **brink of** extinction" (brink = algo negativo, catastrófico)

**3. Horários e cronogramas:**
- **be due to** + verbo / **be due** + tempo: previsto para — "The report **is due to** be published in May." / "The train **is due** at 5."

**4. be to + verbo** (formal): planos oficiais, ordens, instruções, destino
- Plano oficial: "The summit **is to be held** in Belém." / "The president **is to visit** the Amazon."
- Ordem/instrução: "You **are to report** to the director immediately." / "All staff **are not to** leave the building."
- Em condicionais: "If we **are to** meet our targets, we must act now." (= se quisermos)
- Passado (destino): "He **was** never **to** see his homeland again." (nunca mais veria)

**5. Futuro no passado** (revisão): was going to / was about to / was to have + pp (plano frustrado: "The bridge **was to have opened** in 2020.")

**Manchetes:** omitem o **be**: "Emissions **set to** fall" / "PM **to** resign" / "Species **on brink of** extinction".

| Forma | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| be set to | muito provável | Prices are set to rise. |
| be poised to | pronto para | Brazil is poised to lead. |
| be about to | prestes a | The film is about to start. |
| be on the verge/brink of | à beira de | on the brink of collapse |
| be due to | previsto para | The plane is due to land at 8. |
| be to + verbo | plano oficial / ordem | The minister is to resign. |
| If... be to | se quisermos | If we are to succeed, ... |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L1/example-01.m4a) Solar energy is set to become one of the main sources of electricity in Brazil within the next decade. — _A energia solar deve se tornar uma das principais fontes de eletricidade do Brasil na próxima década._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L1/example-02.m4a) Scientists warn that parts of the Amazon could be approaching a tipping point, beyond which the forest might turn into savannah. — _Cientistas alertam que partes da Amazônia podem estar se aproximando de um ponto de não retorno, além do qual a floresta poderia virar savana._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L1/example-03.m4a) If the world is to limit warming to 1.5°C, emissions will need to fall dramatically this decade. — _Se o mundo quiser limitar o aquecimento a 1,5°C, as emissões precisarão cair drasticamente nesta década._

> 💡 **Dica:** **If we are to...** é uma estrutura poderosa em redações: "**If** Brazil **is to** meet its climate goals, deforestation must end." Significa "**se quisermos / para que**" — e mostra domínio C1.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Prices are set to rising.  
  ✅ **Prices are set to rise.**  
  be set to + verbo base.
- ❌ The species is on the verge to disappear.  
  ✅ **The species is on the verge of disappearing.**  
  on the verge of + -ing ou substantivo.
- ❌ The report is due to publish next week.  
  ✅ **The report is due to be published next week.**  
  O relatório é publicado (passiva): due to be published.

## 📖 Reading — The Amazon at a tipping point? [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L1/reading.m4a)

For decades, scientists have warned that the Amazon rainforest may be approaching a critical threshold. The concept is relatively simple. The forest generates a large share of its own rainfall: trees release moisture into the air, which forms clouds that carry rain further inland. As deforestation and climate change reduce the number of trees and increase temperatures, this cycle weakens. Beyond a certain point — a "tipping point" — parts of the forest could be unable to sustain themselves and might gradually turn into a drier, savannah-like ecosystem. Estimates of where exactly that point lies vary. In widely cited work, the Brazilian climate scientist Carlos Nobre and the American biologist Thomas Lovejoy suggested that it might be reached if deforestation exceeded around 20 to 25 percent of the original forest. Some studies indicate that parts of the eastern Amazon are already showing signs of stress, with longer dry seasons and, in certain areas, the forest releasing more carbon than it absorbs. Not all researchers agree on how close the tipping point is, and the forest is likely to respond differently from region to region. What is broadly accepted, however, is that the consequences of large-scale dieback would extend far beyond the Amazon itself. Rainfall in other parts of South America, including Brazil's agricultural heartland, depends partly on moisture from the forest. There are reasons for cautious hope. Deforestation rates have fallen significantly in some recent years, demonstrating that policy and enforcement can make a difference. Restoration projects are also under way. But if the Amazon is to remain a rainforest, many scientists argue, the next two decades are likely to be decisive.

**Questions:**
1. How does the Amazon generate its own rainfall?
2. What might happen beyond the tipping point?
3. What threshold did Nobre and Lovejoy suggest?
4. Why would the consequences extend beyond the Amazon?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — A COP30 em Belém

Em novembro de 2025, Belém do Pará sediou a **COP30**, a conferência do clima da ONU — a primeira realizada na Amazônia. Em inglês, **COP** significa **Conference of the Parties**. Termos comuns na cobertura: **climate finance**, **loss and damage**, **nationally determined contributions (NDCs)** e **just transition**.
