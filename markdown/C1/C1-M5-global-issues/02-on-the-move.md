## 🧩 Grammar: Multi-word verbs with abstract meanings

Muitos phrasal verbs têm um **sentido literal** (B1/B2) e um **sentido abstrato** (C1), que aparece em textos formais.

**1. Causa e origem:**
- **bring about** = causar, provocar (mudança): "The war **brought about** mass migration."
- **stem from** = originar-se de: "Many problems **stem from** a lack of documents."
- **give rise to** = dar origem a: "Climate change **gives rise to** new forms of displacement."
- **lead to / result in** (revisão)

**2. Explicar e quantificar:**
- **account for** = representar (proporção) / explicar: "Women **account for** half of all migrants." / "How do you **account for** the increase?"
- **bear out** = confirmar: "The data **bear out** this theory."
- **point to** = indicar: "Studies **point to** economic factors."

**3. Enfrentar e lidar:**
- **come up against** = deparar-se com (obstáculo)
- **face up to** = encarar (realidade difícil)
- **grapple / contend with** = lutar com, lidar com
- **cope with / deal with** (revisão)

**4. Começar, propor-se, eliminar:**
- **set out to** = propor-se a / **set up** = criar, estabelecer
- **embark on** = embarcar em, começar (projeto, jornada)
- **do away with** = abolir / **phase out** = eliminar gradualmente
- **draw up** = elaborar (plano, documento) / **carry out** = realizar

**5. Outros úteis:**
- **fall through** = fracassar (plano) · **put forward** = propor (ideia) · **rule out** = descartar · **look into** = investigar · **bring up** = mencionar · **step up** = intensificar

**Registro:** muitos phrasal verbs têm equivalentes latinos mais formais:
bring about = **cause** · set up = **establish** · look into = **investigate** · do away with = **abolish** · put forward = **propose** · rule out = **exclude**
Em redações C1, uma **mistura** dos dois soa natural; só latinos soa pesado.

**Gramática:** verbos de três partes são **inseparáveis**: "face up **to** it" (não "face it up to").

| Phrasal verb | Equivalente formal | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| bring about | cause | The reform brought about change. |
| stem from | originate in | It stems from poverty. |
| account for | represent / explain | They account for 10%. |
| come up against | encounter | They came up against prejudice. |
| face up to | confront | We must face up to the facts. |
| do away with | abolish | They did away with the visa. |
| set up | establish | She set up an NGO. |
| rule out | exclude | We can't rule it out. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/example-01.m4a) Much of the hostility towards migrants stems from misinformation about their impact on jobs and public services. — _Grande parte da hostilidade contra migrantes vem da desinformação sobre seu impacto nos empregos e serviços públicos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/example-02.m4a) Many newly arrived refugees come up against language barriers, bureaucracy and, at times, open prejudice. — _Muitos refugiados recém-chegados esbarram em barreiras linguísticas, burocracia e, às vezes, preconceito aberto._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/example-03.m4a) Remittances account for a significant share of the economy in several Latin American countries. — _As remessas representam uma parcela significativa da economia em vários países latino-americanos._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Account for** é muito útil e muito confundido: "Migrants **account for** 10% of the population" (= representam) / "This **accounts for** the rise" (= explica). Não confunda com "count" ou "represent for".

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Migrants account 10% of the workforce.  
  ✅ **Migrants account for 10% of the workforce.**  
  account FOR.
- ❌ The problem stems in poverty.  
  ✅ **The problem stems from poverty.**  
  stem FROM.
- ❌ We must face the problem up to.  
  ✅ **We must face up to the problem.**  
  Verbos de três partes não se separam.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Acento em phrasal verbs

Em phrasal verbs, o acento costuma cair na **partícula**: bring a**BOUT**, set **UP**, rule **OUT**. Em verbos de três partes, acento na **segunda** palavra: come up a**GAINST**? Não — come **UP** against, face **UP** to, do a**WAY** with.

- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/pron-01.m4a) The war brought aBOUT change.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/pron-02.m4a) She set UP an organization.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/pron-03.m4a) We must face UP to it.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/pron-04.m4a) They did aWAY with the rule.

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Historian:** Brazil has been shaped by migration — much of it forced. Over three centuries, an estimated four to five million enslaved Africans were brought to Brazil, more than to any other country in the Americas. Their descendants account for a large part of today's population, and African influences run through Brazilian culture, from music and religion to food and language. From the late nineteenth century, a new wave of immigration began, much of it brought about by government policies to attract European labour after the abolition of slavery in 1888. Italians, Portuguese, Spaniards and Germans arrived in large numbers, followed by Japanese immigrants from 1908 — today, Brazil is home to the largest population of Japanese descent outside Japan. Lebanese and Syrian communities also set down deep roots. In recent decades, the picture has changed again: Brazil has received people from Haiti, Venezuela, Bolivia and African countries, while hundreds of thousands of Brazilians have themselves emigrated, to places such as the United States, Portugal and Japan.

## 📖 Reading — The economics of migration [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M5-L2/reading.m4a)

Few issues provoke as much political debate as migration, and few are as widely misunderstood. Public opinion surveys in many countries suggest that people tend to overestimate the number of immigrants living among them, sometimes considerably. Much of the anxiety surrounding migration stems from economic fears: that newcomers will take jobs, push down wages or place an unbearable burden on public services. What does the evidence say? On the question of jobs, most economic research has failed to bear out the most pessimistic predictions. Migrants often take up work that local workers are unwilling or unavailable to do, or set up businesses that create new employment. Their effect on average wages is generally found to be small, although some studies point to modest negative effects for specific groups of low-skilled workers, particularly in the short term. Migrants also tend to be of working age, which means that, in ageing societies, they can help to pay for pensions and healthcare. At the same time, it would be naive to rule out real challenges. Rapid arrivals can put pressure on housing, schools and health services in particular areas, especially when governments fail to plan or invest accordingly. Integration does not happen automatically: newcomers frequently come up against language barriers, unrecognized qualifications and discrimination, all of which can prevent them from contributing fully. Emigration, meanwhile, has its own consequences for countries of origin. The "brain drain" of doctors, engineers and other professionals can weaken health and education systems, although remittances sent home by migrants account for a substantial share of income in many developing economies. Ultimately, the evidence suggests that migration is neither the catastrophe some fear nor the simple solution others claim. Its effects depend largely on how societies choose to face up to it.

**Questions:**
1. What do people tend to overestimate?
2. What does most research say about migrants and jobs?
3. What real challenges does the text mention?
4. What are the consequences for countries of origin?

## 📝 Writing

Write a formal proposal (280–320 words) to your city council suggesting a programme to help migrants and refugees integrate. Describe the problems they face, your proposal and its expected benefits. Use at least eight multi-word verbs.

**Model:**

> Proposal: The "Welcome Neighbour" integration programme  
>   
> Introduction  
> The aim of this proposal is to put forward a programme to help the growing number of migrants and refugees in our city to integrate more quickly and fully into local life. Over the past five years, newcomers from Venezuela, Haiti and several African countries have come to account for a visible share of our population, yet support services remain limited.  
>   
> Current situation  
> Interviews carried out by our association with more than sixty migrant families suggest that most of their difficulties stem from three sources: language barriers, the slow recognition of professional qualifications and a lack of information about public services. Many highly qualified people, including nurses and engineers, come up against so much bureaucracy that they end up in low-paid, informal work. This represents a waste of talent that the city cannot afford.  
>   
> Proposal  
> We propose that the council set up a "Welcome Neighbour" centre, run in partnership with local NGOs and universities. The centre would:  
> • offer free Portuguese classes at different levels, including evening and weekend options;  
> • help newcomers draw up the documents needed to have their qualifications recognized;  
> • pair each family with a local volunteer "neighbour" for their first six months;  
> • organize regular cultural events to bring migrant and local communities together.  
>   
> Expected benefits  
> The programme is likely to bring about faster integration into the job market, which would in turn increase tax revenue and reduce dependence on social support. Perhaps more importantly, it would help to do away with the misunderstandings and prejudice that often arise when communities live side by side without meeting.  
>   
> Conclusion  
> We are aware that the council is facing budget constraints. However, the costs of the programme would be modest, and we believe the city cannot afford to ignore the issue. We would welcome the opportunity to present the proposal in more detail.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — A Operação Acolhida

Desde 2018, a **Operação Acolhida** recebe migrantes e refugiados venezuelanos na fronteira de Roraima e promove a **interiorização** — a realocação voluntária para outras cidades brasileiras com mais oportunidades. O programa tem sido citado internacionalmente como exemplo de resposta humanitária, embora enfrente desafios de financiamento e integração.
