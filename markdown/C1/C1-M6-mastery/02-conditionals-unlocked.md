# Conditionals unlocked
**C1-M6-L2 · Mastery** — Condicionais avançados: Should you..., Were we to..., Had it not been for..., if + will/would, assuming

## 🎯 Nesta aula você vai
- Usar condicionais invertidos (Should..., Were... to, Had...)
- Usar if + will / would para pedidos educados e resultados
- Usar alternativas: assuming, given that, in the event of, but for, on condition that
- Avaliar riscos e tomar decisões em contextos profissionais

Você já conhece todos os condicionais e a inversão "Had I known...". No C1, o repertório se completa: "**Should** you need help...", "**Were** we **to** lose this client...", "**If you'd** like to follow me...", "**Assuming** the budget is approved...". Tema: **riscos e decisões**.

- What's the biggest risk you've ever taken?
- Do you make decisions quickly or do you think through every possible scenario?

## 🧩 Grammar: Advanced conditionals

**1. Condicionais invertidos** (formais, sem if):
- **Should + S + verbo** (= if... should / if, possibilidade remota — 1º condicional): "**Should** the flight **be** delayed, we will inform you."
- **Were + S + to + verbo** (= if... were to — 2º condicional, hipótese): "**Were** the company **to** close, 500 people would lose their jobs."
- **Were + S + noun/adj** (com be): "**Were** I in your position, I'd accept."
- **Had + S + particípio** (3º condicional): "**Had** we **known**, we would have acted."
- **Negativas:** "**Should** you **not** wish to continue..." / "**Had** it **not been** for..." (nunca "Hadn't it...")

**2. If + will / would** (exceções!):
- **Pedidos educados:** "**If you'll** follow me, I'll show you to your seat." / "**If you would** sign here, please."
- **Resultado na oração do if:** "I'll give you £10 **if it'll** help." (= se isso for ajudar)
- **Insistência/teimosia:** "**If you will** eat so much sugar, of course you'll feel ill!" (will acentuado)

**3. If + should / happen to** (possibilidade remota):
- "**If** you **should** see Ana, tell her to call me." / "**If** you **happen to** pass the bakery..."

**4. Alternativas formais ao if:**
- **Assuming (that) / Supposing (that)**: "**Assuming** the weather is good, we'll go."
- **Given that** (= considerando que): "**Given that** costs are rising, we must act."
- **In the event of** + substantivo / **in the event that**: "**In the event of** fire, use the stairs."
- **On condition that / Provided (that) / As long as** (revisão)
- **But for** + substantivo (= se não fosse por): "**But for** her help, I'd have failed."
- **Otherwise / Or else** (revisão B2)
- **If so / If not** (revisão C1-M1)

**5. Mixed e implícitos:**
- "I'd have called you, **but** I lost my phone." (condição implícita)
- "**Without** your support, this **wouldn't have been** possible."

| Estrutura | Equivale a | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Should + S + V | If + S + should/V | Should you need help, call me. |
| Were + S + to + V | If + S + were to | Were prices to rise, sales would fall. |
| Were + S + ... | If + S + were | Were I you, I'd wait. |
| Had + S + pp | If + S + had + pp | Had I known, I'd have come. |
| If you'll / would... | pedido educado | If you'll take a seat... |
| Assuming / Given that | supondo / dado que | Given that it's late, let's stop. |
| In the event of | em caso de | In the event of rain, ... |

- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L2/example-01.m4a) Should you have any questions about your booking, please do not hesitate to contact us. — _Caso tenha qualquer dúvida sobre sua reserva, não hesite em nos contatar._

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Hadn't it been for you, I would have failed.  
  ✅ **Had it not been for you, I would have failed.**  
  Na inversão, não use contração: Had it not been...
- ❌ Should you will need help, call me.  
  ✅ **Should you need help, call me.**  
  Should + verbo base.
- ❌ Were we lose the client, we'd be in trouble.  
  ✅ **Were we to lose the client, we'd be in trouble.**  
  Were + sujeito + to + verbo.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Inversão e ênfase

Em condicionais invertidos, o auxiliar inicial (**Should / Were / Had**) é pronunciado de forma **clara e forte**, e há uma pausa no fim da condição: "**SHOULD** you need any help, | please call." Em "If you **WILL** eat so much..." (teimosia), o will é **muito acentuado**.

- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L2/pron-01.m4a) SHOULD you need any help, | please call.

## 🎧 Listening — Pre-mortem: imagine it failed

_Uma consultora explica uma técnica para avaliar riscos antes de tomar decisões._

**Listen first, without reading.** [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L2/listening-full.m4a)

**Questions:**
1. What is the problem with asking "What could go wrong?"
2. What happens in a pre-mortem?
3. What is "prospective hindsight"?
4. What does the technique cost?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Consultant:** Most teams evaluate risks by asking, "What could go wrong?" The problem is that, once a team is excited about a project, few people want to be seen as negative. The psychologist Gary Klein proposed a clever alternative called the "pre-mortem". Before making a final decision, the team imagines that it's a year later and the project has failed completely. Then each person writes down, independently, the reasons why it failed. Research on what's called "prospective hindsight" suggests that imagining that an event has already happened can make it easier to identify its causes. So instead of "Were we to fail, what might the reasons be?", you say, "We failed. Why?" People suddenly feel free to mention concerns they'd otherwise keep to themselves. Had more organizations used this technique, many expensive disasters might have been avoided. And it costs nothing but an hour of honest conversation.

</details>

## 📖 Reading — Thinking in bets [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L2/reading.m4a)

In her book Thinking in Bets, the former professional poker player Annie Duke argues that most of us make a fundamental mistake when judging our decisions: we evaluate them by their outcomes. Poker players call this "resulting". If a decision turns out well, we assume it was a good one; if it turns out badly, we conclude it was a mistake. But, as Duke points out, the quality of a decision and the quality of its outcome are not the same thing. Imagine you drive home after drinking too much and arrive safely. Was it a good decision? Clearly not. Had you crashed, nobody would have questioned that conclusion; the fact that you were lucky does not change it. Conversely, a well-reasoned business decision might fail for reasons nobody could have foreseen. Were we to judge every choice purely by what happened afterwards, we would learn the wrong lessons from both. The alternative, Duke suggests, is to think of decisions as bets on an uncertain future. Before deciding, ask: what are the possible outcomes, how likely is each, and what would each cost or gain me? Assuming you have made a reasonable estimate with the information available, you have made a good decision — whatever happens next. This way of thinking has practical benefits. It encourages people to seek out information that might prove them wrong, to admit uncertainty ("I'm about 70 percent sure") and to review their decisions more honestly. Should a plan fail, the question becomes not "Who is to blame?" but "Was this bad luck, bad information or bad judgement?" Given that almost every important decision in life involves uncertainty, that may be one of the most useful questions we can learn to ask.

**Questions:**
1. What is "resulting"?
2. What does the drink-driving example show?
3. What questions should you ask before deciding, according to Duke?
4. What question should you ask if a plan fails?

## 🎤 Speaking

"Pre-mortem": in groups, choose a decision (moving abroad, starting a business, changing careers, launching a product). Imagine it has failed. Each person explains why, using inverted and advanced conditionals. Then suggest how to reduce each risk.

1. Are you risk-averse or a risk-taker?
2. What's a calculated risk you've taken that paid off?
3. Should you judge a decision by its result?
4. Were you to start a business tomorrow, what would it be?
5. Had you known at 18 what you know now, what would you have done differently?

## 📝 Writing

Write a formal risk assessment report (280–320 words) for a decision in your company or community (an event, a new service, an investment). Identify at least three risks and suggest measures. Use at least six advanced conditional structures.

**Model:**

> Risk assessment: Open-air music festival, Parque das Águas  
>   
> Introduction  
> The purpose of this report is to identify the main risks associated with the proposed one-day music festival in Parque das Águas in October, and to recommend measures to reduce them. Assuming the council approves the plan, the event is expected to attract around 8,000 visitors.  
>   
> Weather  
> Given that October is the beginning of the rainy season, heavy rain is a significant risk. Were a storm to occur during the event, the main stage and electrical equipment could be damaged and visitors put at risk. It is therefore recommended that a covered stage be hired and that the weather forecast be reviewed daily in the week before the festival. In the event of severe weather warnings, the event should be postponed to the reserve date in November.  
>   
> Crowd safety  
> The park has only two main entrances. Should a large number of visitors arrive at the same time, there is a serious risk of overcrowding. We recommend opening a third, temporary entrance and introducing staggered entry times for ticket holders.  
>   
> Financial risk  
> The budget depends heavily on ticket sales. Were sales to fall below 5,000, the organizers would make a loss. To reduce this risk, we suggest securing at least two local sponsors before tickets go on sale. Had a similar measure been taken last year, the previous festival would not have ended with a deficit.  
>   
> Conclusion  
> Provided that the measures outlined above are implemented, the risks associated with the festival can be kept at an acceptable level. Should the council require any further information, the organizing committee would be happy to provide it.

- Assuming, given that, in the event of, but for, provided that.
