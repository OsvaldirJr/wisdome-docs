## 🧩 Grammar: Register: formal, neutral and informal

**Marcas de registro formal:**
- Vocabulário de origem **latina**: obtain, require, commence, assist, inform, purchase, sufficient, regarding
- **Passiva** e **impessoalidade**: "It has been decided that..." / "Payment is required..."
- **Nominalização**: "the cancellation of the event" (em vez de "we cancelled the event")
- **Sem contrações**: I am, we will, do not
- **Condicionais invertidos e subjuntivo**: Should you require..., It is essential that he be...
- Frases mais **longas e complexas**

**Marcas de registro informal:**
- **Phrasal verbs** e vocabulário anglo-saxão: get, start, help, find out, put off, sort out
- **Contrações**: I'm, we'll, don't
- **Elipse**: "(Are you) Coming tonight?" / "(I) Hope you're well."
- **Discourse markers e vague language**: anyway, by the way, and stuff, -ish
- **Perguntas diretas e exclamações**: "Guess what!" / "How cool is that?"
- Palavras como **guys, stuff, loads of, a bit, pretty (= bastante)**

**Pares formal ↔ informal:**
- request ↔ ask for · receive ↔ get · inform ↔ tell · investigate ↔ look into · tolerate ↔ put up with · postpone ↔ put off · discover ↔ find out · establish ↔ set up · reduce ↔ cut down on · contact ↔ get in touch with · however ↔ but · therefore ↔ so · in addition ↔ also / plus

**Abertura e fechamento de e-mails:**
- **Formal:** Dear Mr./Ms. Smith / Dear Sir or Madam → Yours sincerely (nome conhecido) / Yours faithfully (nome desconhecido, britânico) / Kind regards
- **Neutro:** Dear Ana / Hello Ana → Best regards / Best wishes
- **Informal:** Hi Ana / Hey! → Cheers / Take care / Love / See you soon

⚠️ **Formal demais também é erro:** num e-mail para um colega próximo, "I am writing to enquire whether you would be available for lunch" soa estranho ou irônico!

| Formal | Neutro | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| I am writing to enquire about... | I'd like to ask about... | Quick question about... |
| We regret to inform you that... | Unfortunately, ... | Sorry, but... |
| Please find attached... | I've attached... | Here's the... |
| Should you require further assistance... | If you need more help... | Shout if you need anything! |
| I look forward to hearing from you. | I hope to hear from you soon. | Speak soon! |
| Yours sincerely / faithfully | Best regards | Cheers / Take care |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L3/example-01.m4a) We regret to inform you that, owing to unforeseen circumstances, the conference has been postponed. — _Lamentamos informar que, devido a circunstâncias imprevistas, a conferência foi adiada._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L3/example-02.m4a) Bad news, I'm afraid — they've put off the conference. No idea when it'll happen now. — _Má notícia — adiaram a conferência. Não faço ideia de quando vai ser agora._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L3/example-03.m4a) Should you require any further information, please do not hesitate to contact me. — _Caso precise de mais informações, não hesite em me contatar._

> 💡 **Dica:** Brasileiros costumam ser **calorosos** em e-mails formais ("Espero que esteja tudo bem com você e sua família!"). Em inglês formal, uma frase curta basta: "**I hope this email finds you well.**" — e vá direto ao ponto.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Dear Mr. Smith, I'm writing coz I wanna know about the job.  
  ✅ **Dear Mr. Smith, I am writing to enquire about the position.**  
  Mistura de registros: e-mail formal pede vocabulário e gramática formais.
- ❌ Dear Sir or Madam, ... Yours sincerely.  
  ✅ **Dear Sir or Madam, ... Yours faithfully.**  
  No britânico: nome desconhecido → Yours faithfully.
- ❌ Hey Ana! I hereby inform you that I can't come tonight.  
  ✅ **Hey Ana! Sorry, I can't make it tonight.**  
  Mensagem informal não combina com linguagem formal.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Registro na fala

Na fala, o registro muda o **ritmo e as reduções**: no informal, "going to" vira "**gonna**", "want to" → "**wanna**", "got to" → "**gotta**", "don't know" → "**dunno**". No formal, pronuncie as palavras completas e com mais clareza. Nunca escreva gonna/wanna em textos formais!

- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L3/pron-01.m4a) (informal) I'm gonna call her later.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L3/pron-02.m4a) (formal) I am going to contact her later this afternoon.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L3/pron-03.m4a) (informal) Dunno, wanna grab a coffee?

## 📖 Reading — Why register matters [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L3/reading.m4a)

Linguists use the term "register" to describe the way language varies according to its context: who is speaking to whom, about what, and through which medium. We all switch registers constantly, usually without thinking. The same person might say "Could you possibly pass the salt?" at a formal dinner, "Pass the salt, please" at home and simply "Salt?" to a close friend. For language learners, however, register can be a hidden trap. Textbooks often teach a fairly neutral style, while films and social media expose learners to very informal speech. The result can be an awkward mixture: an email to a university professor that begins "Hey!" or a casual chat sprinkled with expressions like "furthermore" and "I hereby". Neither is grammatically wrong, but both send an unintended social message. English has a particular feature that makes register especially noticeable: its double vocabulary. After the Norman Conquest of 1066, French became the language of the ruling class in England for centuries, while English continued to be spoken by ordinary people. As a result, English today often has two words for the same idea — a shorter, everyday word of Germanic origin and a longer, more formal one from French or Latin: help and assist, buy and purchase, ask and enquire, begin and commence. A famous illustration involves animals and food. The farm animals kept by English-speaking peasants have Germanic names — cow, pig, sheep — while the meat served at aristocratic tables took French-derived names: beef, pork, mutton. Mastering register, then, is not about learning to be formal. It is about choosing the right words for the right situation. A C1 speaker is not someone who always sounds sophisticated, but someone who knows when not to.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Sincerely ou faithfully?

No inglês **britânico** tradicional: **Dear Sir or Madam → Yours faithfully**; **Dear Mr. Smith → Yours sincerely**. No **americano**, usa-se **Sincerely** em ambos os casos. Hoje, em e-mails profissionais, **Kind regards** e **Best regards** são aceitos quase universalmente — e são uma escolha segura.

## ✅ Resumo
- Formal: latino, passiva, nominalização, sem contrações, frases longas.
- Informal: phrasal verbs, contrações, elipse, discourse markers.
- Pares: postpone/put off, tolerate/put up with, discover/find out.
- Formal demais também é erro: adapte ao destinatário.
