## 🔥 Warm-up
"Sua proposta está errada" ou "**I'm not entirely sure** this proposal **would** work"? Em inglês profissional, especialmente britânico, **dizer "não" diretamente** pode soar agressivo. A linguagem diplomática permite **discordar, criticar e negociar** mantendo o respeito. É uma habilidade-chave do C1.

- How do you disagree with a boss or a client without causing problems?
- Do you think Brazilians are direct or indirect when they disagree?

## 🧩 Grammar: Diplomatic language and softening

**1. Qualificadores e advérbios suavizadores:**
- **not entirely / not quite / not really / not particularly**: "I'm **not entirely** happy with this." (= não estou feliz)
- **slightly / a bit / somewhat / rather**: "The price is **slightly** higher than we expected."
- **a little / a few**: "There are **a few** issues we need to discuss."

**2. Modais e condicionais** (tornam afirmações hipotéticas e menos impositivas):
- "That **would** be difficult." (= é difícil/impossível)
- "We **might** need to rethink this." / "It **could** be a problem."
- "**It might be worth** + -ing": "It might be worth checking the figures again."

**3. Passado e contínuo de distanciamento** (revisão C1-M1-L1):
- "I **was wondering** if..." / "We **were hoping** for a lower price." / "I **was thinking** we could..."

**4. Perguntas negativas** (sugerir sem impor):
- "**Wouldn't it be** better to wait?" / "**Shouldn't we** check with the client first?" / "**Isn't** there a risk that...?"

**5. Understatement e negação do oposto:**
- "That's **not ideal**." (= é ruim) / "It's **not the best** solution." / "I'm **not a huge fan of**..."
- Negue o positivo em vez de afirmar o negativo: "It's **not very** clear" (em vez de "It's confusing").

**6. Passiva e impessoalidade** (tirar o foco da pessoa):
- "**You made** a mistake" → "**A mistake seems to have been made**" / "**There seems to be** an error in..."

**7. Frases de transição e preparação:**
- "**I see your point, but**..." / "**With respect**, ..." / "**I take your point. However**, ..." / "**To be honest**, ..." / "**I'm afraid** that..."

⚠️ **Não exagere:** diplomacia demais pode soar falsa ou confusa. Em culturas diretas (Holanda, Alemanha, Israel), seja mais claro.

| Direto | Diplomático |
|---|---|
| Your idea is bad. | I'm not entirely convinced it would work. |
| It's too expensive. | It's slightly above our budget. |
| We should wait. | Wouldn't it be better to wait? |
| You made a mistake. | There seems to be a small error here. |
| I disagree. | I see your point, but I'm not sure I agree. |
| No. | That might be difficult, I'm afraid. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L1/example-01.m4a) I'm afraid the proposal isn't quite what we were looking for. It might be worth focusing more on the Brazilian market. — _Receio que a proposta não seja bem o que estávamos procurando. Talvez valha a pena focar mais no mercado brasileiro._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L1/example-02.m4a) Wouldn't it make more sense to test the product with a small group before launching it nationally? — _Não faria mais sentido testar o produto com um grupo pequeno antes de lançá-lo nacionalmente?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L1/example-03.m4a) There seem to be a few discrepancies in the figures. Could we go through them together? — _Parece haver algumas discrepâncias nos números. Poderíamos revisá-los juntos?_

> 💡 **Dica:** Atenção ao "**decoder**" britânico: "**With the greatest respect**..." = acho que você está errado. "**That's an interesting idea**" = não gostei. "**I'll bear it in mind**" = não vou fazer nada. "**Quite good**" = meio fraco. O tom e o contexto decidem!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Your report is wrong.  
  ✅ **There seem to be a few inaccuracies in the report.**  
  Use impessoalidade para criticar sem atacar.
- ❌ It might be worth to check.  
  ✅ **It might be worth checking.**  
  worth + -ing.
- ❌ Wouldn't be better to wait?  
  ✅ **Wouldn't it be better to wait?**  
  Não esqueça o it na pergunta negativa.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Tom diplomático

A diplomacia está tanto nas palavras quanto no **tom**. Use uma voz **mais suave**, ritmo **mais lento** e entonação **ascendente** em sugestões: "Wouldn't it be better to wait ↗?". Uma pequena pausa antes da crítica ("I'm afraid... | that might be difficult") também ajuda.

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Expert:** In 1981, Roger Fisher and William Ury of the Harvard Negotiation Project published Getting to Yes, a book that became one of the most influential guides to negotiation ever written. Their central idea was to move away from what they called "positional bargaining", where each side digs in and defends a fixed position, towards "principled negotiation". This rests on a few key principles. First, separate the people from the problem: you can be tough on the issue while remaining respectful to the person. Second, focus on interests, not positions. Two people might both demand the same orange — but if one wants the juice and the other wants the peel for a cake, there's a solution that satisfies both. Third, invent options for mutual gain before deciding. And fourth, insist on objective criteria, such as market prices or expert opinion, rather than a contest of wills. Diplomatic language is essential here. It allows you to challenge ideas without attacking people — which is often what makes agreement possible.

## 📖 Reading — Lost in diplomacy [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M6-L1/reading.m4a)

Every culture has its own rules about how directly people should express disagreement, and misunderstandings between cultures can be costly. In her influential book The Culture Map, Erin Meyer, a professor at the business school INSEAD, describes a scale ranging from cultures where negative feedback is delivered directly to those where it is delivered indirectly. In the Netherlands, Israel and Germany, she argues, people tend to give criticism frankly, often using "upgraders" such as "absolutely" or "totally": "This is totally inappropriate." In the United Kingdom, Japan and much of Southeast Asia, by contrast, criticism is typically softened with "downgraders": "This might be slightly inappropriate." The problem arises when the two styles meet. A Dutch manager who tells a British colleague that his presentation was "a bit weak" may think she is being helpful; the British colleague may hear something far more devastating, because in his culture "a bit weak" is how one might describe a disaster. Conversely, a British manager who describes a project as "not quite what we were hoping for" may be shocked to discover that a Dutch employee interpreted this as minor feedback. Brazil occupies an interesting position. Brazilians are often described as expressive and warm, and comfortable showing emotion. Yet when it comes to negative feedback, especially towards superiors or in public, many Brazilians tend to soften their message considerably — sometimes so much that the criticism is missed entirely. The lesson is not that one style is superior. It is that effective communicators learn to recognize the code being used and, when necessary, check their understanding. A simple question — "Just to make sure I understand, are you saying that we need to redo this?" — can prevent a great deal of confusion.

>

>

Many of our guests are from the creative industries, and a more informal space — such as a converted warehouse — might create a more suitable atmosphere. Would it be possible to suggest one or two alternative venues?  
>

>   
> Thank you once again, and I look forward to hearing from you.  
>   
> Kind regards,  
>   
> Camila Rocha

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — O "jeitinho" da diplomacia brasileira

Brasileiros costumam evitar o "não" direto — "vou ver", "vamos marcar", "pode ser" muitas vezes significam "não". Estrangeiros podem achar isso confuso! Em inglês, é bom equilibrar: seja diplomático no tom, mas **claro na mensagem**. "I'm afraid we won't be able to do that" é educado **e** inequívoco.
