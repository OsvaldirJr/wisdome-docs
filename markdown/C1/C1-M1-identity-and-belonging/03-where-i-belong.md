## 🧩 Grammar: Emphatic structures

**1. do / does / did enfático** (em frases afirmativas) — reforça, contrasta ou corrige:
- "I **do** like your new haircut!" (gosto mesmo)
- "You never listen to me!" — "I **do** listen!" (contradizer)
- "She **did** warn us, to be fair." (admitir)
- No imperativo, soa insistente e caloroso: "**Do** come in!" / "**Do** sit down."
- Na fala, o **do** é sempre **acentuado**.

**2. Fronting** — mover um elemento para o início para dar destaque:
- **Adjetivo/advérbio + as + sujeito + verbo** (concessão = "por mais... que"):
  "**Strange as it may seem**, ..." / "**Tired as she was**, she kept going." / "**Hard as I tried**, I couldn't settle in."
- **Much as** + oração (= although, "por mais que"): "**Much as I love** my hometown, I couldn't live there again."
- Objeto no início (estilo literário/falado): "**That** I don't understand." / "**Some things** you never forget."

**3. so / such... that**
- **so + adjetivo/advérbio + that**: "The city was **so** noisy **that** I couldn't sleep."
- **such + (a/an) + adjetivo + substantivo + that**: "It was **such a** warm welcome **that** I cried."
- **so much / many / little / few + substantivo**: "There were **so many** people **that**..."

**4. Inversão com so / such** (formal/literário):
- "**So** strong **was** the feeling **that** I cried."
- "**Such was** her homesickness **that** she flew back after a month."

| Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| do / did + verbo | I do miss Recife. |
| adjetivo + as + S + V | Small as it is, our flat feels like home. |
| Much as + oração | Much as I love her, she drives me mad. |
| so + adj + that | It was so cold that we went home. |
| such + (a) adj + noun + that | It was such a long trip that I slept for two days. |
| So + adj + V + S + that | So great was the noise that nobody heard. |
| Such + be + noun + that | Such was the demand that tickets sold out. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M1-L3/example-01.m4a) I do appreciate everything you've done to help me settle in. — _Eu agradeço muito tudo o que você fez para me ajudar a me adaptar._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M1-L3/example-02.m4a) Much as I enjoy living abroad, there are days when I'd give anything for my mother's feijoada. — _Por mais que eu goste de morar fora, há dias em que daria tudo pela feijoada da minha mãe._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M1-L3/example-03.m4a) Such was the sense of community in the neighbourhood that nobody ever locked their doors. — _O senso de comunidade no bairro era tanto que ninguém trancava as portas._

> 💡 **Dica:** **"Por mais que..."** em inglês tem várias formas elegantes: **Much as** I love it..., **However much** I love it..., **Hard as** I tried... Usar uma delas numa redação ou na prova oral é uma marca clara de C1.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I did enjoyed the party.  
  ✅ **I did enjoy the party.**  
  Depois de do/did enfático, use o verbo base.
- ❌ It was so a beautiful city that...  
  ✅ **It was such a beautiful city that... / The city was so beautiful that...**  
  such + a + adjetivo + substantivo; so + adjetivo.
- ❌ Strange as it seems may, ...  
  ✅ **Strange as it may seem, ...**  
  Adjetivo + as + sujeito + verbo.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: O do enfático

O **do / does / did** enfático é **sempre forte e acentuado**, com pausa mínima depois: "I **DO** miss home." / "She **DID** warn us." Sem ênfase, a frase perde o sentido.

<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Researcher:** The term "third culture kids" was coined in the 1950s by the American sociologist Ruth Hill Useem, who studied children of families living abroad. The idea is that these children grow up between their parents' culture and the culture of the country where they live, and end up creating a third culture of their own. Ask one of them "Where are you from?" and you may get a long pause. Many describe a feeling of belonging everywhere and nowhere. Strange as it may seem, they often feel most at home with other third culture kids, regardless of nationality. There are clear advantages: adaptability, language skills, an ability to see situations from several perspectives. But there are challenges too. Repeated goodbyes can make some people reluctant to form deep attachments. So common is this experience that many international schools now run programmes to help students deal with transitions.

## 📖 Reading — What makes a place home? [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M1-L3/reading.m4a)

Much as we talk about home as a place, researchers who study belonging suggest it is better understood as a set of relationships. In one influential framework, environmental psychologists describe "place attachment" as developing through three overlapping dimensions: the people we share a place with, the meaningful experiences we have there, and the physical features we come to know intimately — the smell of a particular bakery, the sound of the street at dawn. This helps explain a phenomenon that puzzles many migrants. Returning to their hometown after years away, they find the streets unchanged but the feeling gone. Their friends have moved on, the rhythms of daily life have changed, and they themselves are no longer the same people. Home, it turns out, does not stay where we left it. Brazil offers a vivid illustration. Over the twentieth century, millions of people moved from the countryside and the Northeast to cities in the Southeast. Many of them recreated home in their new neighbourhoods through food, music and festivals: the forró halls and Northeastern fairs of São Paulo are a direct result of this. Such was the scale of the movement that it transformed the culture of the cities that received it. Perhaps the lesson is that belonging is not something we find but something we build. Small as they are, the daily rituals of a place — a greeting from a neighbour, a familiar route to work — slowly turn a city into home.

**Questions:**
1. How do researchers suggest home is better understood?
2. What are the three dimensions of place attachment?
3. Why do returning migrants feel the place has changed?
4. How did internal migrants recreate home in São Paulo?

## 📝 Writing

Write a personal essay or short memoir (260–300 words) titled "The place I call home". Use at least two emphatic do/did, two fronting structures and one inversion with so/such.

**Model:**

> The place I call home  
>   
> When people ask me where I'm from, I usually say Campina Grande, because that's where I was born. But if they asked where home is, I'd have to think much harder.  
>   
> I left Paraíba at eighteen to study in Porto Alegre, more than three thousand kilometres away. The first winter was so cold that I slept in two jumpers and a hat. Such was the shock — the climate, the accent, the reserved way people greeted each other — that I seriously considered going back after the first semester.  
>   
> I didn't, of course. Slowly, the city began to change, or perhaps I did. I found a bakery where the owner remembered my order. I joined a football team made up almost entirely of other "outsiders" from different states. I learned to drink chimarrão, and, strange as it may seem, I came to love it.  
>   
> That was fifteen years ago. Today, I have a flat, a partner, a dog and a job I enjoy. I've put down roots. And yet, every June, I do feel a pull towards the Northeast. Much as I love Porto Alegre, nothing compares to the festa junina in Campina Grande: the forró, the bonfires, the smell of canjica and pamonha.  
>   
> So where is home? I've come to believe that it isn't a single place. Home is wherever my people are, and wherever I feel I can be fully myself. Some days that's a street in the South; other days, it's a memory of the Northeast.  
>   
> I do sometimes envy people who have never left the place where they were born. But I wouldn't change my two homes for anything.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Migração interna no Brasil

Ao longo do século XX, milhões de nordestinos migraram para o Sudeste, principalmente São Paulo e Rio. Essa migração moldou a cultura dessas cidades — do forró à culinária — e também gerou preconceitos que ainda existem. Em inglês, o fenômeno é descrito como **internal migration** ou **rural-to-urban migration**.
