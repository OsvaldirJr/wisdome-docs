## 🔥 Warm-up
Na prova C1 Advanced, a **Parte 3** (word formation) pede que você transforme uma palavra para completar um texto: CRITIC → **uncritically**, PREDICT → **unpredictable**. Mais do que prova, isso é **multiplicar seu vocabulário**: ao aprender uma raiz, você ganha uma família inteira. Tema: **críticas de cinema e séries**.

- Do you read reviews before watching a film or series? Do you trust them?
- What's a film or series that you think was overrated or underrated?

## 🧩 Grammar: Word formation

**1. Prefixos negativos** (o contrário):
- **un-**: unpredictable, unconvincing, unforgettable, unrealistic
- **in-**: inaccurate, incomplete, insensitive
- **im-** (antes de m, p, b): impossible, impatient, immature
- **ir-** (antes de r): irrelevant, irresistible, irresponsible
- **il-** (antes de l): illogical, illegal, illiterate
- **dis-**: dishonest, dislike, disappointing, disagree
- **non-**: non-fiction, non-existent

**2. Prefixos de sentido:**
- **mis-** (errado): misunderstand, misinterpret, miscast, mislead
- **over- / under-** (demais / de menos): overrated / underrated, overpriced, underestimate
- **re-** (de novo): remake, reread, reinvent
- **out-** (superar): outperform, outsell, outlive
- **counter-** (contra): counterproductive, counterpart
- **co-**: co-star, co-writer

**3. Sufixos que formam substantivos:**
- **-ness**: darkness, awareness, effectiveness
- **-ity**: originality, creativity, popularity, authenticity
- **-ment**: disappointment, achievement, entertainment
- **-tion/-sion**: production, adaptation, tension
- **-ance/-ence**: performance, relevance, existence
- **-ship / -hood**: relationship, childhood

**4. Sufixos que formam adjetivos:**
- **-able/-ible**: predictable, forgettable, believable, incredible
- **-ive**: impressive, creative, repetitive
- **-ful / -less**: powerful, pointless, meaningless
- **-ous**: ambitious, humorous, glamorous
- **-al / -ic**: emotional, dramatic, iconic

**5. Sufixos que formam verbos e advérbios:**
- **-ize/-ise**: dramatize, criticize, romanticize · **-en**: strengthen, shorten · **-ify**: intensify, simplify
- **-ly**: surprisingly, unexpectedly, convincingly

**Dica de prova:** leia a frase toda e pergunte: preciso de **substantivo, adjetivo, verbo ou advérbio**? **Positivo ou negativo**? **Singular ou plural**?

- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M4-L2/example-01.m4a) Despite its huge popularity, the series was criticized for its unrealistic dialogue. — _Apesar da enorme popularidade, a série foi criticada pelos diálogos irrealistas._

> 💡 **Dica:** Cuidado com os prefixos negativos que **não** seguem o português: **in**ac**cur**ate (impreciso), **un**able (incapaz), **dis**honest (desonesto), **un**comfortable (desconfortável), **ir**responsible. Na dúvida, consulte o dicionário — e anote a família inteira.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ The plot was very unpredictible.  
  ✅ **The plot was very unpredictable.**  
  predict + -able → predictable.
- ❌ The ending was a big deception.  
  ✅ **The ending was a big disappointment.**  
  Decepção = disappointment; deception = enganação.
- ❌ It's an inbelievable story.  
  ✅ **It's an unbelievable story.**  
  believable → unbelievable (un-, não in-).

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Acento em famílias de palavras

O acento se move com os sufixos: **CRI**tic → **CRI**ticize → criti**CIS**m? Não! **CRI**ticism. Mas: o**RI**ginal → origi**NA**lity; **PHO**tograph → pho**TO**graphy → photo**GRA**phic; pre**DICT** → pre**DIC**table. Sufixos como **-ity, -ic, -ion** puxam o acento para a sílaba anterior.

- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M4-L2/pron-01.m4a) oRIginal → origiNAlity
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M4-L2/pron-02.m4a) PHOtograph → phoTOgraphy → photoGRAphic
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M4-L2/pron-03.m4a) preDICT → preDICtable → unpreDICtable

## 🎧 Listening — Can we trust online ratings?

_Um especialista em mídia fala sobre avaliações online de filmes e séries._

**Listen first, without reading.** [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M4-L2/listening-full.m4a)

**Questions:**
1. What does a high percentage on an aggregator site mean?
2. Why can a divisive masterpiece score lower?
3. What is "review bombing"?
4. What is the speaker's advice?

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Speaker:** Before watching a film, millions of people check a score — the percentage of positive reviews on an aggregator site, or the star rating on a streaming platform. These numbers are useful, but they can be misleading. First, a high percentage doesn't necessarily mean a film is outstanding; it means most critics found it at least acceptable. A film that nobody loves but nobody hates can score higher than a bold, divisive masterpiece. Second, audience ratings can be manipulated. There have been well-documented cases of "review bombing", in which groups of users deliberately flood a film with negative ratings, sometimes before it has even been released. And third, critics and audiences often disagree. Some films are critically acclaimed but commercially unsuccessful; others are box-office hits that critics dismiss. My advice? Don't rely on a single number. Read two or three reviews from critics whose taste you know — and remember that disagreement is part of the pleasure of art.

</details>

## 📖 Reading — In defence of the bad review [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M4-L2/reading.m4a)

In an age of star ratings and thumbs-up icons, the traditional film critic can seem like an endangered species. Many newspapers have reduced their arts coverage, and audiences increasingly rely on algorithms and influencers to decide what to watch. Some argue that critics have become irrelevant — or worse, that they are snobbish gatekeepers out of touch with what ordinary people enjoy. It is worth defending them, however, and especially their least popular product: the negative review. A thoughtful bad review is not simply an expression of dislike. At its best, it is an explanation — of why a story feels unconvincing, why a performance seems mechanical, why a film that cost hundreds of millions to make leaves no lasting impression. In explaining their disappointment, critics help readers understand what makes art work, and sharpen their own judgement in the process. Negative reviews also perform a useful function in an industry dominated by marketing. Studios spend enormous sums persuading audiences that every release is unmissable. Independent criticism offers a counterweight to that pressure, reminding us that our time and money are limited. Of course, criticism can be unfair, careless or simply cruel. A review that ridicules without explaining contributes little. And critics are not infallible: history is full of works that were dismissed on release and later recognized as masterpieces. But the solution to bad criticism is better criticism, not none at all. In the end, a culture in which everything is "amazing" is a culture in which nothing is. The willingness to say "this doesn't work, and here's why" may be one of the most valuable things a critic can offer.

**Questions:**
1. Why can critics seem like an endangered species?
2. What is a thoughtful bad review, at its best?
3. What function do negative reviews perform against marketing?
4. What does the final sentence mean: "a culture in which everything is amazing is a culture in which nothing is"?

## 📝 Writing

Write a review (280–320 words) of a film, series or book for an English-language website. Include a brief summary (no spoilers!), strengths, weaknesses and a recommendation. Use at least ten words with prefixes or suffixes.

**Model:**

> Review: City of the Tides (series, season 1)  
>   
> There has been no shortage of hype around City of the Tides, the new eight-episode Brazilian thriller set in a fictional fishing town on the coast of Bahia. Expectations were high, and for the most part, the series lives up to them.  
>   
> The story follows Inês, a marine biologist who returns to her childhood home after twenty years to investigate the mysterious disappearance of a research boat. What begins as a straightforward mystery gradually develops into a thought-provoking drama about memory, family loyalty and the environmental destruction of the coast.  
>   
> The series' greatest strength is its atmosphere. The photography is breathtaking: sun-drenched beaches and storm-darkened skies create a sense of beauty that is constantly threatened. The lead actress gives a remarkably powerful performance, conveying her character's guilt and determination with impressive subtlety. The supporting cast is equally convincing, particularly the actor playing her estranged father.  
>   
> Unfortunately, the series is not without its weaknesses. The middle episodes are overlong and somewhat repetitive, and a subplot involving a corrupt politician feels underdeveloped and, at times, frankly unbelievable. Some viewers may also find the final twist a little predictable — although, to be fair, it is handled with considerable emotional intensity.  
>   
> Despite these flaws, City of the Tides is an ambitious and genuinely original piece of television. It avoids the temptation to oversimplify its themes, and it trusts its audience to draw their own conclusions. The cliffhanger at the end of the final episode makes a second season almost irresistible.  
>   
> Verdict: not quite the masterpiece some critics have claimed, but a gripping, beautifully made series that deserves a wide international audience. Definitely worth a binge-watch.  
>   
> Rating: ★★★★☆

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Falsos amigos da crítica

Cuidado com estes falsos cognatos ao escrever críticas: **deception** (enganação, não decepção → **disappointment**), **pretentious** (pretensioso, ok!), **sensible** (sensato, não sensível → **sensitive**), **novel** (romance — o gênero literário; telenovela é **soap opera**), **argument** (discussão/argumento, não **plot** = enredo).
