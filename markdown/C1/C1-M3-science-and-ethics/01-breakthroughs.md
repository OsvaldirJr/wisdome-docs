## 🎯 Nesta aula você vai
- Usar a passiva com modais no presente e no passado (must have been, could be done)
- Usar o get passive para eventos inesperados ou negativos
- Usar have / get something done para serviços e experiências negativas

## 🔥 Warm-up
Na ciência, o foco geralmente está no **que foi descoberto**, não em **quem** descobriu — por isso a passiva domina textos científicos. No C1, você vai além do "was discovered": "It **must have been** contaminated", "He **got** bitten", "They **had** their samples **analysed**". Tema: as **grandes descobertas** da ciência.

- Which scientific discovery do you think has changed the world the most?
- Would you volunteer to test a new vaccine or medicine? Why / why not?

## 🧩 Grammar: Advanced passive forms

**1. Modal + passive**
- Presente/futuro: **modal + be + particípio**: "The results **must be checked**." / "This **could be done** faster."
- Passado: **modal + have been + particípio**: "The sample **must have been contaminated**." / "The disease **could have been prevented**."
- Contínuo (raro): "The data **might be being analysed** right now."

**2. Get passive** (informal) → eventos **inesperados, acidentais** ou **negativos**; também mudança de estado:
- "He **got bitten** by a snake." / "Our flight **got cancelled**." / "She **got promoted**."
- Mais comum na fala; evite em textos acadêmicos.

**3. Have / get something done** (causativo)
- **Serviço** feito por outra pessoa: "I **had** my car **repaired**." / "We need to **get** the samples **analysed**."
- **Experiência negativa** (alguém fez algo com você): "She **had** her bag **stolen**." / "He **had** his application **rejected** three times."

**4. Verbos com dois objetos** (give, send, offer, award, show, lend, tell, promise):
- "They **gave** the scientists a prize." →
  • "The scientists **were given** a prize." (mais comum: pessoa como sujeito)
  • "A prize **was given to** the scientists."

**5. Passiva com preposição no final:**
- "The problem **was dealt with** quickly." / "This theory **has been argued about** for years."

**6. Passiva com verbos de percepção + infinitivo com to:**
- Ativa: "They **saw** him **leave**." → Passiva: "He **was seen to leave**."
- "She **was made to** repeat the experiment." (ativa: They made her repeat...)

| Forma | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| modal + be + pp | It should be tested again. |
| modal + have been + pp | It can't have been planned. |
| get + pp | The paper got rejected. |
| have/get + objeto + pp (serviço) | We had the lab cleaned. |
| have + objeto + pp (negativo) | He had his data hacked. |
| dois objetos | She was offered a grant. |
| made/seen + to | They were made to wait. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L1/example-01.m4a) Many deaths could have been prevented if the vaccine had been distributed earlier. — _Muitas mortes poderiam ter sido evitadas se a vacina tivesse sido distribuída antes._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L1/example-02.m4a) The team had their results checked by an independent laboratory before publishing. — _A equipe teve seus resultados verificados por um laboratório independente antes de publicar._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L1/example-03.m4a) Katalin Karikó was refused funding many times before her work was finally recognized. — _Katalin Karikó teve financiamento negado muitas vezes antes de seu trabalho finalmente ser reconhecido._

> 💡 **Dica:** "Fiz um exame" / "cortei o cabelo" (no salão) → em inglês, use o causativo: "I **had** a blood test **done**" / "I **had** my hair **cut**". "I cut my hair" significa que **você mesmo** cortou!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ The sample must have contaminated.  
  ✅ **The sample must have been contaminated.**  
  Passiva no passado com modal: must have been + pp.
- ❌ I cut my hair at the salon yesterday.  
  ✅ **I had my hair cut at the salon yesterday.**  
  Serviço feito por outra pessoa = have something done.
- ❌ The prize was given the scientists.  
  ✅ **The scientists were given the prize. / The prize was given to the scientists.**  
  Com a coisa como sujeito, use to antes da pessoa.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: must have been / could have been

Na fala, **must have been** vira "**mâstavbin**" e **could have been** vira "**kúdavbin**". O **been** costuma ser fraco ("bin"). Já em **have something done**, o acento vai no **objeto** e no **particípio**: "I had my **CAR** re**PAIRED**."

- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L1/pron-01.m4a) It must've been contaminated.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L1/pron-02.m4a) It could've been prevented.
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L1/pron-03.m4a) I had my CAR rePAIRED.

**Journalist:** When people think of great scientific discoveries, Brazil isn't always the first country that comes to mind. Yet Brazilian scientists have made important contributions. In 1909, the physician Carlos Chagas described a new disease — now known as Chagas disease — identifying the parasite that causes it, the insect that transmits it and its clinical symptoms, an achievement that is often described as unique in the history of medicine. ## 📖 Reading — The scientist nobody believed [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L1/reading.m4a)

For decades, the Hungarian-born biochemist Katalin Karikó was considered, by many of her colleagues, to be chasing an impossible dream. Her idea was to use messenger RNA — the molecule that carries genetic instructions inside cells — to make the body produce its own proteins, which could then be used to treat or prevent disease. The problem was that synthetic mRNA, when injected into animals, triggered a strong inflammatory reaction. Grant applications were rejected repeatedly, and in the 1990s she was demoted at the University of Pennsylvania. Most researchers would have given up. Karikó did not. In the early 2000s, working with the immunologist Drew Weissman, she discovered that the inflammatory reaction could be largely avoided if one of the building blocks of the mRNA was slightly modified. Their paper, published in 2005, attracted little attention at the time. Fifteen years later, it was fundamental to one of the fastest medical responses in history. When the COVID-19 pandemic struck, vaccines based on modified mRNA were developed, tested and approved in less than a year — a process that had traditionally taken a decade or more. Hundreds of millions of doses were administered worldwide. In 2023, Karikó and Weissman were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Her story is often told as a lesson in persistence. But it also raises uncomfortable questions about the way science is funded. How many other good ideas have been abandoned because they could not be explained in a short grant proposal, or did not produce quick results?

**Questions:**
1. What was Karikó's idea?
2. What was the main problem with synthetic mRNA?
3. What did Karikó and Weissman discover?
4. What uncomfortable question does the story raise?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short biography (280–320 words) of a scientist whose work was ignored, rejected or underestimated before being recognized. Use at least eight passive forms, including modal passives, have something done and passives with two objects.

**Model:**

> Ignaz Semmelweis: the doctor who asked for clean hands  
>   
> In the 1840s, the maternity wards of Vienna General Hospital were divided into two clinics. In the first, women were attended by doctors and medical students; in the second, by midwives. Strangely, the death rate from a condition called childbed fever was far higher in the first clinic. Many mothers begged to be admitted to the second.  
>   
> The young Hungarian doctor Ignaz Semmelweis was determined to find out why. A breakthrough was made in 1847, when a colleague died after being cut accidentally during an autopsy. His symptoms were remarkably similar to those of the dying mothers. Semmelweis concluded that the disease must have been carried by doctors who went straight from dissecting bodies to examining patients.  
>   
> His solution was simple: all staff were required to have their hands washed in a chlorine solution before touching patients. The results were dramatic. Within months, the death rate in the first clinic had fallen sharply.  
>   
> Yet his ideas were not welcomed. Many senior doctors were offended by the suggestion that they themselves could be responsible for their patients' deaths. Germ theory had not yet been developed, so his findings could not be fully explained. Semmelweis was not given a permanent position, and he eventually left Vienna.  
>   
> His later years were tragic. His behaviour became increasingly erratic, and in 1865 he was committed to an asylum, where he died shortly afterwards, apparently after being beaten by guards.  
>   
> Only years later, after the work of Louis Pasteur and Joseph Lister, was Semmelweis recognized as a pioneer of antiseptic practice. Today he is often called "the saviour of mothers". His story is a painful reminder that good evidence can be ignored when it challenges powerful people — and that many lives might have been saved if he had been listened to.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Publish or perish"

No mundo acadêmico anglófono, a expressão **"publish or perish"** (publique ou pereça) resume a pressão sobre pesquisadores para publicar artigos constantemente. Críticos dizem que isso incentiva pesquisas rápidas e "seguras" em vez de ideias arriscadas — como as de Katalin Karikó, que levaram décadas para dar frutos.
