## 🧩 Grammar: The subjunctive and formal structures

**1. Subjuntivo presente** = **verbo base** para **todas as pessoas** (sem -s, sem do, sem tempo passado).
Aparece depois de verbos e adjetivos de **exigência, recomendação ou importância**:
- Verbos: **recommend, suggest, insist, demand, propose, request, require, ask, urge** + **that**
- Adjetivos: **it is essential / vital / crucial / important / necessary / imperative that**

"The doctor **recommended that** she **take** a break." (não "takes")
"They **insisted that** the trial **be** stopped." (não "is" / "was")
"It is **vital that** he **not** be informed yet." (negativa: **not** + base)

⚠️ O tempo do verbo principal **não muda** o subjuntivo: "They **demanded** that he **resign**."

**Alternativa britânica (menos formal):** **should** + verbo: "They recommended that the study **should be** suspended." Ambas são corretas.

**2. Should no lugar de if (inversão)** — hipóteses educadas e formais:
- "**Should you need** any further information, do not hesitate to contact us." (= If you need...)

**3. Were it not for / Had it not been for** (= se não fosse por):
- "**Were it not for** vaccines, millions more would die every year."
- "**Had it not been for** the transplant, he would have died."

**4. Expressões fixas com subjuntivo** (muito comuns!):
- **Be that as it may** (seja como for)
- **Come what may** (aconteça o que acontecer)
- **God save the King / Long live...**
- **Suffice it to say** (basta dizer)
- **If need be** (se for necessário)
- **So be it** (que assim seja)
- **..., be it X or Y** (seja X ou Y): "All life, **be it** human or animal, deserves respect."

| Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| recommend / insist + that + base | They insisted that she leave. |
| It is vital that + base | It is vital that he be told. |
| negativa: that + not + base | We suggest that it not be used. |
| Should + sujeito + verbo | Should you require help, call us. |
| Were it not for + noun | Were it not for you, I'd have failed. |
| Expressões fixas | Be that as it may... / Come what may... |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L2/example-01.m4a) Several scientists have proposed that a global moratorium be placed on inheritable gene editing. — _Vários cientistas propuseram que se estabeleça uma moratória global sobre a edição genética hereditária._
- [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L2/example-02.m4a) It is crucial that the public be involved in decisions about new technologies. — _É crucial que o público seja envolvido nas decisões sobre novas tecnologias._

> 💡 **Dica:** Brasileiros acertam o subjuntivo **intuitivamente** quando pensam em português: "insistiram que ele **saísse**" → "insisted that he **leave**". O truque: use o **verbo base**, sem conjugar!

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ The doctor recommended that she takes a break.  
  ✅ **The doctor recommended that she take a break. / ...that she should take a break.**  
  Subjuntivo = verbo base, sem -s.
- ❌ It is essential that he is informed.  
  ✅ **It is essential that he be informed.**  
  Em registro formal, use be (subjuntivo).
- ❌ If you should need help, please to contact us.  
  ✅ **Should you need help, please contact us.**  
  Inversão com should; please + verbo base.

<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Journalist:** In November 2018, the Chinese scientist He Jiankui announced that he had used the gene-editing tool CRISPR to alter the DNA of human embryos, resulting in the birth of twin girls. His stated aim was to make them resistant to HIV. The reaction from the scientific community was almost universally negative. Critics argued that the procedure was medically unnecessary, that its long-term effects were unknown, and that the changes could be passed on to future generations. Many questioned whether the parents had given truly informed consent. He was later sentenced to prison in China. The case led to calls from leading scientists that a global moratorium be placed on heritable genome editing until its safety and ethics could be properly assessed. It's worth noting that gene editing in non-reproductive cells — which only affects the patient — is a different matter, and therapies of this kind have already been approved in some countries. The line, most experts agree, is crossed when changes are made that will be inherited.

## 📖 Reading — Who decides? [🔊](../../../audio/C1/C1-M3-L2/reading.m4a)

Few technologies have raised hopes and fears as dramatically as gene editing. Tools such as CRISPR, whose developers Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier were awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2020, allow scientists to modify DNA with a precision that would have been unimaginable a generation ago. The potential benefits are enormous. Inherited diseases that have caused suffering for centuries could, in theory, be corrected. In 2023, regulators in the United Kingdom and the United States approved the first therapy based on CRISPR, designed to treat sickle cell disease. Yet the same technology raises profound ethical questions. If it becomes possible to eliminate a disease, should it also be possible to select for height, intelligence or appearance? Critics warn of a slippery slope towards "designer babies" and a world in which genetic advantages are available only to the wealthy. Disability rights advocates have also asked who gets to decide which human traits count as "defects". Most scientific bodies currently insist that a clear distinction be maintained between somatic editing, which affects only the individual patient, and germline editing, whose changes would be inherited by future generations. The first is increasingly accepted; the second remains, in most countries, prohibited. But laws vary from one country to another, and technology tends to move faster than regulation. Were it not for international cooperation, the rules could easily be bypassed by researchers moving to countries with weaker oversight. Perhaps the most important question is not what science can do, but who should decide what it ought to do — scientists, governments, patients or society as a whole. Be that as it may, it seems essential that these decisions not be left to a few laboratories alone.

**Questions:**
1. Who developed CRISPR and what were they awarded?
2. What was approved in 2023?
3. What is the difference between somatic and germline editing?
4. Why is international cooperation important?

## 📝 Writing

Write a formal essay (280–320 words): "Gene editing should be used only to treat disease, never to enhance human abilities." Discuss both sides and give your opinion. Use at least four subjunctive forms and two other formal structures from this lesson.

**Model:**

> Treatment or enhancement? The limits of gene editing  
>   
> Advances in gene-editing technology have made it possible, for the first time in history, to alter human DNA with remarkable precision. While few would object to its use in curing devastating diseases, many experts insist that a firm line be drawn between treatment and enhancement. This essay will examine whether such a line is both desirable and realistic.  
>   
> The case for restricting gene editing to medical treatment is a strong one. Were it not for clear limits, the technology could easily be used to create genetic advantages for those who could afford them, deepening existing inequalities. Moreover, our understanding of the human genome remains incomplete. A change intended to improve one characteristic might have unforeseen consequences elsewhere, and if made in embryos, those consequences would be inherited by future generations. It is therefore vital that any intervention be justified by a serious medical need.  
>   
> On the other hand, critics argue that the distinction between treatment and enhancement is far from clear. Is strengthening the immune system a treatment or an enhancement? What about reducing the risk of a condition that may never develop? Some have also pointed out that we already accept many forms of enhancement, from vaccines to education, and that refusing genetic improvements on principle may be inconsistent.  
>   
> Be that as it may, I believe the risks of allowing enhancement currently outweigh the potential benefits. The technology is too new, its long-term effects too uncertain and its social consequences too unpredictable. I would therefore recommend that its use be limited to the treatment of serious diseases, and that any change to that policy be decided through public debate rather than by scientists or companies alone. Should our knowledge improve significantly in the future, the question could be reconsidered. Until then, caution seems the wisest course.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — O subjuntivo: EUA x Reino Unido

O subjuntivo ("I suggest that he **go**") é **mais comum no inglês americano**, inclusive na fala. No inglês britânico, é mais frequente usar **should** ("I suggest that he **should go**") ou até o indicativo ("I suggest that he **goes**") em contextos informais. Em provas e textos formais, qualquer uma das duas primeiras formas é segura.
