## 🧩 Grammar: Should have / shouldn't have + particípio

**Should have + particípio** → algo que **deveria ter acontecido, mas não aconteceu** (arrependimento ou crítica sobre o passado):
- "I **should have checked** the numbers." (não conferi — me arrependo)
- "You **should have told** me earlier!" (você não me contou — crítica)

**Shouldn't have + particípio** → algo que **aconteceu, mas não deveria**:
- "I **shouldn't have sent** that email." (mandei — erro)
- "You **shouldn't have spent** so much money!" (às vezes é carinho, ao receber um presente!)

Na fala: **should have** = "**shúdav**" ou até "**shúda**".

**Estrutura de um bom pedido de desculpas profissional:**
1. **Reconheça:** "I'm sorry for / I apologize for + -ing / substantivo": "I apologize for **missing** the deadline."
2. **Assuma a responsabilidade** (sem culpar outros): "It was my fault. I **should have** asked for help earlier."
3. **Explique** brevemente (sem desculpas longas): "I underestimated how long it would take."
4. **Ofereça solução:** "I'll send it by 10 tomorrow."
5. **Prevenção:** "In future, I'll make sure to..."

⚠️ **Sorry for** + -ing/substantivo (sobre algo que você fez) · **sorry to** + verbo (algo que você está fazendo agora): "Sorry **to bother** you." / "Sorry **for being** late."

| Situação | Frase |
|---|---|
| Arrependimento (não fiz) | I should have studied more. |
| Arrependimento (fiz) | I shouldn't have eaten so much. |
| Crítica (não fez) | You should have called me. |
| Crítica (fez) | They shouldn't have cancelled the event. |
| Desculpa formal | We apologize for the delay. |
| Desculpa informal | Sorry, my bad! |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L4/example-01.m4a) I apologize for the mistake in the report. I should have double-checked the figures. — _Peço desculpas pelo erro no relatório. Eu deveria ter conferido os números duas vezes._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L4/example-02.m4a) We shouldn't have promised such a tight deadline. — _Não deveríamos ter prometido um prazo tão apertado._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L4/example-03.m4a) Sorry to interrupt, but the client is on the phone. — _Desculpe interromper, mas o cliente está no telefone._

> 💡 **Dica:** O famoso **"I did my best"** (fiz o meu melhor) pode soar como desculpa se vier sozinho. Combine com responsabilidade e solução: "I did my best, but I should have asked for help sooner. Here's what I'll do now..."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I should checked the email.  
  ✅ **I should have checked the email.**  
  Should have + particípio.
- ❌ I should have went to the meeting.  
  ✅ **I should have gone to the meeting.**  
  Use o particípio (gone), não o passado (went).
- ❌ Sorry for disturb you.  
  ✅ **Sorry to disturb you. / Sorry for disturbing you.**  
  Sorry to + verbo base ou sorry for + -ing.

## 📖 Reading — The anatomy of a good apology [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L4/reading.m4a)

Why do some apologies make things better while others make them worse? Researchers who study communication have identified several elements that make an apology effective. The most important one, according to some studies, is acknowledging responsibility: saying clearly "It was my fault" or "I made a mistake". The second is offering to repair the damage. Expressing regret ("I'm sorry") matters too, but on its own it often isn't enough. Interestingly, long explanations can backfire: if you spend too much time explaining why something happened, people may hear it as an excuse. The worst apologies are "non-apologies" — phrases like "I'm sorry if you were offended" or "Mistakes were made", which avoid taking real responsibility. Public figures and companies use them all the time, and audiences usually notice. The lesson? A good apology is short, specific and focused on the other person, not on yourself.

**Questions:**
1. What is the most important element of an apology, according to some studies?
2. Why can long explanations backfire?
3. What is a "non-apology"?
4. How should a good apology be?

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Sorry" britânico

Britânicos dizem **sorry** o tempo todo — até quando **outra pessoa** esbarra neles! É mais um sinal de educação do que um pedido de desculpas real. Já em contextos jurídicos nos EUA, empresas às vezes evitam dizer "sorry" porque pode ser interpretado como admissão de culpa.
