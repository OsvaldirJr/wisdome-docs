## 🧩 Grammar: Present perfect simple x continuous

**Present perfect continuous (have been + -ing)** → foco no **processo**, na **duração**, na **atividade** (que pode ainda não ter terminado):
- "I**'ve been writing** my thesis **for** two years." (processo em andamento)
- "She**'s been training** every day." (esforço repetido)
- "Why are you so tired? — I**'ve been studying** all night." (atividade recente com efeito visível)

**Present perfect simple (have + particípio)** → foco no **resultado**, na **quantidade**, no que foi **concluído**:
- "I**'ve written** three chapters." (quantidade concluída)
- "She**'s won** two medals."
- "I**'ve finished** my thesis!" (completo)

**Pergunta-chave:**
- **How long** have you been...? → duração (continuous)
- **How many / How much** have you...? → quantidade (simple)

**Stative verbs** (know, believe, have [posse], want, own) → sempre simple: "I**'ve known** her for years."

**Live, work, study, teach** → com for/since, os dois são possíveis com pouca diferença; o continuous sugere algo mais **temporário**: "I've lived here all my life" (permanente) x "I've been living here since March" (temporário).

| Continuous (processo) | Simple (resultado) |
|---|---|
| I've been running since 7. | I've run 15 km. |
| She's been saving for a car. | She's saved R$20,000. |
| They've been building their startup for 3 years. | They've hired 40 people. |
| How long have you been learning guitar? | How many songs have you learned? |
| I've been reading this book. | I've read this book. (terminei) |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L3/example-01.m4a) We've been developing this app for three years, and we've finally launched it! — _Estamos desenvolvendo este app há três anos, e finalmente o lançamos!_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L3/example-02.m4a) How many marathons have you run? — Four. I've been running for ten years. — _Quantas maratonas você já correu? — Quatro. Corro há dez anos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L3/example-03.m4a) I've been struggling with this exercise all morning, and I still haven't solved it. — _Estou batalhando com este exercício a manhã toda, e ainda não resolvi._

> 💡 **Dica:** Se você diz **um número de vezes ou uma quantidade**, quase sempre é **simple**: "I've **called** her **three times**" (❌ "I've been calling her three times").

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I've been writing four emails this morning.  
  ✅ **I've written four emails this morning.**  
  Quantidade (four) = simple.
- ❌ I've been knowing him since school.  
  ✅ **I've known him since school.**  
  Know é stative: sempre simple.
- ❌ How long have you read this book? (ainda está lendo)  
  ✅ **How long have you been reading this book?**  
  Duração de atividade em andamento = continuous.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Ritmo no present perfect continuous

"I've been working" tem só uma sílaba forte: **WORK**. O resto é rápido: "aivbin-WÓR-kin". Pratique batendo na mesa só na sílaba forte.

## 📖 Reading — The myth of talent [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L3/reading.m4a)

When we see a brilliant musician, athlete or entrepreneur, we often assume they were simply born with a special talent. But researchers who study expertise tell a different story. In a famous study in the 1990s, psychologist Anders Ericsson looked at violin students at a music academy in Berlin. He found that the best students had practiced significantly more hours than the others — and, crucially, that they had practiced in a specific way. Ericsson called it "deliberate practice": working on your weaknesses, getting regular feedback and constantly pushing beyond your comfort zone. Writer Malcolm Gladwell later popularized the idea as the "10,000-hour rule", although Ericsson himself said that the number was not a magic rule. Since then, other scientists have argued that factors like genes, starting age and opportunity also matter. Still, the main lesson remains: behind most "overnight successes" there are years of invisible, focused work.

**Questions:**
1. What did Ericsson find about the best violin students?
2. What is deliberate practice?
3. Who popularized the 10,000-hour rule?
4. What other factors have scientists mentioned?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short profile (150–180 words) of someone who reached success after a long process (a family member, a celebrity or yourself). Use present perfect simple and continuous.

**Model:**

> My aunt Lúcia has been running a small restaurant in Belém for almost twenty years. She started with four tables in her garage, cooking the recipes she had learned from her grandmother. For years, she struggled: she worked fourteen hours a day, and there were months when she couldn't pay the bills. But she kept at it. Little by little, people started talking about her maniçoba and her tacacá. In the last five years, her restaurant has appeared in national magazines, and she's won two regional awards. Recently, she's been training young cooks from her neighborhood, and she's hired eleven of them. She's also been writing a cookbook, which she hopes to publish next year. When journalists call her an overnight success, she laughs. "I've been cooking every single day for twenty years," she says. "This isn't luck. It's work."

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Fail fast" e a cultura do erro

No Vale do Silício, expressões como **"fail fast"** e **"fail forward"** celebram o erro como parte do aprendizado. Muitos empreendedores contam com orgulho quantas vezes **have failed** antes de dar certo. Em entrevistas americanas, falar de um fracasso e do que aprendeu com ele é visto como força, não fraqueza.
