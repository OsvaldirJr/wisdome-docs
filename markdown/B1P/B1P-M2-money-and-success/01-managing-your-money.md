## 🧩 Grammar: Quantifiers

**Grande quantidade:**
- **a lot of / lots of** + contável e incontável (principalmente afirmativas): a lot of money, lots of bills
- **many** + contável · **much** + incontável → principalmente em **perguntas e negativas**: How much debt? / not many options
- **plenty of** = mais que suficiente: "Don't worry, we have **plenty of** time."

**Pequena quantidade — a diferença sutil:**
- **a few** (contável) / **a little** (incontável) = **alguns, um pouco** → sentido **positivo** (é suficiente)
  "I have **a little** money saved. Let's go out!"
- **few** / **little** (sem "a") = **poucos, pouco** → sentido **negativo** (quase nada)
  "**Few** people save enough for retirement." / "There's **little** hope."

**Excesso e falta:**
- **too much** + incontável / **too many** + contável = demais (problema): "I spend **too much** money on apps." / "I have **too many** subscriptions."
- **(not) enough** + substantivo = (não) suficiente: "I don't have **enough** savings."

**Nenhum:**
- **no** + substantivo = "There's **no** money left." (= not any)
- **none (of)** sozinho: "How much is left? **None**."

| Quantificador | Contável (bills) | Incontável (money) |
|---|---|---|
| muito | a lot of / many bills | a lot of / much money |
| mais que suficiente | plenty of bills | plenty of money |
| alguns / um pouco (+) | a few bills | a little money |
| poucos / pouco (−) | few bills | little money |
| demais | too many bills | too much money |
| suficiente | enough bills | enough money |
| nenhum | no bills | no money |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L1/example-01.m4a) Few Brazilians have an emergency fund, but a few apps are trying to change that. — _Poucos brasileiros têm reserva de emergência, mas alguns apps estão tentando mudar isso._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L1/example-02.m4a) I had too many subscriptions, so I cancelled a few of them. — _Eu tinha assinaturas demais, então cancelei algumas._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L1/example-03.m4a) We don't have much time, but we have plenty of ideas. — _Não temos muito tempo, mas temos ideias de sobra._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Money** é incontável, mas **reais / dollars / coins / bills** são contáveis: "How **much money**?" x "How **many dollars**?"

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I have too much bills to pay.  
  ✅ **I have too many bills to pay.**  
  Bills é contável: too many.
- ❌ I have few money, so let's have a coffee.  
  ✅ **I have a little money, so let's have a coffee.**  
  Sentido positivo (é suficiente) = a little.
- ❌ There are much people in debt.  
  ✅ **There are a lot of / many people in debt.**  
  People é contável: many ou a lot of.

**Educator:** Here are a few rules I teach everyone. One: before you buy something that isn't essential, wait 48 hours. Most impulse purchases don't survive the wait. Two: try the 50-30-20 rule — 50% of your income for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings or paying debt. Three: build an emergency fund. ## 📖 Reading — Cashless societies [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M2-L1/reading.m4a)

In just a few years, the way people pay for things has changed dramatically. In Sweden, very few shops still accept cash, and some banks no longer handle coins and notes at all. In Brazil, Pix — the instant payment system launched by the Central Bank in 2020 — became one of the most widely used payment methods in the country in a remarkably short time. Street vendors, taxi drivers and even beggars now display QR codes. The advantages are clear: payments are fast, free for individuals and convenient. But there are concerns too. Digital payments make it easier to spend without noticing — paying with a phone doesn't "hurt" as much as handing over a banknote. Fraud and scams have also increased. And not everyone has a smartphone or a bank account, so a fully cashless society could leave the most vulnerable people behind. The question is not whether cash will disappear, but how quickly — and who will pay the price.

**Questions:**
1. What is happening in Sweden?
2. What is Pix?
3. Why can digital payments make us spend more?
4. Who could be left behind in a cashless society?

## 📝 Writing

Write an article (150–180 words) for a student magazine: "5 money tips for young people". Use at least 6 different quantifiers.

**Model:**

> 5 money tips for young people  
>   
> 1. Track every expense for a month. Most people are surprised by how much money they spend on small things like snacks and apps.  
> 2. Cancel what you don't use. Many of us have too many subscriptions — a few of them we haven't opened in months.  
> 3. Be careful with installments. Paying in ten installments feels easy, but if you have too many, there will be little money left at the end of the month.  
> 4. Start an emergency fund, even with a little money. Few students can save a lot, but saving something every month builds a powerful habit.  
> 5. Learn the basics of investing. You don't need much money to start, and there are plenty of free courses online.  
>   
> Remember: financial freedom isn't about earning a lot. It's about spending less than you earn — and making sure you always have enough for the unexpected.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Parcelado sem juros" — uma invenção brasileira

O parcelamento sem juros no cartão é muito comum no Brasil, mas raro em outros países. Nos EUA e na Europa, existem serviços de **BNPL (Buy Now, Pay Later)**, mas pagar uma TV em 10x sem juros surpreende estrangeiros. Para explicar: **"In Brazil, you can split the payment into installments with no interest."**
