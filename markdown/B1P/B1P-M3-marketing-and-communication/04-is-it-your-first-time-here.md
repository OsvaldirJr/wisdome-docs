## 🧩 Grammar: Linguagem funcional de networking

Networking usa estruturas que você já conhece, combinadas em **funções**:

**1. Abrir a conversa (comentário + pergunta):**
- Comentário sobre o contexto + tag question ou pergunta: "Great talk, **wasn't it**?" / "Is it your first time **at this conference**?"
- Pedir para se juntar: "**Mind if I join you?**" / "**Do you mind if** I sit here?"

**2. Apresentar-se e apresentar outros:**
- "I don't think we've met. I'm Ana, **I work for**... / **I'm in charge of**..."
- "**Have you met** Pedro? Pedro, **this is** Laura. **She's the one who** organized the event." (relative clause!)

**3. Manter a conversa — perguntas abertas** (What / How / Why), não sim/não:
- "**What brings you** here?" / "**How did you get into** marketing?" / "**What are you working on** at the moment?"
- **Comentários de interesse:** "Oh, that sounds fascinating!" / "Really? How interesting."
- **Encontrar algo em comum:** "**So do I!**" / "**Me too** — I've been working in tech for five years."

**4. Encerrar com elegância** (nunca sair sem dizer nada):
- "**I'll let you** go and mingle." / "**I should** go and say hi to my colleague."
- "**It's been** great talking to you." (present perfect!)
- "**Let's** connect on LinkedIn." / "**I'll** send you an email next week." (will = promessa)

| Função | Frases |
|---|---|
| Abrir | Is it your first time here? / Mind if I join you? |
| Apresentar-se | I don't think we've met. I'm... |
| Apresentar outros | Have you met...? / This is..., who... |
| Manter | What brings you here? / How did you get into...? |
| Reagir | That sounds fascinating! / So do I! |
| Encerrar | I'll let you mingle. / It's been great talking to you. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L4/example-01.m4a) Excuse me, is this seat taken? — No, go ahead. — Thanks. Is it your first time at this conference? — _Com licença, este lugar está ocupado? — Não, fique à vontade. — Obrigado. É sua primeira vez nesta conferência?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L4/example-02.m4a) Have you met Leo? He's the developer who built our app. — _Você conhece o Leo? Ele é o desenvolvedor que construiu nosso app._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L4/example-03.m4a) Anyway, I'll let you get some coffee. It's been great talking to you — let's keep in touch! — _Enfim, vou te deixar pegar um café. Foi ótimo conversar — vamos manter contato!_

> 💡 **Dica:** Brasileiros costumam ser ótimos em networking pela simpatia natural. O desafio em inglês é **encerrar a conversa** — use as frases de encerramento para não ficar preso nem sair de forma abrupta.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Do you work here? — Yes. (silêncio)  
  ✅ **Yes, I'm in the marketing team. What about you?**  
  Respostas curtas matam a conversa. Responda e devolva uma pergunta.
- ❌ I present you Pedro.  
  ✅ **Let me introduce you to Pedro. / This is Pedro.**  
  "Apresentar alguém" = introduce (present é apresentar um trabalho/projeto).
- ❌ Was a pleasure.  
  ✅ **It was a pleasure. / It's been great talking to you.**  
  A frase precisa de sujeito.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Entonação entusiasmada

Em inglês, demonstrar interesse exige **mais variação de entonação** do que em português. "That sounds **FAS**cinating!" com a voz subindo e descendo bastante. Uma entonação plana pode soar como desinteresse.

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Coach:** Many people hate networking because they think it's about selling themselves. It isn't. Good networking is about being curious. Here are my top tips. First, arrive early — it's much easier to start conversations when the room is half empty. Second, prepare three open questions, like "What are you working on at the moment?" Third, listen more than you talk; people remember how you made them feel, not your job title. Fourth, don't stay with the same person all night — after ten minutes, politely move on. And finally, follow up within 48 hours. Send a short message mentioning something specific you talked about. That's how a five-minute conversation becomes a long-term relationship.

## 📖 Reading — The strength of weak ties [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L4/reading.m4a)

Who is most likely to help you find your next job: your best friend, or someone you met once at a conference? Surprisingly, research suggests it's often the second. In a classic 1973 study, sociologist Mark Granovetter interviewed people who had recently changed jobs and found that many of them had heard about the opportunity through acquaintances — people they saw only occasionally — rather than close friends. He called this "the strength of weak ties". The explanation is simple: your close friends usually know the same people and hear about the same opportunities as you. Acquaintances, on the other hand, move in different circles, so they bring new information. A large study published in 2022, which analyzed data from millions of LinkedIn users, found further support for the idea, showing that moderately weak connections were the most useful for job mobility. So the next time you're at an event and feel tempted to talk only to your colleagues, remember: the stranger by the coffee machine might be the key to your next opportunity.

**Questions:**
1. What did Granovetter find?
2. Why are acquaintances useful?
3. What did the 2022 LinkedIn study show?
4. What's the advice at the end?
