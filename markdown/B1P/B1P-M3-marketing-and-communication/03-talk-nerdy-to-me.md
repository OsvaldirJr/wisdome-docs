# Talk nerdy to me
**B1P-M3-L3 · Marketing and Communication** — Formas de futuro e como explicar ciência de forma simples  
⏱ 70 min

## 🎯 Nesta aula você vai
- Escolher entre will, going to, present continuous e present simple para o futuro
- Usar will para previsões e decisões, going to para planos e evidências
- Explicar uma ideia técnica para um público leigo
- Usar linguagem de apresentação (Today I'm going to talk about...)

## 🔥 Warm-up
Você já tentou explicar seu trabalho para sua avó? Cientistas, engenheiros e médicos muitas vezes têm dificuldade em comunicar suas ideias para o público. Hoje vamos aprender a "falar nerd" de um jeito que todo mundo entende.

- Can you explain your job or studies to a 10-year-old?
- Who's the best explainer you know (teacher, YouTuber, scientist)?

## 🧩 Grammar: Future forms (revisão avançada)

No A2 você conheceu as formas de futuro. No B1+, use-as com precisão — especialmente em apresentações e no trabalho:

**will + verbo:**
- **previsão / opinião** (sem evidência concreta): "I think AI **will** change medicine." / "Scientists probably **won't** find a cure soon."
- **decisão no momento**: "The projector isn't working. — I**'ll** call IT."
- **promessa / oferta**: "I**'ll** send you the slides after the talk."

**be going to + verbo:**
- **plano / intenção** já decidido: "In this talk, I**'m going to** explain three ideas."
- **previsão com evidência** visível agora: "Look at the data — the temperature **is going to** rise."

**Present continuous:** **compromisso marcado** (agenda): "I**'m presenting** at the conference on Friday."

**Present simple:** **horários fixos** e programações: "The talk **starts** at 10 and **finishes** at 11."

**Expressões úteis para graduar certeza:** will **definitely** / will **probably** / **might** / probably **won't** / definitely **won't**.

**Na estrutura de uma apresentação:**
- Abertura (plano): "Today **I'm going to** talk about..."
- Transição: "Now **I'm going to** show you..." / "Let's move on to..."
- Previsão: "In ten years, this technology **will** probably..."
- Encerramento (oferta): "**I'll** be happy to answer your questions."

| Forma | Uso | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| will | previsão / opinião | I think robots will do most housework. |
| will | decisão na hora / oferta | I'll explain that again. |
| going to | plano / intenção | I'm going to show you three examples. |
| going to | previsão com evidência | Look at this graph — sales are going to fall. |
| present continuous | compromisso marcado | I'm giving a talk on Monday. |
| present simple | horário fixo | The session starts at 2 p.m. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L3/example-01.m4a) Today I'm going to explain how vaccines train your immune system. — _Hoje vou explicar como as vacinas treinam seu sistema imunológico._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L3/example-02.m4a) Think of your immune system like a security guard. The vaccine is a photo of the criminal. — _Pense no seu sistema imunológico como um segurança. A vacina é uma foto do criminoso._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L3/example-03.m4a) I'm presenting my research at a conference in Lisbon next month. It starts on the 15th. — _Vou apresentar minha pesquisa numa conferência em Lisboa mês que vem. Começa dia 15._

> 💡 **Dica:** Na dúvida em apresentações, **going to** para anunciar o que você vai fazer e **will** para previsões e ofertas resolvem 90% dos casos.

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Today I will talk about climate change. (abertura de apresentação já planejada)  
  ✅ **Today I'm going to talk about climate change.**  
  Plano já decidido = going to (will soa como decisão na hora).
- ❌ The conference is starting at 9 a.m. tomorrow, according to the program.  
  ✅ **The conference starts at 9 a.m. tomorrow.**  
  Horários de programação: present simple é o mais natural.
- ❌ "The mic isn't working." "I'm going to check it."  
  ✅ **"I'll check it."**  
  Decisão tomada na hora = will.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Ênfase em apresentações

Bons apresentadores **variam o ritmo**: falam devagar nas ideias importantes e fazem **pausas** antes delas. "The answer is... (pausa) ...simpler than you think." Evite ler os slides — olhe para o público.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L3/pron-01.m4a) Today, I'm going to talk about... (pausa) ...sleep.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L3/pron-02.m4a) In a nutshell, ...
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L3/pron-03.m4a) Think of it like a library in your brain.

## 📖 Reading — Why explaining matters [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L3/reading.m4a)

During the COVID-19 pandemic, millions of people suddenly needed to understand complex scientific ideas: how viruses spread, how vaccines work, what an "R number" is. Scientists who could explain these ideas clearly became incredibly influential, while those who used technical language often failed to reach the public — and misinformation filled the gap. Experts in science communication say there are a few simple rules. First, start with why it matters to the audience, not with the method. Second, use analogies: comparing the immune system to a security team or DNA to a recipe book helps people make sense of abstract ideas. Third, avoid jargon, or explain it the first time you use it. Fourth, tell stories: people remember narratives far better than statistics. Finally, be honest about uncertainty. Saying "we don't know yet, but we're going to find out" builds more trust than pretending to have all the answers. In the future, many experts believe these communication skills will be as important for scientists as research itself.

**Questions:**
1. What happened to scientists who could explain ideas clearly during the pandemic?
2. What should you start with when explaining science?
3. Give an example of an analogy from the text.
4. Why should scientists be honest about uncertainty?

## 📝 Writing

Write the script for the first 90 seconds of a short talk (150–180 words) about a topic you know well. Include an opening with going to, an analogy, a prediction with will and a closing offer.

**Model:**

> Good evening, everyone. Have you ever wondered why you feel so tired after a night of bad sleep, even if you've spent eight hours in bed? Today I'm going to talk about something that happens in your brain every night while you sleep.  
>   
> Think of your brain like a busy city. During the day, the city produces a lot of garbage. At night, while you're sleeping, a cleaning team comes out and washes the streets. Scientists have discovered that during deep sleep, a fluid in your brain does exactly that: it removes waste that builds up during the day.  
>   
> In other words, sleep isn't just rest — it's maintenance. And when we don't sleep enough, the garbage stays in the streets.  
>   
> In the next few minutes, I'm going to show you three simple habits that help your brain's cleaning team do its job. I'm sure that by the end of this talk, you'll never think of sleep in the same way. And of course, I'll be happy to answer your questions at the end.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Elevator pitch

Um **elevator pitch** é a explicação de uma ideia, projeto ou de você mesmo no tempo de uma viagem de elevador: 30 a 60 segundos. É muito usado em eventos, entrevistas e startups. A regra é a mesma da divulgação científica: comece pelo **porquê**, use linguagem simples e termine com um **call to action**.
