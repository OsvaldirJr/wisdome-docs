## 🧩 Grammar: Articles

Artigos são um dos maiores desafios para brasileiros, porque em português usamos "o/a" muito mais.

**A / an** → algo **não específico** ou mencionado pela **primeira vez**; profissões:
- "They launched **a** new campaign." / "She's **an** influencer."

**The** → algo **específico**, **já mencionado**, **único** ou que todos sabem qual é:
- "They launched a new campaign. **The** campaign was a disaster." (já mencionada)
- "**The** CEO of the company..." (só há um)
- "**The** internet / **the** environment / **the** world"
- superlativos: "**the** best brand"; ordinais: "**the** first ad"
- instrumentos musicais: "play **the** guitar"
- países com plural/União: **the** USA, **the** UK, **the** Netherlands

**Zero article (sem artigo)** → **generalizações** com plurais e incontáveis — aqui o português usa artigo, o inglês **não**:
- "**Customers** trust **reviews**." (os clientes em geral) ❌ "The customers trust the reviews"
- "**Marketing** is changing." / "**Social media** is powerful." / "I love **coffee**."
- nomes de pessoas, cidades, a maioria dos países, idiomas, refeições, esportes: "**Brazil**", "**English**", "have **lunch**", "play **soccer**"
- go to **work / school / bed / church** (função), at **home**

**Teste rápido:** a frase fala de **algo em geral** ou de **algo específico**? Geral → sem artigo. Específico → the.

| Uso | Exemplo |
|---|---|
| a/an: primeira menção | There's a new coffee shop near my house. |
| the: já mencionado | The coffee shop has great reviews. |
| the: único / específico | The CEO of Nike / the internet |
| zero: generalização (plural) | People trust recommendations. |
| zero: generalização (incontável) | Advertising is everywhere. |
| the: generalização específica | The people in my office love this brand. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L1/example-01.m4a) Brands can't control what people say about them on social media. — _As marcas não controlam o que as pessoas dizem sobre elas nas redes sociais._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L1/example-02.m4a) The brands that listen to their customers usually grow faster. — _As marcas que ouvem seus clientes geralmente crescem mais rápido._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L1/example-03.m4a) She's a marketing manager at an airline in the UK. — _Ela é gerente de marketing numa companhia aérea no Reino Unido._

> 💡 **Dica:** Repare no segundo exemplo: **"The brands that listen..."** leva *the* porque não são todas as marcas, mas um grupo **específico** (as que ouvem os clientes).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ The people love the social media.  
  ✅ **People love social media.**  
  Generalizações não levam artigo em inglês.
- ❌ She is marketing manager.  
  ✅ **She is a marketing manager.**  
  Profissão no singular leva a/an.
- ❌ I live in the Brazil and I speak the Portuguese.  
  ✅ **I live in Brazil and I speak Portuguese.**  
  A maioria dos países e todos os idiomas: sem artigo.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: The: "dâ" ou "dí"

**The** tem dois sons: "**dâ**" antes de som de consoante (the brand, the campaign) e "**dí**" antes de som de vogal (the internet, the end, the hour). E **a** é quase sempre fraco: "â".

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L1/pron-01.m4a) the brand · the campaign · the logo
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L1/pron-02.m4a) the internet · the end · the only one
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L1/pron-03.m4a) It's a brand with an amazing logo.

<details>
<summary>Transcript</summary>

**Expert:** Today, the first thing most people do before buying a product is read reviews. Many consumers say they trust online reviews almost as much as personal recommendations. That's a big change. In the past, brands controlled the message through advertising. Now, customers write the story. A single negative review that goes viral can damage a reputation built over decades. But there's good news too: the brands that respond to reviews — especially negative ones — usually earn more trust. A polite, honest reply shows that there are real people behind the logo.

## 📖 Reading — From influencers to "de-influencers" [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L1/reading.m4a)

For more than a decade, influencer marketing has been one of the fastest-growing areas of advertising. Brands pay creators — from celebrities with millions of followers to "micro-influencers" with just a few thousand — to recommend their products. The logic is simple: people trust people more than they trust ads. But audiences have become more skeptical. Many followers now know that a glowing review may be a paid partnership, and regulators in several countries require influencers to label sponsored posts clearly. In this context, a new trend has appeared: "de-influencing". Creators post videos explaining which popular products are not worth the money and suggesting cheaper alternatives. Ironically, de-influencing has itself become a marketing strategy, as some brands now position themselves as the honest alternative. The lesson for marketers is clear: in a world full of advertising, authenticity is the most valuable — and the most difficult — thing to sell.

**Questions:**
1. Why does influencer marketing work?
2. What do regulators in several countries require?
3. What is de-influencing?
4. Why is it ironic?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short brand analysis (150–180 words) of a company you like: who its target audience is, how it communicates, and one thing it could do better online.

**Model:**

> One brand I really admire is a Brazilian cosmetics company that started as a small shop in Curitiba. Its target audience is mainly young women who care about sustainability and natural ingredients. What makes the brand special is the way it communicates. Instead of using famous models, the company shows real customers in its ads, with different body types, ages and skin tones. On social media, the tone is friendly and honest: when a product has a problem, the company says so publicly. This authenticity has created a community of loyal customers who recommend the brand to friends. However, there is one thing the company could do better: customer service. The online chat is often slow, and many complaints on review sites are about late deliveries. If the brand answered customers faster, it would protect the reputation it has worked so hard to build.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — #ad e #publi

Nos EUA, a FTC exige que influenciadores sinalizem conteúdo pago com **#ad** ou **#sponsored**. No Reino Unido, a ASA tem regras parecidas. No Brasil, o CONAR orienta o uso de **#publi** ou **#publicidade**. Em inglês: **"This post is sponsored by..."** / **"paid partnership with..."**.
