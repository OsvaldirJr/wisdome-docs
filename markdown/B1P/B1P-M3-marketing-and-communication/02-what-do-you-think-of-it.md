## 🧩 Grammar: Relative clauses

**Relative clauses** adicionam informação sobre um substantivo:

- **who** → pessoas: "The woman **who** designed this app is Brazilian."
- **which** → coisas e animais: "The phone **which** I bought last year is already slow."
- **that** → pessoas ou coisas (informal, muito comum nas defining clauses): "It's a gadget **that** everyone wants."
- **whose** → posse (cujo/cuja): "I have a friend **whose** brother works at Apple."
- **where** → lugares: "This is the shop **where** I bought my laptop."
- **when** → tempo: "I remember the day **when** I got my first phone."

**1. Defining relative clauses** (sem vírgulas) → informação **essencial** para saber de quem/do que falamos:
"The app **that** I use for running is free."
➡ Quando o pronome é **objeto** do verbo seguinte, pode ser **omitido**: "The app (that) **I use**..." / "The man (who) **we met**..."
➡ Quando é **sujeito**, não pode: "The app **that helps** you..." (❌ "The app helps you...")

**2. Non-defining relative clauses** (com vírgulas) → informação **extra**, não essencial. Use **who/which** (nunca **that**) e nunca omita:
"My laptop, **which** I bought in 2020, still works perfectly."
"Melissa Marshall, **who** teaches communication, gave a great talk."

**Which** também pode se referir à frase inteira: "The battery lasts two days, **which** is amazing."

| Pronome | Para | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| who / that | pessoas | The teacher who helped me... |
| which / that | coisas | The phone that I bought... |
| whose | posse | The friend whose car we borrowed... |
| where | lugares | The city where I was born... |
| when | tempo | The year when we met... |
| which (non-defining) | frase inteira | It was free, which was great. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L2/example-01.m4a) It's a feature that most users never discover. — _É uma funcionalidade que a maioria dos usuários nunca descobre._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L2/example-02.m4a) The headphones I bought online didn't live up to the reviews. — _Os fones que comprei online não corresponderam às avaliações._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L2/example-03.m4a) My grandmother, who is 82, uses video calls every day, which I think is amazing. — _Minha avó, que tem 82 anos, faz chamadas de vídeo todo dia, o que eu acho incrível._

> 💡 **Dica:** Em português usamos "que" para tudo. Em inglês, nunca use **what** como pronome relativo: ❌ "The phone what I bought" ✅ "The phone **that/which** I bought".

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ The man which called you is my boss.  
  ✅ **The man who/that called you is my boss.**  
  Para pessoas, use who ou that.
- ❌ The app what I use is free.  
  ✅ **The app (that/which) I use is free.**  
  What não é pronome relativo.
- ❌ My phone, that I bought in 2020, still works.  
  ✅ **My phone, which I bought in 2020, still works.**  
  Non-defining (com vírgulas) não aceita that.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Pausas nas non-defining clauses

Na fala, as vírgulas viram **pausas** curtas e a voz cai um pouco no meio: "My sister — who lives in Paris — is coming next week." Já as defining clauses são ditas de uma vez, sem pausa.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L2/pron-01.m4a) The app that I use is free. (sem pausa)
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L2/pron-02.m4a) My laptop, which is old, still works. (com pausas)
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L2/pron-03.m4a) She's the person whose idea won.

## 📖 Reading — The products that changed everyday life [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M3-L2/reading.m4a)

Some products are so ordinary that we forget they were once revolutionary. Take the ballpoint pen, which was patented in 1938 by László Bíró, a Hungarian journalist who was tired of smudged ink. Or the shopping cart, which was invented in the 1930s by a supermarket owner in Oklahoma whose customers stopped shopping when their baskets got too heavy. At first, shoppers refused to use carts — men thought they looked weak and women thought they looked like baby strollers — so the owner hired actors to push them around the store. In Brazil, an example of a product that changed daily life is the flip-flop. Havaianas, which were created in 1962, were inspired by Japanese zori sandals, and for decades they were seen as cheap shoes for poor people. A clever marketing strategy in the 1990s transformed them into a fashion item that celebrities wear all over the world. The lesson? A good product is important, but the story that people tell about it can matter just as much.

**Questions:**
1. Who invented the ballpoint pen?
2. Why did the supermarket owner invent the shopping cart?
3. How did he convince shoppers to use carts?
4. How did the image of Havaianas change?

**Model:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Review: the language app LinguaGo  
>   
> I've been using LinguaGo, which a colleague recommended to me, for about six months. It's an app that combines short lessons with conversations with real people, which makes it different from most apps I've tried.  
>   
> Pros: the lessons, which only take ten minutes, are perfect for people who don't have much free time. I also love the conversation feature, where you can talk to native speakers from different countries. The teachers I've had were patient and friendly.  
>   
> Cons: the free version, which has a lot of ads, is quite limited. And the premium plan, whose price went up last month, is a bit overpriced compared to similar apps.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — "Hype" e "overrated"

Ao opinar sobre produtos, falantes nativos usam muito: **"It's overhyped / overrated"** (superestimado), **"It's underrated"** (subestimado), **"It lives up to the hype"** (corresponde à expectativa) e **"It's a game changer"** (revolucionário).

## ✅ Resumo
- who (pessoas) · which (coisas) · that (ambos) · whose (posse) · where (lugar).
- Defining: sem vírgula; objeto pode ser omitido.
- Non-defining: com vírgulas; nunca that.
- Nunca "what" como pronome relativo.
