## 🧩 Grammar: Indirect questions

**Perguntas indiretas** soam mais **educadas e profissionais**. Começam com uma expressão introdutória e usam a **ordem de afirmativa** (sujeito + verbo), **sem do/does/did**:

**Expressões introdutórias:**
- Could you tell me...? / Can you tell me...?
- Do you know...? / Do you have any idea...?
- I was wondering... / I'd like to know...
- Would you mind telling me...?

**Transformação:**
- "What **does the job involve**?" → "Could you tell me what **the job involves**?"
- "When **will I hear** from you?" → "Do you know when **I'll hear** from you?"
- "Why **did she leave**?" → "I was wondering why **she left**."

**Perguntas de sim/não** usam **if** ou **whether**:
- "**Is** remote work possible?" → "Could you tell me **if/whether** remote work **is** possible?"

Repare na pontuação: se a frase introdutória é uma afirmação ("I was wondering..."), termina com **ponto**, não interrogação.

**No contexto de entrevista**, perguntas indiretas são ótimas para fazer perguntas sensíveis com elegância: "I was wondering **what the salary range is** for this position."

| Pergunta direta | Pergunta indireta |
|---|---|
| What are the working hours? | Could you tell me what the working hours are? |
| Does the company offer training? | Do you know if the company offers training? |
| Why is the position open? | I was wondering why the position is open. |
| When can I start? | Would you mind telling me when I could start? |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L4/example-01.m4a) Could you tell me what a typical day in this role looks like? — _Você poderia me dizer como é um dia típico nessa função?_
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L4/example-02.m4a) I was wondering whether there are opportunities for international mobility. — _Eu queria saber se há oportunidades de mobilidade internacional._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L4/example-03.m4a) Do you know when you'll make a decision? — _Você sabe quando vão tomar uma decisão?_

> 💡 **Dica:** Na resposta sobre **pontos fracos**, a estratégia é: um ponto fraco real (mas não crítico para a vaga) + o que você está fazendo para melhorar: "I sometimes find it hard to delegate, **so I've been working on** trusting my team more."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ Could you tell me what does the job involve?  
  ✅ **Could you tell me what the job involves?**  
  Perguntas indiretas usam ordem de afirmativa, sem does.
- ❌ Do you know where is the office?  
  ✅ **Do you know where the office is?**  
  Sujeito antes do verbo na pergunta indireta.
- ❌ I was wondering if is it possible to work remotely?  
  ✅ **I was wondering if it's possible to work remotely.**  
  Ordem afirmativa e ponto final.

Em entrevistas, **ritmo calmo** e **entonação descendente** no fim das afirmações transmitem confiança: "One of my strengths is problem-solving ↘." Subir a voz no fim de afirmações ("uptalk") pode soar inseguro.

## 📖 Reading — The questions behind the questions [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L4/reading.m4a)

Most interview questions aren't really about what they seem to ask. "Tell me about yourself" isn't an invitation to share your life story; the interviewer wants a short, relevant summary that explains why you're right for the job. "Why do you want to leave your current job?" is testing your professionalism: criticizing your current boss is a red flag, even if your boss is terrible. "Where do you see yourself in five years?" checks whether your goals match what the company can offer — and whether you're likely to stay. And the famous "What's your biggest weakness?" is about self-awareness: the worst answers are "I don't have any" or the cliché "I'm a perfectionist" without any example. Experts suggest preparing three or four short stories about your experience that you can adapt to different questions. When you understand the question behind the question, you can answer what the interviewer really wants to know.

**Questions:**
1. What does the interviewer want when asking "Tell me about yourself"?
2. Why is criticizing your current boss a bad idea?
3. What does "Where do you see yourself in five years?" check?
4. What do experts suggest preparing?

## 📝 Writing

Write a follow-up email (120–150 words) after a job interview: thank the interviewer, mention something specific from the conversation, reinforce why you're a good fit and ask about next steps indirectly.

**Model:**

> Subject: Thank you – Data Analyst interview  
>   
> Dear Ms. Ferreira,  
>   
> Thank you for taking the time to speak with me yesterday about the Data Analyst position. I really enjoyed learning about your team's plans to expand into new markets, and I was particularly excited to hear about the project to automate customer reports.  
>   
> Our conversation confirmed that this role is an excellent fit for my experience. In my current position, I've built automated dashboards that saved our sales team more than ten hours a week, and I'd love to bring that experience to your team.  
>   
> Please let me know if you need any further information from me. I was also wondering whether you have an idea of when you expect to make a decision.  
>   
> Thank you again for the opportunity.  
>   
> Kind regards,  
> Igor Santos

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Falar de dinheiro em entrevistas

Em muitos países de língua inglesa, é comum o recrutador perguntar **"What are your salary expectations?"** logo no início. Pesquise a faixa de mercado antes e responda com uma faixa: **"Based on my research and experience, I'm looking for something between X and Y."** E lembre que benefícios (**package**) também contam.
