## 🧩 Grammar: Used to / would / be & get used to

**Used to + verbo base** → hábitos ou estados do passado que **não são mais verdade**:
- "I **used to** play video games every night." (não jogo mais)
- "There **used to** be a cinema here."
- Negativa: "I **didn't use to** like coffee." (sem -d!) · Pergunta: "**Did** you **use to** live here?"

**Would + verbo base** → **ações** repetidas no passado (estilo nostálgico, narrativo). **Não** funciona com estados:
- "Every summer, we **would** go to the beach and **would** stay for weeks." ✅
- "I would have long hair." ❌ (estado → "I **used to** have long hair")

**Be used to + -ing / substantivo** → **estar acostumado**:
- "I**'m used to** wak**ing** up early." / "She **isn't used to** the cold."

**Get used to + -ing / substantivo** → **acostumar-se** (processo):
- "I**'m getting used to** speak**ing** English at work." / "You'll **get used to** it."

**Narrar a origem de algo:** past continuous (cenário) + simple past (eventos) + used to (como era antes):
"Before streaming, people **used to** buy CDs. One day, a student **was looking** for a way to share music and **created** a website. It **spread** quickly..."

| Estrutura | Sentido | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| used to + verbo | hábito/estado passado | I used to live in Recife. |
| would + verbo | ação repetida no passado | We would play outside for hours. |
| be used to + -ing | estar acostumado | I'm used to driving in traffic. |
| get used to + -ing | acostumar-se | I'm getting used to the new app. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L3/example-01.m4a) When I was a teenager, I used to spend hours on MSN Messenger. — _Quando eu era adolescente, passava horas no MSN._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L3/example-02.m4a) My grandmother would tell us stories every night before bed. — _Minha avó nos contava histórias toda noite antes de dormir._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L3/example-03.m4a) At first, I wasn't used to working with people from different time zones, but I got used to it. — _No começo, eu não estava acostumado a trabalhar com fusos diferentes, mas me acostumei._

> 💡 **Dica:** Cuidado: **"I use to"** (presente) praticamente não existe para hábitos. Para o presente, use **usually**: "I **usually** wake up at 7" (❌ "I use to wake up at 7") — erro muito comum de brasileiros, por causa do "costumo".

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I use to go to the gym every day.  
  ✅ **I usually go to the gym every day.**  
  "Costumo" no presente = usually. Used to é só passado.
- ❌ I didn't used to like vegetables.  
  ✅ **I didn't use to like vegetables.**  
  Depois de did/didn't, use (sem d).
- ❌ I'm used to wake up early.  
  ✅ **I'm used to waking up early.**  
  Be used to + -ing.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Used to = "iústa"

**Used to** (hábito) soa "**iústa**" — com S, não Z, e o D some. Compare com **used** (verbo usar no passado): "I **used** (iúzd) a pen." O som muda conforme o sentido!

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L3/pron-01.m4a) I used to live there. ("iústa")

## 📖 Reading — From fad to mainstream [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L3/reading.m4a)

Why do some ideas disappear after a few months while others change the world? Sociologist Everett Rogers studied this question in the 1960s and developed a theory called the "diffusion of innovations". According to Rogers, new ideas spread through a population in stages. First come the innovators, a tiny group who love taking risks. Next, the early adopters try the idea and, crucially, tell others about it. Only after that does the majority follow — first the "early majority", then the more skeptical "late majority". Finally, the "laggards" accept the change, often reluctantly. Smartphones followed this pattern: in 2007 they were expensive gadgets for tech fans; a decade later, even grandparents were sending voice messages. Fads, on the other hand, usually never get past the early adopters. The difference? Real innovations solve a problem people actually have.

## 📝 Writing

Write the origin story (150–180 words) of a trend, movement, brand or tradition you know. Use narrative tenses, used to and would.

**Model:**

> Twenty years ago, people in Brazil didn't use to drink craft beer — most bars sold the same three or four brands. Then, in the early 2000s, a few homebrewers in the South started experimenting with new recipes in their garages. At first, they would share their beer only with friends and family, who were usually surprised by the strong flavors. Some of these friends were early adopters: they began to ask for more, and small breweries opened. The tipping point came when festivals in cities like Blumenau and Curitiba started to attract thousands of visitors, and the media began to talk about the "craft beer revolution". Today, there are more than a thousand breweries in the country, and people are used to ordering IPAs and stouts in normal bars. What used to be a niche hobby has become mainstream — and the movement is still growing.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Gírias de tendências

Para falar de tendências como um nativo: algo **in** ou **trending** está na moda; **out** ou **so last year** está ultrapassado; **cringe** é constrangedor; e algo **old school** é das antigas (às vezes com carinho!).
