## 🧩 Grammar: Verb + object + infinitive / make & let

No B1 você viu **verbo + -ing** e **verbo + to**. Agora, o padrão com **objeto** (a pessoa que recebe a ação):

**verbo + objeto + to + verbo**
want, ask, tell, expect, encourage, allow, persuade, remind, advise, teach, need, would like
- "My manager **wants me to lead** the project."
- "She **asked us to arrive** early."
- "My teacher **encouraged me to apply**."
- "They don't **allow employees to use** their phones."

⚠️ Em português dizemos "quero **que você** venha"; em inglês **nunca** use "that" aqui: ❌ "I want that you come" ✅ "I want **you to come**."

**make / let + objeto + verbo base (sem to):**
- **make** = obrigar, fazer com que: "My first boss **made me redo** the report three times."
- **let** = deixar, permitir: "My company **lets us work** from anywhere."
- (allow = let, mas com to: "They **allow us to work**...")

**Adjetivo + preposição + -ing:**
good **at**, capable **of**, keen **on**, interested **in**, used **to**, committed **to**, responsible **for**, tired **of**
- "She's **capable of managing** a big team." / "I'm **committed to improving** my English."

| Padrão | Verbos | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| verb + object + to | want, ask, tell, encourage, allow, expect | They asked me to stay late. |
| make + object + base | make (obrigar/causar) | The movie made me cry. |
| let + object + base | let (permitir) | Let me help you. |
| adj + prep + -ing | good at, keen on, capable of | He's good at solving problems. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L2/example-01.m4a) To be a pilot, you need to be capable of making quick decisions under pressure. — _Para ser piloto, você precisa ser capaz de tomar decisões rápidas sob pressão._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L2/example-02.m4a) My mentor persuaded me to take the leadership course. — _Minha mentora me convenceu a fazer o curso de liderança._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L2/example-03.m4a) My parents never let me quit anything I started — and I'm grateful now. — _Meus pais nunca me deixaram desistir de nada que comecei — e hoje sou grato._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Let me** + verbo é uma expressão muito útil: "**Let me** check" (deixa eu ver), "**Let me** explain", "**Let me** know" (me avisa).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ My boss wants that I travel.  
  ✅ **My boss wants me to travel.**  
  Want + objeto + to (sem that).
- ❌ She made me to wait.  
  ✅ **She made me wait.**  
  Make + objeto + verbo base (sem to).
- ❌ He's very good in solving problems.  
  ✅ **He's very good at solving problems.**  
  Good at + -ing.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Let me = "lémi", want you to = "uóntchu tu"

**Let me** se funde em "**lémi**" ("Lemme check"). **Want you to** vira "**uóntchu tu**" e **asked me to** fica "**ésktmi tu**". Na fala, ouça os blocos, não as palavras isoladas.

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L2/pron-01.m4a) Let me check. ("lémi tchék")

## 📖 Reading — Grit: the secret ingredient? [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L2/reading.m4a)

Why do some people succeed while others with the same talent give up? Psychologist Angela Duckworth spent years studying this question with groups as different as military cadets, salespeople and children in spelling competitions. Her answer was a quality she called "grit": a combination of passion and perseverance for long-term goals. According to her research, grit was often a better predictor of success than talent or IQ. Gritty people aren't necessarily the most intelligent; they're the ones who keep going after failure. The good news is that grit can be developed. Parents and teachers can encourage children to finish what they start, let them experience small failures, and teach them to see mistakes as part of learning. Some critics argue that the concept is too similar to older ideas like conscientiousness, but the message remains popular: talent is important, but effort counts twice.

**Questions:**
1. What is grit, according to Duckworth?
2. What was grit often a better predictor of success than?
3. How can parents and teachers develop grit?
4. What do some critics argue?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short personal statement (150–180 words) for a job or scholarship explaining why you have what it takes. Use verb patterns from this lesson.

**Model:**

> I'm applying for the Youth Leadership Scholarship because I believe I've got what it takes to make a difference in my community. Growing up in a small town in Piauí, I saw many talented friends give up on their studies. My teachers encouraged me to keep going, and my mother never let me miss a day of school, even when it was difficult. At university, I founded a tutoring program that helps first-generation students. Leading a team of 25 volunteers taught me to listen, to take the initiative and to stay calm under pressure. I'm particularly good at organizing projects and I'm committed to improving education in the Northeast. This scholarship would allow me to study public policy and would make me a more effective leader. I'd like the selection committee to know that I'm capable of turning opportunities into real change for others.
