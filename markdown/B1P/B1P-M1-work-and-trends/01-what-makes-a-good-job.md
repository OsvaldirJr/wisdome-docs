## 🧩 Grammar: Present simple x continuous: além do básico

Você já sabe o básico: simple = rotina/fatos; continuous = agora/temporário. No B1+, acrescente:

**Present continuous para tendências e mudanças em andamento:**
"More people **are working** from home." / "Salaries **are rising**." / "The job market **is changing** fast."

**Present simple para estados permanentes e opiniões:**
"I **believe** that work should have purpose." / "This job **requires** patience."

**Verbos com dois sentidos** — são stative num sentido e de ação em outro:
- **think**: "I **think** it's a good idea" (opinião = simple) x "I**'m thinking** about quitting" (processo de pensar = continuous)
- **have**: "She **has** two jobs" (posse) x "She**'s having** a hard time" (experiência)
- **see**: "I **see** what you mean" (entender) x "I**'m seeing** a client at 3" (encontrar)
- **feel**: "I **feel** that..." (opinião) x "I**'m feeling** tired today" (sensação agora — ambos possíveis)
- **be**: "He **is** rude" (característica) x "He**'s being** rude" (comportamento temporário, agora)

**Always + continuous** = hábito irritante: "My boss **is always changing** deadlines!"

| Stative (simple) | Ação / processo (continuous) |
|---|---|
| I think remote work is better. | I'm thinking of applying for the job. |
| We have a great team. | We're having a meeting now. |
| I see the problem. | I'm seeing my mentor tomorrow. |
| She is very patient. | She's being very patient today. |
| This costs too much. | Prices are rising every month. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L1/example-01.m4a) More and more companies are offering four-day weeks. — _Cada vez mais empresas estão oferecendo semanas de quatro dias._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L1/example-02.m4a) I'm thinking about volunteering on weekends. — _Estou pensando em fazer trabalho voluntário nos fins de semana._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L1/example-03.m4a) My colleague is always taking credit for my ideas! — _Meu colega está sempre levando crédito pelas minhas ideias!_

> 💡 **Dica:** Para falar de **mudanças graduais**, combine continuous com **get / become / increase / decrease / more and more**: "Housing **is getting more expensive**."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ I'm thinking that it's a good idea.  
  ✅ **I think it's a good idea.**  
  Opinião = think no simple.
- ❌ Nowadays more people work from home more and more.  
  ✅ **Nowadays more and more people are working from home.**  
  Tendências em andamento = present continuous.
- ❌ I'm having a car.  
  ✅ **I have a car.**  
  Have como posse não usa -ing.

## 📖 Reading — The meaning of work [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M1-L1/reading.m4a)

For decades, the typical career path was simple: study, get a stable job, climb the career ladder and retire. Today, many workers are questioning that model. Surveys in several countries suggest that a large number of employees — especially younger ones — are prepared to leave a job that doesn't match their values. Researchers who study job satisfaction say that money matters, but only up to a point. Once people earn enough to live comfortably, other factors become more important: autonomy (control over how you work), mastery (the feeling that you're improving) and purpose (the sense that your work matters to others). Interestingly, purpose doesn't require a "heroic" job. Hospital cleaners who see their role as helping patients recover often report high satisfaction. In other words, meaning doesn't only come from what we do, but from how we think about what we do.

**Questions:**
1. What was the typical career path in the past?
2. What do researchers say about money?
3. What are the three other factors?
4. What's the point of the hospital cleaners example?

## 📝 Writing

Write an opinion paragraph (150–180 words): "Is purpose more important than salary?" Use present simple for opinions and present continuous for current trends.

**Model:**

> In my opinion, purpose and salary are both important, but purpose becomes more important once you earn enough to live comfortably. Today, more and more people are leaving well-paid jobs because they don't feel their work matters. I understand them. A few years ago, I was working for a company that paid very well, but I felt empty at the end of every day. Now I'm working for an education startup. I earn a little less, but I see the impact of my work when students tell me they've learned something new. That said, I don't think everyone has the privilege of choosing purpose over money. Many people are struggling to pay their bills, and for them, a stable salary is the priority. So I believe the ideal job offers both: a fair salary and a sense that you're contributing to something bigger than yourself.
