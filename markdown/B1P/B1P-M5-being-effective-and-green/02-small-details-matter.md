## 🧩 Grammar: Obligation and advice: nuances

No B1 você aprendeu must/have to/mustn't/don't have to. No B1+, as **nuances**:

**Must x have to (afirmativa):**
- **must** → obrigação que **o falante** sente ou impõe (interna, pessoal) e regras **escritas** formais: "I **must** call my mother — I promised." / "Passengers **must** wear seatbelts." (aviso oficial)
- **have to** → obrigação **externa** (regras, circunstâncias), mais comum na fala: "I **have to** wear a uniform at work." (a empresa exige)

**Need to / don't need to / needn't:**
- **need to** = é necessário: "We **need to** fix this bug before the launch."
- **don't need to / needn't** = não é necessário (= don't have to): "You **don't need to** bring anything." / "You **needn't** worry." (needn't é mais britânico/formal)

**Mustn't x don't have to (revisão — o erro mais comum):**
- **mustn't** = proibido: "You **mustn't** share your password."
- **don't have to / don't need to** = não é necessário: "You **don't have to** reply today."

**Should / ought to** → recomendação (o certo a fazer, mas não obrigatório):
- "You **should** proofread every email before sending it." / "We **ought to** update the checklist."

**Passado:** **had to** (obrigação), **didn't have to / didn't need to** (não era necessário), **should have** (crítica — Módulo 2).

| Força | Estrutura | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Obrigação forte (regra escrita / pessoal) | must | All visitors must sign in. |
| Obrigação externa | have to / need to | I have to submit the report by Friday. |
| Proibição | mustn't / can't | You mustn't park here. |
| Não necessário | don't have to / don't need to / needn't | You needn't print it. |
| Recomendação | should / ought to | You should double-check the numbers. |
| Passado | had to / didn't have to | We had to redo the whole report. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M5-L2/example-01.m4a) In aviation, pilots have to follow a checklist before every flight, no matter how experienced they are. — _Na aviação, pilotos têm que seguir uma checklist antes de cada voo, por mais experientes que sejam._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M5-L2/example-02.m4a) You don't need to write a long email. You should just make sure the numbers are correct. — _Você não precisa escrever um e-mail longo. Só deveria garantir que os números estão corretos._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M5-L2/example-03.m4a) I must stop checking my phone during meetings. It's a terrible habit. — _Preciso parar de olhar o celular durante as reuniões. É um hábito terrível._

> 💡 **Dica:** **Need** como verbo comum pede **to** (need to do) e **do/does** nas perguntas (Do I need to...?). **Needn't** (modal) é usado sem to: "You needn't come."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ You mustn't bring food. It's provided. (= não precisa)  
  ✅ **You don't need to / don't have to bring food.**  
  Mustn't = proibido. Não é necessário = don't need to / don't have to.
- ❌ Yesterday I must stay late.  
  ✅ **Yesterday I had to stay late.**  
  Must não tem passado: use had to.
- ❌ Do I must sign this?  
  ✅ **Do I have to sign this? / Must I sign this?**  
  Must não usa do. A pergunta natural é com have to.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Need to / have to / ought to

Na fala: **need to** = "**nídu**" (o D e o T se juntam), **have to** = "**réfta**", **has to** = "**résta**", **ought to** = "**órâ**" (EUA). **Mustn't** = "**mâsent**".

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M5-L2/pron-01.m4a) We need to fix it. ("nídu")
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M5-L2/pron-02.m4a) You have to sign here. ("réfta")
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M5-L2/pron-03.m4a) You ought to call her. ("órâ")

## 📖 Reading — The power of checklists [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M5-L2/reading.m4a)

In 1935, a new American bomber plane crashed during a test flight, killing two of its crew. The investigation found that the pilots hadn't made a mechanical error — they had simply forgotten one step: they hadn't released a lock on the controls. The plane was considered "too complex for one person to fly". Instead of giving up, test pilots created a simple solution: a checklist. With it, the plane went on to fly safely for millions of miles. Decades later, surgeon and writer Atul Gawande argued in his book The Checklist Manifesto that the same idea could save lives in hospitals. A study coordinated by the World Health Organization in eight hospitals around the world found that a simple surgical safety checklist was associated with a significant reduction in complications and deaths. The checklist didn't teach surgeons anything new; it made sure that the basic steps — confirming the patient's identity, checking allergies, counting instruments — weren't overlooked. The lesson goes beyond medicine: experts don't fail because they lack knowledge, but because even the best people sometimes forget the obvious.

**Questions:**
1. Why did the plane crash in 1935?
2. What solution did test pilots create?
3. What did the WHO-coordinated study find?
4. Why do experts sometimes fail, according to the text?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short guide (150–180 words) for new employees or students: "Rules and tips for your first month". Use at least 6 different modals of obligation, prohibition and advice.

**Model:**

> Welcome to the team! Your first month: rules and tips  
>   
> Rules  
> • You have to complete the online security training in your first week. It's compulsory for all employees.  
> • You must wear your ID badge at all times in the building.  
> • You mustn't share client information on personal devices or social media.  
> • You don't need to come to the office on Fridays — it's our remote day — but you do need to be online between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.  
>   
> Tips  
> • You should ask questions. Nobody expects you to know everything, and it's much better to ask than to guess.  
> • You ought to introduce yourself to people in other departments. Coffee breaks are a great opportunity.  
> • Before you send anything to a client, double-check the details: names, dates and numbers. A small typo can cause big problems.  
> • Finally, you don't have to be perfect. Everyone makes mistakes in their first month — the important thing is to learn from them.
