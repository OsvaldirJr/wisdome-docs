## 🧩 Grammar: Modals of deduction: present and past

Usamos **modais de dedução** para dizer o que achamos que **é verdade** com base em evidências.

**PRESENTE — modal + verbo base (ou be + -ing):**
- **must** = tenho quase certeza que **sim**: "You've been working for 12 hours. You **must be** tired."
- **might / may / could** = **talvez**: "He's not answering. He **might be** in a meeting."
- **can't** = tenho quase certeza que **não**: "That **can't be** Ana. She's in Japan."
⚠️ O oposto de **must** (dedução) é **can't**, não "mustn't" (mustn't = proibição).

**PASSADO — modal + have + particípio:**
- **must have** + particípio = com certeza aconteceu: "The streets are wet. It **must have rained**."
- **might / may / could have** + particípio = talvez tenha acontecido: "She's late. She **might have missed** the bus."
- **can't / couldn't have** + particípio = com certeza **não** aconteceu: "He **can't have stolen** the money — he was with me all night."

**Para ação em andamento:**
- presente: "She **must be working** late."
- passado: "They **must have been sleeping** when the alarm went off."

Na fala, **must have** = "**mâstav**", **might have** = "**máitav**", **can't have** = "**kéntav**".

| Certeza | Presente | Passado |
|---|---|---|
| ~95% sim | must be / must work | must have been / must have worked |
| ~50% | might / may / could be | might / may / could have been |
| ~95% não | can't be | can't / couldn't have been |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M4-L3/example-01.m4a) The office lights are still on at midnight. Someone must be working late. — _As luzes do escritório ainda estão acesas à meia-noite. Alguém deve estar trabalhando até tarde._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M4-L3/example-02.m4a) This invention could save millions of lives. It might be the answer we've been looking for. — _Esta invenção pode salvar milhões de vidas. Pode ser a resposta que estávamos procurando._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M4-L3/example-03.m4a) My keys aren't here. I must have left them at the office — or I might have dropped them in the taxi. — _Minhas chaves não estão aqui. Devo ter deixado no escritório — ou posso ter deixado cair no táxi._

> 💡 **Dica:** Não confunda **must have** (dedução: deve ter) com **should have** (crítica/arrependimento: deveria ter). "He **must have** forgotten" (tenho quase certeza que esqueceu) x "He **should have** remembered" (era para ter lembrado).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ She mustn't be at home. The lights are off. (dedução)  
  ✅ **She can't be at home. The lights are off.**  
  O negativo de must (dedução) é can't.
- ❌ He must forgot his phone.  
  ✅ **He must have forgotten his phone.**  
  Dedução sobre o passado = must have + particípio.
- ❌ It might rained last night.  
  ✅ **It might have rained last night.**  
  Passado = might have + particípio.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: must have / might have / can't have

Na fala, o **have** vira "**av**" ou "**â**": **must have** = "MÂS-tav", **might have** = "MÁI-tav", **can't have** = "KÉNT-av", **could have** = "KÚ-dav". Por isso nativos às vezes escrevem errado "must of".

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M4-L3/pron-01.m4a) She must have forgotten. ("mâstav")

## 📖 Reading — The mystery of the Voynich manuscript [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M4-L3/reading.m4a)

In 1912, a book dealer named Wilfrid Voynich bought a strange manuscript from a collection in Italy. The book, which is now kept at Yale University, has about 240 pages full of drawings of unknown plants, astronomical diagrams and women bathing in green pools. But the strangest thing is the text: it's written in an alphabet that nobody has ever been able to read. Carbon dating has shown that the parchment must have been made in the early 15th century. So who wrote it, and why? Some researchers believe it might be written in a lost natural language. Others think it could be a code designed to hide secret medical or alchemical knowledge. Some skeptics argue that it can't contain real meaning at all: it might have been a clever hoax, created to sell to a rich collector. Over the years, linguists, cryptographers and even AI programs have tried to decode it, and several people have claimed to have solved the mystery — but none of their solutions has been widely accepted. For now, the Voynich manuscript remains one of the most famous unsolved puzzles in the world.

**Questions:**
1. When was the parchment made, according to carbon dating?
2. What does the book contain?
3. What are two theories about the text?
4. Has anyone solved the mystery?

## 📝 Writing

Write a short "detective report" (150–180 words) about a real or invented mystery: describe the evidence and give at least 3 possible explanations using modals of deduction.

**Model:**

> Case report: The empty office  
>   
> On Monday morning, employees at a design studio in Belo Horizonte found something strange. All the plants in the office had been moved to the meeting room, every chair was facing the window, and there was a birthday cake on the table with no name on it. Nobody remembered ordering it.  
>   
> There are several possible explanations. First, the cleaning team might have moved the plants to clean the floor, but this can't explain the cake. Second, someone must have had a key, because the door wasn't damaged. The manager, who was the only person in the office on Sunday, could have organized a surprise for somebody — but he says he didn't. Finally, the chairs facing the window are a clue: someone might have been planning a photo session.  
>   
> The truth? On Tuesday, the receptionist admitted that she had organized a surprise party for her colleague's birthday — but she had got the date wrong. It must have been an awkward Monday!

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Sherlock Holmes e "Elementary"

O detetive Sherlock Holmes é famoso por suas deduções ("You must have been in Afghanistan"). A frase **"Elementary, my dear Watson"** é uma das mais citadas da cultura britânica — embora, curiosamente, ela nunca apareça exatamente assim nos livros originais de Arthur Conan Doyle.

- must (sim) · might/may/could (talvez) · can't (não).
- Passado: must / might / can't + have + particípio.
- Oposto de must (dedução) = can't (não mustn't).
- must have (dedução) ≠ should have (arrependimento).
