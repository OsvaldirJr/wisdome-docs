## 🧩 Grammar: Reported speech

Para relatar o que alguém disse, usamos **say** e **tell** e geralmente **voltamos um tempo** no passado (backshift):

**Say x tell:**
- **say** (something) (**to someone**): "She **said** (that) she was busy." / "She **said to me** that..."
- **tell + pessoa** (sempre com objeto!): "She **told me** (that) she was busy." (❌ "She said me" / ❌ "She told that...")

**Backshift (quando o verbo introdutório está no passado):**
- present simple → past simple: "I **work** here." → She said she **worked** there.
- present continuous → past continuous: "I'**m leaving**." → He said he **was leaving**.
- present perfect / past simple → past perfect: "I'**ve finished**." / "I **finished**." → She said she **had finished**.
- will → would: "I'**ll help**." → He said he **would help**.
- can → could: "I **can** come." → She said she **could** come.
- must → had to: "You **must** go." → He said I **had to** go.

Também mudam **pronomes** e **referências de tempo/lugar**: today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that.

💡 Se a informação **ainda é verdade**, o backshift é opcional: "He said he **lives** in Rio." (ainda mora)

**Perguntas relatadas** — ordem de **afirmativa**, sem do/does/did:
- sim/não → **if / whether**: "Are you ready?" → She asked **if I was** ready.
- wh- → "Where **do you live**?" → He asked **where I lived**.

**Pedidos e ordens:** **ask / tell + pessoa + (not) to + verbo**: "Please sit down." → She **asked me to sit** down. / "Don't be late!" → He **told us not to be** late.

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| "I love my job." | She said (that) she loved her job. |
| "We're moving to Lisbon." | They told me they were moving to Lisbon. |
| "I've never failed." | He claimed he had never failed. |
| "I'll call you tomorrow." | She said she would call me the next day. |
| "Can you help me?" | He asked if I could help him. |
| "Where do you work?" | She asked me where I worked. |
| "Don't give up!" | My coach told me not to give up. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L1/example-01.m4a) Steve Jobs told Stanford graduates to "stay hungry, stay foolish". — _Steve Jobs disse aos formandos de Stanford para "continuarem famintos, continuarem tolos"._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L1/example-02.m4a) My manager said she was proud of the team and asked if we wanted to celebrate. — _Minha gestora disse que estava orgulhosa da equipe e perguntou se queríamos comemorar._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L1/example-03.m4a) The interviewer asked me why I had left my last job. — _O entrevistador me perguntou por que eu tinha saído do último emprego._

> 💡 **Dica:** Além de say e tell, use **reporting verbs** mais precisos para soar mais natural: **explained** (explicou), **admitted** (admitiu), **claimed** (afirmou), **warned** (alertou), **promised** (prometeu), **suggested** (sugeriu), **reminded** (lembrou).

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ She said me that she was tired.  
  ✅ **She told me (that) she was tired. / She said (that) she was tired.**  
  Say não leva a pessoa diretamente; tell sempre leva.
- ❌ He asked me where did I live.  
  ✅ **He asked me where I lived.**  
  Pergunta relatada: ordem de afirmativa, sem did.
- ❌ She told me don't worry.  
  ✅ **She told me not to worry.**  
  Ordem/pedido negativo relatado: not to + verbo.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: Said e told

**Said** = "séd" (não "sêid"!). **Told** = "tôuld". **Asked** = "ésk(t)" — o -ed vira um T leve e quase some: "She ask(t) me...".

- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L1/pron-01.m4a) She SAID (séd) she was tired.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L1/pron-02.m4a) He TOLD me to wait.
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L1/pron-03.m4a) They ASKED (éskt) if I could help.

## 📖 Reading — Quotes that weren't [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L1/reading.m4a)

"Be the change you wish to see in the world," said Gandhi. Or did he? This famous quote appears on posters, mugs and social media posts, but researchers who have studied Gandhi's writings say he never wrote or said those exact words. He did write something with a similar idea, but in a much longer and more complex sentence. The same thing has happened to many other famous people. Einstein is often quoted as saying that doing the same thing over and over and expecting different results is "the definition of insanity" — but there's no evidence he ever said it. And Marie Antoinette almost certainly never told hungry French peasants to "eat cake". Why do fake quotes spread so easily? Psychologists suggest that a sentence seems more true and more wise when it's attached to a famous name. So the next time someone tells you that a celebrity said something brilliant, you might want to ask a simple question: "Where exactly did they say it?"

**Questions:**
1. What do researchers say about the Gandhi quote?
2. Which quote is wrongly attributed to Einstein?
3. What did Marie Antoinette almost certainly never say?
4. Why do fake quotes spread easily, according to psychologists?
