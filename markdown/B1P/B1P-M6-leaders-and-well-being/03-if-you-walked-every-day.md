## 🧩 Grammar: Second conditional (avançado) + I wish / If only

**Second conditional** = **If + past simple, would / could / might + verbo** → situações **hipotéticas**, imaginárias ou **diferentes da realidade atual**:
- "If I **slept** more, I**'d be** less stressed." (realidade: não durmo o suficiente)
- "If we **walked** to work, we **could save** money." (could = seria possível)
- "If she **took** a break, she **might feel** better." (might = talvez)

**First x second — a escolha depende de quão real você acha a situação:**
- "If I **get** a promotion, I**'ll** buy a car." → possível, realista
- "If I **got** a promotion, I**'d** buy a car." → menos provável, hipotético

**If I were you...** → conselho (were para todas as pessoas): "If I **were** you, I**'d** see a doctor."

**Wh- questions:** "What **would** you **do** if you **had** a month off?"

**I wish / If only + past simple** → desejo de que o **presente** fosse diferente:
- "I **wish** I **had** more free time." (mas não tenho)
- "**If only** I **didn't have** to work on Saturdays!" (If only = mais enfático)
- com habilidade: "I wish I **could** sleep better."
- com be: "I wish I **were** more relaxed."

**I wish + would** → reclamação sobre o comportamento de **outra pessoa**: "I wish my neighbor **would** stop playing loud music."

| Estrutura | Uso | Exemplo |
|---|---|---|
| If + past, would + verbo | hipótese / resultado | If I exercised, I'd feel better. |
| If + past, could + verbo | possibilidade hipotética | If I had a bike, I could cycle to work. |
| If + past, might + verbo | resultado incerto | If you meditated, you might sleep better. |
| If I were you, I'd... | conselho | If I were you, I'd take a holiday. |
| I wish / If only + past | desejo sobre o presente | I wish I lived near the beach. |
| I wish + would | irritação com outra pessoa | I wish he would stop smoking. |

**Examples:**
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L3/example-01.m4a) If people got enough sleep, they would make better decisions at work. — _Se as pessoas dormissem o suficiente, tomariam decisões melhores no trabalho._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L3/example-02.m4a) If I didn't have to commute two hours a day, I could go to the gym. — _Se eu não precisasse gastar duas horas no trajeto, poderia ir à academia._
- [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L3/example-03.m4a) I wish I could switch off after work, but I keep checking my emails. — _Queria conseguir me desligar depois do trabalho, mas fico checando os e-mails._

> 💡 **Dica:** Na fala, **I would** quase sempre vira **I'd**, e **would not** vira **wouldn't**. E nunca use would na oração com **if**: ❌ "If I would have time..." ✅ "If I **had** time..."

## ⚠️ Atenção, brasileiro!

- ❌ If I would sleep more, I would feel better.  
  ✅ **If I slept more, I would feel better.**  
  Na oração com if, use o past simple (não would).
- ❌ I wish I have more time.  
  ✅ **I wish I had more time.**  
  I wish (presente) + past simple.
- ❌ If I was you, I'd rest. (em contexto formal)  
  ✅ **If I were you, I'd rest.**  
  A forma padrão é If I were.

## 🗣️ Pronunciation: I'd / you'd / wouldn't

**I'd** = "áid", **you'd** = "iúd", **she'd** = "shíd" — o D é curto, mas essencial (sem ele, a frase vira presente). **Wouldn't** = "uúdent". **I wish** = "ái uísh".

**Doctor:** My patients often ask me: "If you could recommend just one change, what would it be?" My answer is almost always the same: sleep. If most adults slept seven to nine hours a night, we would see fewer cases of heart disease, depression and obesity. The second change would be movement. You don't need to run marathons. ## 📖 Reading — The Blue Zones [🔊](../../../audio/B1P/B1P-M6-L3/reading.m4a)

Why do people in some parts of the world live much longer than others? Journalist Dan Buettner, working with researchers and demographers, identified five regions with unusually high numbers of centenarians and called them the "Blue Zones": Okinawa (Japan), Sardinia (Italy), Nicoya (Costa Rica), Ikaria (Greece) and Loma Linda (California, USA). In these places, people don't go to the gym or follow strict diets. Instead, they share some simple habits. They move naturally throughout the day — walking, gardening, doing housework. They eat mostly plants, especially beans, and they stop eating before they feel completely full. They have a strong sense of purpose and close family and community ties. If we all lived like this, would we live to 100? Not necessarily — genes and healthcare also matter, and some researchers have questioned parts of the data. But the main message is hard to argue with: if our daily lives included more movement, better food and stronger relationships, most of us would probably be healthier and happier.

**Questions:**
1. What are the Blue Zones?
2. How do people there exercise?
3. What do they eat?
4. What other factors matter, according to the text?

## 📝 Writing

Write an opinion essay (150–180 words): "If companies cared more about employees' well-being, they would be more successful." Do you agree? Use the second conditional and I wish.

**Model:**

> I strongly agree that companies would be more successful if they cared more about their employees' well-being.  
>   
> First, if employees slept better and worked fewer extra hours, they would make fewer mistakes and have better ideas. Tired people are rarely creative. Second, if companies offered flexible hours, many workers could exercise, take their children to school and still be productive. People who feel trusted usually work harder, not less. Third, if managers paid attention to mental health, fewer employees would burn out and leave. Replacing a good employee is expensive, so investing in well-being also saves money.  
>   
> Some people argue that well-being programs are just a trend and that results are what matter. However, in my experience, results and well-being are connected. In my previous job, I worked twelve hours a day, and I wish someone had told me to stop earlier — I burned out after two years.  
>   
> In conclusion, if more leaders saw well-being as an investment rather than a cost, both companies and people would be healthier.

## 🌎 Cultura & Curiosidade — Work-life balance e "right to disconnect"

Alguns países, como França, Portugal e Austrália, criaram leis de **right to disconnect** (direito à desconexão), que limitam o contato de chefes com funcionários fora do horário de trabalho. Em inglês, expressões comuns: **work-life balance**, **burnout**, **mental health day** e **switch off** (desligar).
